# Published research on how strong the income-health link is (November 15, 2026)

Found by a search agent that opened each source. Numbers are as reported by the source. Items marked
**unconfirmed** need checking before they go in the post.

## The built-in part (PLACES is modeled, partly from poverty)

- **Kong AY, Zhang X. "The Use of Small Area Estimates in Place-Based Health Research." Am J Public Health
  2020;110(6):829-832.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7204458. "Regressing small area estimates on
  the same variables that were used to create them will result in associations." Zhang leads CDC's small-area
  methods work.
- **Greenlund KJ et al. "PLACES: Local Data for Better Health." Prev Chronic Dis 2022;19:210459.**
  https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2022/21_0459.htm. Model inputs: age, sex, race and ethnicity, education, county
  share below 150% of poverty. "Use caution when including PLACES estimates in other models."
- **CDC PLACES FAQ (December 11, 2025).** https://www.cdc.gov/places/faqs/index.html. "In the prediction step of
  the modeling process, we do use census-tract poverty estimates."

## How well the model matches surveyed local numbers

- **Zhang X et al. Am J Epidemiol 2015;182(2):127-137.** doi:10.1093/aje/kwv002. Model vs. direct survey,
  115 Missouri counties, Pearson r: COPD 0.69, diabetes 0.51, smoking 0.40, obesity 0.28.
- **Wang Y et al. Prev Chronic Dis 2017;14:170281 (Boston, 2013).** https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2017/17_0281.htm.
  25 ZIP codes: r 0.53 to 0.89 for most measures; smoking 0.27, frequent mental distress 0.16. Model estimates
  "suggest a narrower range than that suggested by direct surveys." Tracts were not validated.

## Strength and shape of the link with counted outcomes

- **Chetty R et al. "The Association Between Income and Life Expectancy in the United States, 2001-2014." JAMA
  2016;315(16):1750-1766.** doi:10.1001/jama.2016.4226. Individuals, tax records. Top 1% vs. bottom 1% gap: 14.6
  years (men), 10.1 (women). "There was no dividing line above or below which higher income was not associated
  with higher life expectancy." Concave in dollars.
- **Dwyer-Lindgren L et al. JAMA Intern Med 2017;177(7):1003-1011.** doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2017.0918.
  Counties, life expectancy at birth in 2009. Confirmed November 19, 2026 from the JAMA full text (abstract,
  Methods, Tables 1 and 2): "Socioeconomic and race/ethnicity factors, behavioral and metabolic risk factors, and
  health care factors explained 60%, 74%, and 27% of county-level variation in life expectancy, respectively";
  combined, 74%. The socioeconomic and race/ethnicity group is poverty share, log median household income, high
  school and college graduates, unemployment, and Black, American Indian/Alaska Native/Native Hawaiian and Hispanic
  population shares. Single-variable R² (Table 1): log median household income 0.41, poverty share 0.47. Most of
  the socioeconomic association runs through behavioral and metabolic risk factors.

## Tract-level, 500 Cities

- **Fitzpatrick KM, Willis D. IJERPH 2020;17(8):2961.** doi:10.3390/ijerph17082961. About 27,000 tracts, adjusted
  R² about 0.51 to 0.55 for diabetes, stroke, high blood pressure from several area measures; no income-only
  figure; does not address the built-in issue.
- King County diabetes by tract from health plan records (Prev Chronic Dis 2014,
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4112927/): r = -0.18 with median household income
  (**unconfirmed**: paper not opened, author not confirmed).

## Not found

No published tract-level correlation between income and PLACES or 500 Cities estimates, and no study that
measures how much of that link the model creates.
