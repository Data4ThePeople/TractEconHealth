# R046 sourcing pass: Pine Ridge Reservation (Oglala Lakota Oyate), South Dakota

**Summary:** All 15 entries now rest on tier A or B sources that were opened, and none rests only on Wikipedia. The biggest fix: the 2004 Interior Board of Indian Appeals decision did not say Bennett County is reservation land. It says the parties agreed Bennett County "was removed from the reservation" (Cook v. Parkinson, 1975), so the "courts split three ways" framing must change. Other fixes: the Wounded Knee toll, the gunnery range acreage, IHS spending "per person" (not "per patient"), the Whiteclay population, and the date of the Nebraska Supreme Court ruling (September 29, 2017).

## Section 3 claims checked

1. "The land base shrank in steps: the 1868 reservation lost the Black Hills in 1877, about half of what remained in 1889, and then settler land sales in Bennett County after 1910." **VERIFIED.** Kneip (1877, 1889); Cook v. Parkinson (1910 act opened Bennett County land). Note that Bennett County's legal status is contested in the record (entry 6).
2. "The Supreme Court itself found that the 1877 taking required payment, so that link is on the record." **VERIFIED.** Sioux Nation, 448 U.S. 371: "the 1877 Act effected a taking of tribal property."
3. "Courts and the Census give dates and counts for each loss, but no source opened here measures how much each step cut income on Pine Ridge, so for income the honest phrase is that the timing lines up." **VERIFIED** as a statement about the sources. Acreage figures for 1868, 1877, 1889 and the gunnery range are sourced. The acreage lost in the 1910 Bennett County opening is not in any source opened here. A search summary gave 169,592 acres opened, but that source was not opened.
4. "The U.S. Commission on Civil Rights says the IHS has been underfunded for years, and the life expectancy gap between this county and the nation stayed near 12 years from 1980 to 2014; the timing lines up, but neither source proves one caused the other." **VERIFIED.** USCCR p. 66: "Over the years, Native American health care has been chronically underfunded." Gap: 73.8 - 61.3 = 12.5 years (1980); 79.1 - 66.8 = 12.3 years (2014), from ICT (county) and JAMA (nation). The county values should be confirmed in the IHME file (see pull list).
5. "Whiteclay alcohol sales and the relocation program added pressure in later decades, and the tribe's own alcohol ban and its 2012 lawsuit show it pushed back on both." **CHANGED / partly UNSUPPORTED.** The ban and the February 2012 lawsuit are confirmed (AP, Feb. 9, 2012). No source opened shows the tribe pushing back on relocation. "Added pressure" is a judgment no source states. Suggested wording: "Whiteclay beer sales and the federal relocation program came in later decades. The tribe's alcohol ban and its 2012 lawsuit against the Whiteclay stores and brewers show it pushed back on the beer sales."
