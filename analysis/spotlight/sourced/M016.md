## Section 3 claims checked

1. "By 1900 most Black farm families in Coahoma County worked land they did not own, and by 1930 almost every farmer was a tenant or sharecropper." **VERIFIED** (tier B). Mississippi Encyclopedia, "Coahoma County": 6% of Black farm households owned land in 1900; "94 percent of all farmers were tenants or sharecroppers" in 1930. Census of Agriculture table not reached.
2. "When cotton prices fell around 1920, the Illinois Central line out of Clarksdale became a main route to Chicago (Lemann)." **UNSUPPORTED** until the Lemann page is pulled. Only Wikipedia's summary of Lemann p. 6 was read.
3. "The first all-machine cotton crop was grown at Hopson in 1944, and witnesses at the 1967 Senate hearing said machines and federal acreage rules had put thousands of sharecroppers out of work." **CHANGED.** The 1944 crop is verified (Holley, EH.Net). For the hearing, a 1967 news report says witnesses "told of widespread hunger and unemployment among farm workers displaced by mechanization and reduced cotton acreage" (*The Carolinian*, April 15, 1967). Suggested wording: "...and witnesses at the 1967 Senate hearing in Jackson described farm workers left jobless by machines and by cuts in cotton acreage." "Thousands" needs the hearing transcript.
4. "Historian Donald Holley argues the arrow also ran the other way: people leaving for better wages pushed planters to buy machines." **VERIFIED** (tier A). Holley: "The evidence is overwhelming that migration greatly accelerated mechanization"; mechanization "accounted for less than 40 percent of the decrease in handpicking."
5. "When the public schools fully integrated in 1970, nearly all white students moved to private academies, and in 2015 the county's private schools were still 7% Black while its public schools were more than 90% Black." **CHANGED.** The source gives one school's count, not a county-wide one. Suggested wording: "When Clarksdale's schools fully integrated in February 1970, 574 of 585 white students at the junior high did not return, and Lee Academy opened that fall with 654 students." The 2015 figures are verified (Hechinger, tier B).
6. "The county peaked in 1950, the city peaked in 1970, and both have shrunk every decade since 2000; the timing lines up with each of these changes, but no single source ties the recent losses to one cause." **PARTLY VERIFIED.** County peak 1950 (49,361) and declines 2000 to 2020 for both county (30,622; 26,151; 21,390) and city (20,645; 17,962; 14,903) are confirmed from census data (NBER copy and Census API). The city's 1970 peak needs the 1970 census report. The census also lists Clarksdale as having "gained territory" between 1990 and 2000 (PHC-3-26), which supports the deep file's caution about the city series.
