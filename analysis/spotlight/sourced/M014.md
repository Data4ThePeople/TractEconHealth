## Section 3 claims checked

1. "Clarksville's decline started with cotton." **UNSUPPORTED as written.** It is a framing claim; TSHA shows the cotton trade was hit hard in the 1930s, but no source says the decline "started with" cotton. Suggested: "Clarksville's long decline shows up first in cotton."
2. "The county's crop and population both peaked in 1920, when most farmers did not own the land they worked, and by 1970 the county had lost 60% of its people." **CHANGED.** The 1920 peaks and the 1970 loss are VERIFIED (TSHA Red River County). The farm ownership figure is from 1930, not 1920. Suggested: "The county's cotton crop and population both peaked in 1920. By 1930, fewer than 30% of its farmers owned all or part of their farms, and by 1970 the county had lost 60% of its people."
3. "A federal court found in 1983 that the town's housing authority assigned tenants by race, and the city's complexes were still split by race in 1988." **VERIFIED** (Tarlton Law Library; October 11, 1983 ruling; 1988 shares 33%, 48%, 89% Black).
4. "In the same years, three local banks failed between 1985 and 1990 (FDIC), and the town began a 40-year population loss after its 1980 peak; the timing lines up, but no source here ties the bank failures to the population loss." **VERIFIED** (FDIC failures API; TSHA and Census 2020 population). The caveat stays.
5. "School enrollment fell 64% from 1990 to 2022, and the hospital closed in 2014; its operator blamed low use and payment cuts." **VERIFIED** (NCES CCD 1,466 to 522; Sheps Center 2014; KETR quotes from ETMC's statement).
6. "In 2010-2015, the old tract had the county's shortest life expectancy, 71.3 years." **VERIFIED.** A. CDC NCHS USALEEP, Texas file TX_A.CSV (https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Datasets/NVSS/USALEEP/CSV/TX_A.CSV): tract 48387950500 e(0) 71.3; the other Red River tracts are 77.1, 78.7 and 76.5.

Not in section 3 but worth adding as a sentence the sources now support: the 1985 opinion found that HUD "intentionally and knowingly continued to promote purposefully segregated housing" across 36 East Texas counties.
