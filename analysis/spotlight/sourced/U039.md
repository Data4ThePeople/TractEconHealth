## Section 3 claims checked

1. "The city's own 2024 grant application draws the line from the 1910 ordinance through racial covenants and the 1937 map to today: residents... 'could not access credit,' and 'these patterns of red lining are echoed in today's maps...'" VERIFIED. City of Baltimore 2024 application, p. 8.
2. "A federal court found that the housing authority placed its earliest public housing for Black families (1937 to 1943), including Gilmor Homes, inside existing Black neighborhoods, and later put scattered-site units in Sandtown, Harlem Park, Franklin Square and Upton; it found that the majority of scattered-site units 'are located in high poverty Black neighborhoods.'" VERIFIED. Thompson v. HUD, Supplemental Findings A and B.4, and scattered-site section.
3. "The city says the highway took 971 homes and 62 businesses and that West Baltimore communities 'have continued to face blight and disinvestment in the decades since.'" VERIFIED. City of Baltimore 2024 application, p. 3.
4. "Our census numbers show the neighborhood lost 20% of its people in the 1970s, the decade of highway clearance; the timing lines up." VERIFIED against history_final.csv (40,903 in 1970, 32,896 in 1980, fit 0.911). No causal source; worded as timing only.
5. "The Justice Policy Institute reports that the community with the most people in prison also faces the most concentrated problems with work, education, health and housing, but it does not claim one causes the other." VERIFIED. JPI/PPI 2015, p. 18: "it also faces the most concentrated challenges related to work, education, health, and housing."
6. "The Health Department reports life expectancy several years below the city's in all three areas." VERIFIED. 2017 profiles: 3.6 to 5.4 years below 73.6.
