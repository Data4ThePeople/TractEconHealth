## Section 3 claims checked

1. "North Birmingham was built as worker housing next to coke ovens, pipe plants and furnaces, and the city's racial zoning and the HOLC map marked it as a place for Black families and for industry, not for home loans." **CHANGED.** Racial zoning (Monk, 87 F. Supp. 538) and D grades (Mapping Inequality overlay) are supported. "Built as worker housing" has no source I opened. Suggested: "The plants sat next to the homes, the city zoned by race from 1926 until a court struck the law down in 1949, and federal appraisers graded most of the area D."
2. Allen et al. write that zoning "kept the location racially segregated." **VERIFIED** (Allen 2019: "government policy kept the location racially segregated through zoning ordinances for decades").
3. "...land use decisions left the neighborhoods isolated with few services." **VERIFIED** (Allen 2019, Sec. 2: "land use and zoning decisions have resulted in isolated neighborhoods with limited amenities").
4. "When the furnaces and pipe plants cut back or closed, from Sloss around 1970 to U.S. Pipe in 2010, the people left but the coke plants and their pollution stayed." **CHANGED.** U.S. Pipe 2010 (Mueller release) and the coke plants' continued TRI reports (TRI through 2021 and 2024) are supported. Sloss's closing furnaces were near downtown, outside the four tracts. "The people left" is supported only by our census numbers (population on the four kept tracts: 29,946 in 1980, 21,071 in 1990, 11,467 in 2010, per history_final.csv).
5. "...the timing of job loss and population loss lines up in our census numbers." **UNSUPPORTED.** The deep file's own check says the losses "run steadily from 1980 on, with no single break," and the 1970 row is too weak to use. No series shows the 1970 or 2010 closings lining up with a drop. Suggest dropping.
6. "EPA's soil tests found arsenic, lead and benzo(a)pyrene in hundreds of yards." **VERIFIED** (EPA NPL narrative: "Hundreds of residential lots are contaminated with benzo(a)pyrene, arsenic, or lead"; EPA 2021: 645 properties required cleanup).
7. "...a court found that a coke plant owner's lawyer and executive paid a legislator to fight the Superfund listing that would have widened the cleanup." **CHANGED.** A jury convicted Drummond's vice president and a partner at its outside law firm of bribing a state legislator through a contract with his foundation. The scheme opposed EPA's proposed NPL listing and the possible expansion of the site into Tarrant, where ABC Coke operates. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed (Roberson, 998 F.3d 1237). Suggested: "A federal jury convicted a Drummond Co. executive and the company's outside lawyer of bribing a state legislator to oppose EPA's plans for the site, and an appeals court upheld the convictions."
8. "Each step made the next one harder to escape, but the sources do not measure how much each one added." **UNSUPPORTED.** No source makes this claim; it implies causality. Suggest dropping.

Related caveat (deep section 6), checked: the 2015 ATSDR air assessment found current particulate exposures "unlikely to result in harmful effects in individuals" (Conclusion 2), but also found past (1999-2012) particulate levels "could have resulted in harmful effects in sensitive individuals" (Conclusion 1) and past air toxics at two stations above EPA's cancer target range (Conclusion 5). https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/NorthBirminghamAirSite/35th%20Avenue%20Site_PHA_Final_04-21-2015_508.pdf. ATSDR's January 2017 soil health consultation concludes that past and current exposure to arsenic and lead in some yards "could harm people's health," and that long-term exposure to PAHs in some yards "increases the risk of cancer." https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/35thAvenueSite/35th_Ave_Site_Soil_HC_Final_01-18-2017_508.pdf. The JCDH 2000-2009 mortality study is known only through ATSDR and Allen, which cite an al.com story (see pull list). The story should present both health findings.
