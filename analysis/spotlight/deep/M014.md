## 2. Timeline

1. **1872 or 1876.** The Texas and Pacific Railway reached the area. The two Handbook entries give different years. [TSHA, Clarksville](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/clarksville-tx-red-river-county); [TSHA, Red River County](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/red-river-county)
2. **1920-1970.** Red River County hit its peak population, 35,829, and its largest cotton crop in any census year, 46,263 bales, in 1920. By 1970 it was down to 14,298 people, 60% below the peak. [TSHA, Red River County](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/red-river-county); [NBER copy of Census counts](https://www.nber.org/research/data/census-us-decennial-county-population-data-1900-1990)
3. **1930.** Fewer than 30% of the county's farmers owned any of the land they worked. The rest rented the land they farmed. [TSHA, Red River County](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/red-river-county)
4. **1931-1936.** Rated businesses in Clarksville fell from 165 to 104 as the cotton trade was hit hard. [TSHA, Clarksville](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/clarksville-tx-red-river-county)
5. **1956.** The Paris and Mount Pleasant Railroad, built through the county's southwest corner in 1913, was abandoned. [TSHA, Red River County](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/red-river-county). Whether rail service through Clarksville itself was cut, and when, is UNCONFIRMED. A US 82 bypass of Clarksville is UNCONFIRMED.
6. **1979-1981.** 20.5% of county families were below the poverty line (1979). The county ranked 233rd of 254 Texas counties in per capita income (1981). [TSHA, Red River County](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/red-river-county)
7. **March 14, 1980.** Lucille Young and other public housing tenants filed *Young v. Pierce* in federal court in East Texas. Clarksville plaintiffs led the case. [Tarlton Law Library](https://tarlton.law.utexas.edu/william-wayne-justice/young-case); [Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/11152/)
8. **October 1983.** The court found the Clarksville Housing Authority had assigned tenants by race on purpose. The fix swapped 25 families from a white complex into two Black complexes, and 24 the other way. [Tarlton Law Library](https://tarlton.law.utexas.edu/william-wayne-justice/young-case)
9. **January 24, 1985.** First National Bank in Clarksville failed ($35.0 million in assets). Red River National Bank in Clarksville followed on March 19, 1987 ($22.8 million), and Citizens Bank on September 13, 1990 ($15.9 million). [FDIC failed bank data](https://api.fdic.gov/banks/failures?filters=CITYST:%22CLARKSVILLE,%20TX%22); [FDIC BankFind](https://banks.data.fdic.gov/bankfind-suite/failures). The Handbook says one local bank failed on "bad farm and energy loans" ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/clarksville-tx-red-river-county)). The causes of the other two are UNCONFIRMED.
10. **July 1985.** Judge William Wayne Justice ruled for the tenants in *Young v. Pierce*, a class action covering more than 70 housing authorities in 36 East Texas counties. [Tarlton Law Library](https://tarlton.law.utexas.edu/william-wayne-justice/young-case). The wording of the ruling (that HUD knowingly funded segregated housing) and the reporter citation are UNCONFIRMED. The opinion text could not be opened (Justia returned an error).
11. **1988.** Clarksville's three public housing complexes were still 33%, 48% and 89% Black. [Tarlton Law Library](https://tarlton.law.utexas.edu/william-wayne-justice/young-case)
12. **1990-2022.** Clarksville ISD enrollment fell from 1,466 to 522 students, a 64% drop (1,183 in 2000; 711 in 2010). [NCES Common Core of Data via the Urban Institute Education Data Portal](https://educationdata.urban.org/documentation/school-districts.html), district ID 4814190.
13. **2003-2004.** The *Young* case settled with 1,500 more desegregated housing opportunities across the region. It closed in 2004. [Tarlton Law Library](https://tarlton.law.utexas.edu/william-wayne-justice/young-case); [Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/11152/)
14. **August-December 2014.** East Texas Medical Center said it would leave its Clarksville hospital. It cited an ER that averaged less than one patient an hour, and Medicare and Medicaid payment cuts ([KETR, Aug. 7, 2014](https://www.ketr.org/news/2014-08-07/clarksville-could-lose-hospital)). The hospital at 3000 W. Main closed completely in December 2014 ([UNC Sheps Center list](https://www.shepscenter.unc.edu/programs-projects/rural-health/rural-hospital-closures/)). Whether a hospital has reopened is UNCONFIRMED.
15. **1980-2020.** Clarksville peaked at 4,917 people in 1980, then fell to 4,311 (1990) and 2,857 (2020), 42% below the peak ([TSHA, Clarksville](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/clarksville-tx-red-river-county); [Census 2020 PL](https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl)). Red River County had 11,587 people in 2020.

## 3. How the cuts compound

Clarksville's decline started with cotton. The county's crop and population both peaked in 1920, when most farmers did not own the land they worked, and by 1970 the county had lost 60% of its people ([TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/red-river-county)). A federal court found in 1983 that the town's housing authority assigned tenants by race, and the city's complexes were still split by race in 1988 ([Tarlton Law Library](https://tarlton.law.utexas.edu/william-wayne-justice/young-case)). In the same years, three local banks failed between 1985 and 1990 (FDIC), and the town began a 40-year population loss after its 1980 peak; the timing lines up, but no source here ties the bank failures to the population loss. School enrollment fell 64% from 1990 to 2022, and the hospital closed in 2014; its operator blamed low use and payment cuts ([KETR](https://www.ketr.org/news/2014-08-07/clarksville-could-lose-hospital)). In 2010-2015, the old tract had the county's shortest life expectancy, 71.3 years.

## 5. What makes it distinct

A federal court record names this town's own public housing as segregated by race, and the same complexes still house the poorest part of town. That record sits on top of a century of cotton loss and a decade of bank failures, followed by the loss of the hospital and two thirds of the school enrollment.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **The latest ACS does not show deep poverty.** Poverty in 9505.01 is 13.4%, and 14.8% across the outline, down from 28.6% in 1990. The median household income that put 9505.01 in the screen ($34,167) has a margin of plus or minus $19,915, wider than half the estimate. The ACS population for 9505.02 (1,692) is far below the 2020 count (2,178). These are signs of a noisy sample. The pick rests mostly on the health side (PLACES: diabetes 22.6%, obesity 46.3%) and on the very low employment rate (prime-age employment 30.2%, labor force participation 34.5%). Those two figures are also ACS-based and noisy. **This is the main risk to the pick.**
- **Adding 9505.02 makes the place less deep.** It is mostly rural and better off (health index 29, prime-age employment 94%). Show the old outline over time, and 9505.01 as today's pocket.
- **Triage and brief items not confirmed.** "HUD knowingly funded segregated housing" is the common summary of the 1985 ruling, but the opinion text was not opened. The highway bypass and railroad decline through Clarksville are UNCONFIRMED. The "1980s bank failure" was in fact three failures (1985, 1987, 1990).
- **Source conflicts.** The Handbook gives 1872 (town entry) and 1876 (county entry) for the railroad. Sheps lists the hospital at 43 beds, while KETR says 30.
- **Population drop 2000-2010.** Tract 9505 fell from 5,916 to 4,907 (down 17%) with no boundary change (the NHGIS 2000-2010 crosswalk is 100%). That is real loss or a counting change, not a map change. Worth a check in the 2000 and 2010 block data.
- **Our history numbers.** `history_long.csv` has only ACS 2020-2024 rows for M014. Rebuild 1990-2020 on 9505.01 + 9505.02 as above.
- **Causal claims.** The Handbook ties the 1930s business losses to the cotton trade, and the hospital operator ties the closing to low use and payment cuts. No cited source links the housing case, the bank failures or the school losses to one another; the story should say the timing lines up.
