The year of the move is UNCONFIRMED from a dated article.
- What the 186 "other noninstitutional" residents are (shelter, group home, treatment beds) is UNCONFIRMED.
- Effect: prison residents are left out of poverty counts, but "other noninstitutional" residents are counted. At most they are 7% of 1.02's poverty count base. Population charts should note that the 2030 count will drop by about 100 or more when the prerelease center is gone. That drop is a move, not people leaving.

## 2. Timeline

1. **1900-1920.** Silver Bow County grew from 47,635 to 60,313 people. The city of Butte counted 41,611 in 1920, its highest census count. County: [NBER copy of Census county counts 1900-1990](https://www.nber.org/research/data/census-us-decennial-county-population-data-1900-1990). City: [Wikipedia census table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte,_Montana) (secondary; confirm in NHGIS place tables).
2. **June 8, 1917.** The Speculator Mine fire killed 168 miners. Copper output peaked that year. About 15,000 workers then walked off the job. [Wikipedia, Butte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte,_Montana) (secondary). The peak number of Anaconda employees in Butte is UNCONFIRMED from a primary source.
3. **July 1955.** Open-pit mining began at the Berkeley Pit. The pit mined the upper Butte ore body until 1982. [Gammons and Duaime, MBMG, 2020](https://mbmg.mtech.edu/pdf/geologyvolume/GammonsDuaimeMineWatersFinal.pdf); month from [Wikipedia, Berkeley Pit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Pit).
4. **1950s-1970s.** Anaconda bought and tore down "thousands of houses and hundreds of commercial buildings" in Meaderville, East Butte, Finntown and McQueen to make room for the pit. Holy Savior church in McQueen was buried under waste rock. [Verdigris Project, Life Underground ep. 5](https://www.verdigrisproject.org/life-underground-blog/life-underground-episode-5-get-off-the-hill); [KPAX](https://www.kpax.com/news/montana-news/preserved-church-paintings-hold-memories-of-buttes-lost-neighborhoods). The exact years for each neighborhood are UNCONFIRMED. Sources range from the late 1950s to the 1970s.
5. **1971.** Chile took over American-owned copper mines, "causing a financial crisis" for Anaconda. [Montana Historical Society](https://mths.mt.gov/education/StoriesOfTheLand/Part2/Chapter10/Ch10Educators/ACCompany)
6. **November 1973.** The Columbia Gardens, the company-built park, burned. The Continental Pit was later dug on the site. [Wikipedia, Butte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte,_Montana) (secondary)
7. **1975.** Underground mining in Butte ended. [Gammons and Duaime, 2020](https://mbmg.mtech.edu/pdf/geologyvolume/GammonsDuaimeMineWatersFinal.pdf)
8. **1977.** Atlantic Richfield (ARCO) bought Anaconda ([MHS](https://mths.mt.gov/education/StoriesOfTheLand/Part2/Chapter10/Ch10Educators/ACCompany)). The same year, Butte and Silver Bow County merged their governments ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte,_Montana)).
9. **1980.** "Operations at the Anaconda Smelter ceased in 1980." The smelter is 25 miles west, in Anaconda. [EPA, Anaconda Co. Smelter site](https://cumulis.epa.gov/supercpad/SiteProfiles/index.cfm?fuseaction=second.cleanup&id=0800403). Jobs lost: UNCONFIRMED.
10. **April 22, 1982.** The mine pumps were turned off and Berkeley Pit mining stopped. The underground workings began to flood. **This was 1982, not 1983.** The water reached the pit floor and the pit lake formed in November 1983. [Gammons and Duaime, 2020](https://mbmg.mtech.edu/pdf/geologyvolume/GammonsDuaimeMineWatersFinal.pdf); [EPA release, Mar. 25, 2002](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/746732fc9e0255f185256b88005adc40.html)
11. **Dec. 30, 1982 / Sept. 8, 1983.** EPA proposed, then finalized, Silver Bow Creek on the Superfund National Priorities List. The Butte Area was added to the site in 1987. [EPA site milestones](https://www.epa.gov/butte/site-milestones); [EPA background](https://www.epa.gov/butte/background). The 1983 unemployment rate ("over 17%" in the triage note) is UNCONFIRMED. BLS county figures start in 1990 on the API.
12. **1986, then July 2000-November 2003.** Montana Resources reopened mining at the Continental Pit in 1986. Production stopped from July 2000 to November 2003 ([Gammons and Duaime](https://mbmg.mtech.edu/pdf/geologyvolume/GammonsDuaimeMineWatersFinal.pdf)). MSHA reports show average Continental Mine employment falling from 320 (2000) to 6 (2002), then back to 357 (2005) and 428 (2025) ([MSHA open data](https://arlweb.msha.gov/OpenGovernmentData/OGIMSHA.asp)).
13. **November 1995 and December 2016.** In 1995, 342 snow geese were found dead in the pit ([High Country News, Dec. 11, 1995](https://www.hcn.org/issues/49/1520); [EPA 2002](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/746732fc9e0255f185256b88005adc40.html)). In 2016, an estimated 3,000 to 4,000 died ([Gammons and Duaime](https://mbmg.mtech.edu/pdf/geologyvolume/GammonsDuaimeMineWatersFinal.pdf)).
14. **1988-August 2019.** Cleanup started in 1988. By August 2019, the Residential Metals Abatement Program had assessed 3,495 homes and cleaned 795 attics, 484 yards and 150 lead paint jobs. [BPSOU history](https://bpsou.com/history/). The share of Butte children with high blood lead fell more than 80% over about 20 years, but it is still above the national rate. The main remaining source is lead paint in older homes. [MTPR, Mar. 2, 2023, on the 2020 RMAP medical monitoring report](https://www.mtpr.org/montana-news/2023-03-02/study-finds-fewer-children-with-elevated-blood-lead-levels-in-butte)
15. **September 21, 2026.** EPA cut the residential soil lead cleanup level from 1,200 ppm to 456 ppm. It grew the program area by about 3,627 acres, adding more than 7,000 properties, with about 15 years of work ahead. [EPA news release](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-announces-record-decision-amendment-implement-stricter-lead-cleanup-level-and)

Population today: Silver Bow County had 35,133 people in 2020, 42% fewer than in 1920 (Census 2020 PL; NBER for 1920).

## 3. How the cuts compound

Butte's loss came in steps over 70 years, not in one blow. The Berkeley Pit replaced underground mining after 1955, and the company bought out and tore down whole east side neighborhoods to dig it ([Verdigris Project](https://www.verdigrisproject.org/life-underground-blog/life-underground-episode-5-get-off-the-hill)). Chile's 1971 takeover put Anaconda in a "financial crisis" ([MHS](https://mths.mt.gov/education/StoriesOfTheLand/Part2/Chapter10/Ch10Educators/ACCompany)), and the timing lines up with the end of underground mining in 1975, the sale to ARCO in 1977, the smelter closing in 1980 and the pit closing in 1982. EPA says that when pumping stopped in 1982, the pit began filling with acid mine drainage ([EPA 2002](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/746732fc9e0255f185256b88005adc40.html)), so the place lost its jobs and gained a toxic lake in the same year. Residents of old tract 1 then lived through decades of yard and attic cleanup, with blood lead falling but still above the national rate ([MTPR](https://www.mtpr.org/montana-news/2023-03-02/study-finds-fewer-children-with-elevated-blood-lead-levels-in-butte)). In 2010-2015 the tract had the county's shortest life expectancy, 70.4 years. The timing lines up with this history, but no cited source links the tract's health to it directly.

## 5. What makes it distinct

This is the only spotlight where the employer dug up neighboring blocks for its mine, then left a toxic lake beside downtown that still has to be pumped and treated. The cleanup is not over: in September 2026, EPA set a stricter lead standard that adds more than 7,000 properties.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Brief and triage errors.** The pumps went off on April 22, 1982, not in 1983. The 1983 Superfund listing covered Silver Bow Creek; Butte was added in 1987. The 186 group quarters residents in 1.02 are "other noninstitutional," not college students.
- **Adding 1.01 makes the place less deep today.** Tract 1.01 is not low income (poverty 12.1%, median income $44,138, health index 51). The combined poverty rate is 28.5%, compared with 39.6% for 1.02 alone. The story should show both: the old tract over time, and 1.02 as the pocket that is worst today.
- **Poverty in the old tract has not risen.** It has stayed near one third since 1990 and dipped in the latest ACS. The big losses (jobs, people) came before 1990, and there is no tract data before 1990. Earlier decline has to be shown with county and city series.
- **County unemployment is low now** (3.4% in 2024, BLS). The story is about long-run loss and health, not current job loss.
- **Our history numbers.** `history_long.csv` has only ACS 2020-2024 rows for M002. The 1990-2010 rows were never built because the screen flagged the boundary. Rebuild on 1.01 + 1.02. The 2.3% unemployment rate for 1.02 rests on 29 unemployed people. With a 52% labor force participation rate, it is not a sign of a healthy job market. ACS shows 468 people in group quarters, but the 2020 census shows 286. Use the census figure.
- **Secondary sources.** The 1917 figures, the 1920 city count, the 1973 Columbia Gardens fire and the July 1955 start date come from Wikipedia. They should be checked against Census and MBMG records before publishing.
- **Health links.** No cited source ties this tract's life expectancy to mine waste. Lead paint in old homes is named as the main current lead risk ([MTPR](https://www.mtpr.org/montana-news/2023-03-02/study-finds-fewer-children-with-elevated-blood-lead-levels-in-butte)). Smoking (27.8% in PLACES) and poverty are likely large factors.
- **Method note for the team.** `DATASETS.md` says NHGIS has no 1970 or 1980 tract crosswalk, but `data/raw/nhgis/` holds `nhgis_tr1970_tr2010.zip` and `nhgis_tr1980_tr2010.zip`. That line may be out of date.
