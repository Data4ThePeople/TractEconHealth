## 3. How the cuts compound

Rocky Ford grew on two things: melons and sugar beets, both fed by ditch water. The end of federal sugar quotas in 1974 is described by the Colorado Sun as killing the valley's largest industry, and the Rocky Ford sugar plant closed five years later. The plant's water rights were then bought and sold to Aurora, and a local water official says about 7,500 acres on the Rocky Ford Ditch dried up. Food processing jobs in the county fell by about half from 1987 to 1994, and the town lost 13% of its people in the 1980s; the timing lines up with the plant closing and the water sale, but no source I found measures how much each one caused. More sales followed in 2003 and 2024, and the 2011 listeria outbreak put the "Rocky Ford" name on a deadly recall even though the farm was about 75 miles away.

## 5. What makes it distinct

Rocky Ford's farmland was not lost to drought or bad prices alone: its water was bought by a growing city and moved off the land by contract. It is a farm town where the decline can be followed in water records, acre by acre.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **The key 1980 and 1987 deal numbers are not confirmed.** The 58% share, 8,200 acre-feet and $25.5 million come from a state record that would not open for me. Pull the record or the water court decree before publishing them.
- **The tract is only 61% of the town.** The south side (tract 9682, 1,524 people) is left out. Town-level series are a better fit for the history.
- **Population did not fall in a straight line.** The town grew from 4,162 (1990) to 4,286 (2000) before falling again. Our tract shows the same bump (2,966 to 3,008).
- **Food jobs bounced back in the mid 1990s** (347 in 1994 to 559 in 1997), so the job story is not a steady slide.
- **The listeria outbreak came from a farm in Prowers County, not Rocky Ford.** Any harm to local growers is UNCONFIRMED.
- **The 2024 Aurora deal is set up to keep land farmed**, which cuts against a simple "buy and dry" arc for recent years.
- **Our history numbers look off in places:**
  - `candidates.csv` lists the destination as "Census Tract 9685," but the tract is 9681.
  - The "ACS 2008-2012" owner share (51.29) exactly matches the 2010 census value (51.29). I checked the 2010 census directly (336 + 240 owners of 1,123 homes = 51.3%), so the ACS row looks like a copy of the census row, not a separate estimate.
  - Poverty swings from 44.2% (1990) to 27.6% (2000) to 39.6% (2008-2012). Unemployment goes from 19.2% (2000) to 7.3% (2020-2024). These small-sample numbers are noisy and should not be charted as a trend without error bars.
  - ACS 2020-2024 population is 2,736, but the 2020 count is 2,529.
- The 1928 melon shipping peak (3,000 rail cars) in the triage notes came from a search summary and is UNCONFIRMED.
- Whether the Holly Sugar plant in nearby Swink closed, and when, is UNCONFIRMED.
