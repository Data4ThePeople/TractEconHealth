# U034 deep dive: North Birmingham (Collegeville, Harriman Park, Fairmont, North Birmingham), Birmingham, AL

Prepared 2026-11-19. Facts below were checked against the URL given with each one. Anything not checked against a source is marked UNCONFIRMED. Numbers labeled "our pull" or "our overlay" were computed for this note and still need a tie-out.

## 2. Timeline

1. **1900.** Sloss-Sheffield Steel & Iron held nearly 500 men, almost all Black, at its "SSS&I Convict Prison" in North Birmingham, leased for $9 to $10 a month and paid nothing. They were close to half the Sloss workforce. Mitchell and Frederickson, Facing South / Southern Exposure, 1984: https://www.facingsouth.org/1984/11/steel-workers-boom-town
2. **1902 and 1910.** North Birmingham incorporates in 1902 with about 3,500 people and is annexed into Birmingham in 1910. Allen et al. 2019, IJERPH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6617205/
3. **1918 to 1920.** Construction of the ABC Coke plant (Alabama By-Products Corp.) begins in 1918, next to what is now tract 0055. The Sloss coke plant at 3500 35th Ave N (later Walter Coke, ERP, Bluestone) has run on its site since 1920. Allen et al. 2019 (above).
4. **1926 to 1950.** Birmingham writes racial zones into its zoning law, which made it a crime for Black and white residents to live in each other's zones (General City Code 1944, Sections 1604-1605). A federal district court strikes the law down on December 16, 1949, and the Fifth Circuit affirms on December 19, 1950. City of Birmingham v. Monk, 185 F.2d 859: https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/city-of-birmingham-v-885951209 . The 1926 adoption year comes from secondary sources (Connerly, "The Most Segregated City in America," 2005) and is UNCONFIRMED against the ordinance itself. Allen et al. 2019 say the city "chose to ignore" the 1917 Supreme Court ruling against racial zoning.
5. **Late 1930s.** HOLC grades the area. Our overlay of the Mapping Inequality polygons on 2020 tracts: D ("hazardous") covers 64% of tract 0005, 41% of 0007, 42% of 0008 and 50% of 0055. No part of any kept tract got an A or B. Most of the rest was mapped as "Industrial." Source data: https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/ . Map year (1937 is often cited) and the text of area descriptions D1, D4, D9 and D17 are UNCONFIRMED.
6. **1970 or 1971.** Sloss Furnaces stops making iron. Encyclopedia of Alabama says 1970 (https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/?p=15223); Facing South says Jim Walter Corp. closed the furnaces in 1971 (above). Exact year UNCONFIRMED. Jim Walter Corp. bought the business in 1969.
7. **November 18, 1971.** At EPA's request, U.S. District Judge Sam Pointer Jr. orders 23 Birmingham industries to shut down during an air pollution emergency, the first use of the Clean Air Act's emergency power. AP report in the Michigan Daily: https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/midaily/mdp.39015071754407/633 ; GASP: https://gaspgroup.org/a-toast-to-clean-air-honoring-the-past-committing-to-the-future/
8. **1987 to 1989.** The first years of federal Toxics Release Inventory reports. Our pull of EPA Envirofacts TRI data: the 35th Avenue coke plant (TRI ID 35207SLSSN35003) reported 1,401,515 pounds of air releases in 1989; ABC Coke (35217BCCKDRAILR) reported 881,136 pounds of air releases in 1988, about 850,000 of them benzene. By 2019 the two plants reported 25,180 and 49,104 pounds. https://data.epa.gov/efservice/tri_reporting_form/tri_facility_id/35207SLSSN35003/tri_release_qty/JSON
9. **2010.** U.S. Pipe's North Birmingham plant (3000 30th Ave N, tract 0008) files its last TRI report for 2010 (our pull), which fits the reported 2010 shutdown (https://www.bhamwiki.com/w/US_Pipe). Closure date and job count UNCONFIRMED against a primary source.
10. **2011 to 2012.** EPA uses emergency Superfund authority after finding contamination leaving the coke plant site (2011), and in October 2012 starts asking 2,049 property owners for access to test yards. Allen et al. 2019 (above).
11. **September 2014.** EPA proposes the 35th Avenue site for the Superfund National Priorities List. The site: more than 2,000 properties and more than 1,000 acres in Collegeville, Fairmont and Harriman Park; benzo(a)pyrene, arsenic and lead in yard soil; Walter Coke's 122 coke ovens, ABC Coke, U.S. Pipe and others named as possible sources; more than 1,000 properties sampled. EPA narrative: https://19january2021snapshot.epa.gov/sites/static/files/2014-09/documents/ns-35th-avenue-9-2-2014.pdf . Allen et al. give the date as September 22, 2014, with a Hazard Ranking score of 50.
12. **2017 to 2018 (court record).** A federal grand jury indicts a Balch & Bingham partner (Joel Gilbert) and a Drummond Co. vice president (David Roberson) in United States v. Gilbert, No. 2:17-cr-00419 (N.D. Ala.), filed September 2017. Former state Rep. Oliver Robinson pleads guilty in September 2017. On July 20, 2018, a jury convicts Gilbert and Roberson on six counts, including bribery, conspiracy, honest services wire fraud and money laundering conspiracy, for paying Robinson through a contract with his foundation to oppose EPA's plans to list and expand the site. Drummond owns ABC Coke. Robinson is sentenced to 33 months on September 27, 2018. Sources: Georgetown Environmental Law Review: https://www.law.georgetown.edu/environmental-law-review/blog/birmingham-bribery-trial-ends-but-who-should-pay-for-the-35th-avenue-superfund-site/ ; ENR: https://www.enr.com/articles/44902-execs-convicted-in-alabama-bribe-scheme-over-cleanup-site ; DOJ: https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndal/pr/federal-jury-convicts-lawyer-and-coal-company-executive-bribing-state-legislator . Payment amount ($360,000 or $375,000; reports differ), Gilbert's and Roberson's sentences, and the appeal outcome are UNCONFIRMED.
13. **2016 to 2019.** Walter Coke goes through bankruptcy; ERP Compliant Coke buys the plant in February 2016 (Allen et al. 2019), and Bluestone Resources buys ERP in 2019 (https://businessalabama.com/birmingham-coke-plant-sold/).
14. **2021.** Bluestone Coke stops all operations after the Jefferson County Department of Health sued to close it. SELC says the plant had violated its air permit for "over a decade." SELC, December 7, 2021: https://www.selc.org/?p=18293 . TRI air releases in 2021: 4,816 pounds (our pull). Exact shutdown date UNCONFIRMED.
15. **July 2022.** EPA's on-scene coordinator reports about 2,030 of about 2,100 properties sampled, about 669 needing cleanup and 643 cleaned. The site is still not on the final NPL. Southern Science, September 19, 2022: https://southern-science.com/2022/09/19/birminghams-pollution-master-class/ . Current status after 2022 UNCONFIRMED.

**Check against our census numbers (history_long.csv, all eight original tracts, rows with fit 0.8 or more):** population fell from 45,575 (1980) to 39,107 (1990), 30,884 (2000), 24,542 (2010) and 23,340 (2020), a 49% drop over 40 years. Unemployment was 15.4% in 1980 and 15.5% in 1990, then 20.3% in 2000 and 25.8% in 2008-2012. Vacancy rose from 13.1% (1990) to 21.5% (2010). The losses run steadily from 1980 on, with no single break, which fits a slow, many-cause decline. The 1970 rows have fit 0.70 and are left out. Life expectancy (USALEEP 2010-2015) in the four kept tracts: 66.8 (0005), 70.1 (0007), 66.7 (0008), 66.5 (0055).

## 3. How the cuts compound

North Birmingham was built as worker housing next to coke ovens, pipe plants and furnaces, and the city's racial zoning and the HOLC map marked it as a place for Black families and for industry, not for home loans. Allen et al. (2019) write that zoning "kept the location racially segregated" and that land use decisions left the neighborhoods isolated with few services. When the furnaces and pipe plants cut back or closed, from Sloss around 1970 to U.S. Pipe in 2010, the people left but the coke plants and their pollution stayed; the timing of job loss and population loss lines up in our census numbers. EPA's soil tests found arsenic, lead and benzo(a)pyrene in hundreds of yards, and a court found that a coke plant owner's lawyer and executive paid a legislator to fight the Superfund listing that would have widened the cleanup. Each step made the next one harder to escape, but the sources do not measure how much each one added.

## 5. What makes it distinct

This is a neighborhood where the pollution source is still inside the tract lines and the cleanup fight produced a federal bribery conviction, so the environmental layer has court records, EPA counts and 35 years of plant-level emissions data. The other layers (convict labor, racial zoning, HOLC D grades and the loss of steel and pipe jobs) all sit on the same few square miles.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Health studies cut both ways.** Allen et al. (2019) report that a 2015 ATSDR air assessment found current air exposures "unlikely to result in harmful effects," and that the Jefferson County Department of Health found no significant difference in 2000-2009 cancer, asthma and COPD death rates compared with the rest of the county. The story should not claim the plants caused the low life expectancy. Our data show the two together; no source here shows cause.
- **TRI is self-reported, and early years differ.** The steep drop after 1990-1993 may reflect changes in what plants had to report and how they estimated releases, not only cleaner operations. Our TRI totals are a first pass (deduplicated by form and medium) and need a tie-out against TRI Explorer before use.
- **The site was proposed, not listed.** The 35th Avenue site was proposed for the NPL in 2014 and, as of the 2022 sources, never added. Cleanup has gone on under EPA removal authority.
- **Our history numbers have problems.** (a) The 2020 "standardized to 2010 tracts" population (23,340) is 36% above the 2020-2024 ACS count for the eight 2020 tracts (17,151), because the 2010-tract geography includes all of old tracts 0024 and 0027, not just 0024.02 and 0027.02, and includes the jail. (b) Percent Black drops from 84.6% (2010) to 73.1% (2020) and is back to 84.7% in the 2020-2024 ACS. A swing that large in the 2020 census row looks like a definition or geography problem; check it before charting. (c) Unemployment falls from 25.8% (2008-2012) to 8.1% (2020-2024); part of this may be real recovery, but check whether the jail and prison tracts affect the older rows. (d) All rows cover the original eight tracts, including the jail and prison tracts we drop. Rebuild on the four kept tracts.
- **1970 rows are out** (fit 0.70), so the chart can start in 1980, which misses the Sloss closure and the 1971 emergency in the numbers.
- **Regional vs. local jobs.** U.S. Steel's Fairfield cuts (2015) and Ensley closure (1976) are regional, not in these tracts. Use them only as county context.
- **Neighborhood names** for tract 0005 and the exact edges of Harriman Park and Fairmont were not checked against the City of Birmingham's official neighborhood map.
