# BOS_NUBIAN: Nubian Square (formerly Dudley Square), Roxbury, Boston

Deep research, November 19, 2026. Tracts 25025080300 (tract 803) and 25025080401 (tract 804.01). Grove Hall (U064) work is not repeated here. Census history for these tracts will be computed separately; the few 2020 census counts below are only for the boundary and group quarters check.

## 1. Boundary

**Where the tracts sit.** The two tracts cover the east side of Nubian Square and the land north of it toward Melnea Cass Boulevard. They do not hold the square's commercial core. Washington Street is the west edge of both tracts, and the buildings on its west side (the Bolling Building, the old Ferdinand's site and the Nubian bus station) fall in tract 806.01. Checked with the Census geocoder (2020 tracts): 2400 Washington St geocodes to 817, odd-numbered Washington St addresses 2125 to 2263 to 804.01, and our point for the station falls in 806.01.

What this means:
- **Neither tract is one complex.** Orchard Gardens, the 331-unit HOPE VI rebuild of Orchard Park, is split across both tracts. Adding the HUD-assisted and tax-credit properties, subsidized homes look like roughly two-thirds of all homes in each tract (803: about 555 of 871; 804.01: about 1,000 of 1,463). Treat these as rough: the lists can overlap (a tax-credit deal can sit on top of public housing), and LPI Portfolio is a scattered-site property recorded at one address. A story should say "most homes here are subsidized," not give an exact share, unless the Boston Housing Authority or the city's income-restricted housing list confirms it.
- **Low income here partly reflects where subsidized housing was placed.** Income limits for these homes keep incomes low by design. That is part of the story (where clearance land went), but it is also a caveat (Section 6).
- **Group quarters in 804.01: 387 people (11% of the tract) in "other noninstitutional" quarters.** That category usually means shelters, group homes or treatment residences. I could not identify the facility: UNCONFIRMED. It is not a college dorm (0 in that line) or a prison. Resolve before publishing, since a large shelter population would pull down income and health numbers. Tract 803 has only 35.
- **Whittier Street** public housing and most of the Madison Park campus (Madison Park High School, Beryl Gardens) are in tract 806.01, not in these two tracts. Tract 806.01 also holds 2,484 people in college dorms, so it should stay out.
- **Dudley Street Neighborhood Initiative.** Its office at 504 Dudley St geocodes to tract 906, about a mile east. The Dudley Triangle is not in these tracts.

## 2. Timeline

1. **June 10, 1901.** The Boston Elevated Railway opened the Main Line Elevated over Washington Street, with its terminal at Dudley Square. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_station (citing Belcher, *Changes to Transit Service in the MBTA District*, http://www.transithistory.org/roster/MBTARouteHistory.pdf)
2. **1938.** The Home Owners' Loan Corporation map graded all of tract 803 and 92% of tract 804.01 as "D" (red, "hazardous"), area D9; the other 8% of 804.01 is in red area D8. Computed for this note by overlaying the Mapping Inequality polygons on 2020 tract shapes. https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining . The wording of the D9 area description: UNCONFIRMED (the description file would not download). A secondary source reports the Black share in the D9 area at 5% in 1950, 20% in 1960 and 53% in 1970, citing Medoff and Sklar, *Streets of Hope* (1994); not checked against the book.
3. **1941 to 1942.** The Boston Housing Authority built Orchard Park, 350 units of three-story brick public housing on Eustis Street, in what is now tract 803 and 804.01. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard_Park_Projects
4. **February 1, 1948.** A state Joint Board delivered the Master Highway Plan for the Boston Metropolitan Area, which drew an Inner Belt loop across Roxbury and an expressway along the Boston-Providence rail line (later the Southwest Expressway, part of I-95). Federal law in 1956 shifted 90% of the cost to the federal government. https://whenandwhereinboston.org/entry/master-highway-plan-for-the-boston-metropolitan-area ; https://archive.org/details/masterhighwaypla00char ; *Boston Globe*, December 6, 2022, https://www.boston.com/news/the-boston-globe/2022/12/06/transformative-decision-a-half-century-ago-to-scrap-i-95-extension-still-resonates-in-boston-today/
5. **1961 to April 22, 1963.** The second Washington Park urban renewal plan covered 502 acres, called for tearing down 2,600 housing units and moving about 2,200 families, and for 1,500 new units. The City Council approved it February 18, 1963, and the federal government April 22, 1963. https://whenandwhereinboston.org/entry/boston-housing-authority-development-division-washington-park-urban-renewal-plan-first . Segregation by Design gives a higher count for the Nubian Square area (about 2,600 families, and about 60% of structures leveled), including the 1852 Dudley Street Baptist Church, replaced by a police station. https://www.segregationbydesign.com/boston/dudley-street-baptist-church . Whether the Washington Park area took in tracts 803 or 804.01: UNCONFIRMED. The renewal area lies mostly south and west of the square; overlay the BRA map (https://collections.leventhalmap.org/search/commonwealth:4168b121m) to check.
6. **1960s.** State highway takings cleared land for the Southwest Expressway and the Inner Belt (I-695). The Inner Belt would have run along today's Melnea Cass Boulevard, the north edge of tract 804.01, and the two highways would have met about half a mile northwest of the square. Next City (Erick Trickey, August 14, 2017), https://nextcity.org/features/view/fixing-urban-renewal-highways-cities-black-boston-neighborhoods . Across Roxbury, Jamaica Plain and the South End, more than 500 homes and businesses were razed and "hundreds of acres" cleared (*Boston Globe*, 2022, link above). Acres cleared in or next to these two tracts alone: UNCONFIRMED.
7. **January 5, 1967.** After the Boston Redevelopment Authority planned to clear Lower Roxbury's Madison Park area for a citywide high school and the Inner Belt, residents formed the Lower Roxbury Community Corporation and signed an agreement with the BRA setting aside part of a 57-acre area for new homes; they had proposed 400 affordable homes. The group became Madison Park Development Corporation, which built Madison Park Village (parts of which, Madison Park III and IV, are in tract 804.01). *Banker & Tradesman*, January 2, 2022, https://www.bankerandtradesman.com/this-month-in-history-mpdc-is-born/
8. **June 2 to 4, 1967.** The welfare office sit-in and three nights of unrest were on Blue Hill Avenue in Grove Hall, about 1.5 miles south, not in these tracts. https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2017/5/22/roxbury-riots-1967/ . No source found ties 1967 or April 1968 damage to Dudley Square itself: UNCONFIRMED.
9. **February 1970 to November 30, 1972.** The governor ordered a halt to new expressway building inside Route 128 in February 1970 and canceled the Inner Belt and Southwest Expressway for good on November 30, 1972. A 1969 "People Before Highways" rally drew about 2,000 people. The cleared land stayed mostly empty for decades. https://historycambridge.org/innerbelt/chronology.html ; *Boston Globe* 2022 (link above); Bay State Banner, August 1, 2019, https://baystatebanner.com/2019/08/01/tremont-crossing-project-to-begin-site-preparation
10. **1979 to April 30, 1987.** Ferdinand's, the square's anchor store, closed in 1979. The last trains ran on the Washington Street Elevated on April 30, 1987, and the Orange Line reopened May 4 along the Southwest Corridor, half a mile west. Sales at the square's remaining businesses fell 30%, and the Ferdinand Building stood empty for about four decades. Next City, 2017 (link above); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Street_Elevated
11. **Mid to late 1980s.** Crack cocaine dealers moved into Orchard Park, and a gang based there became known for violence. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard_Park_Projects (citing the *Boston Phoenix* and *Boston Globe*, May 14, 2015)
12. **1996 to 1999.** Orchard Park won a federal HOPE VI grant in 1996; it was torn down from 1996 to 1998 and rebuilt as Orchard Gardens, 331 units on a 16-acre site, finished in 1999 at a total cost of $159 million. https://bostonhousing.org/Departments/Planning-and-Real-Estate-Development/Mixed-Finance-Development/Orchard-Gardens.aspx ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard_Park_Projects
13. **July 20, 2002.** In place of the light rail the MBTA had promised when the El came down, Silver Line buses began running from Dudley to Downtown Crossing, after about 15 years of debate. Critics note it lacks fully dedicated lanes and makes fewer stops than the old 49 bus. A 2012 state study again recommended light rail on Washington Street as a long-term project. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Street_Elevated ; https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2018/03/02/RDMreport.pdf
14. **2015 to 2023.** The $125 million Bruce C. Bolling Municipal Building opened on the old Ferdinand's site in 2015 (west side of Washington St, tract 806.01). The square was renamed Nubian Square in December 2019. In January 2023 the city's planning agency picked a developer for Parcel P-3, cleared highway land nearby that had sat empty for decades. Next City, 2017 (link above); https://www.bostonherald.com/2019/12/19/roxburys-dudley-square-renamed-nubian-square/ ; https://bostonplans.org/news-calendar/news-updates/2023/01/19/bpda-designates-community-recommended-team-to-rede . At 1127 Harrison Avenue (tract 804.01), a civil rights group says at least 10 minority-owned businesses left ahead of a redevelopment, and others got eviction notices. https://lawyersforcivilrights.org/our-impact/immigrant-rights/wrongful-eviction-of-black-businesses/ . Date of that dispute: UNCONFIRMED (page is undated).
15. **2003-2007 and 2015-2021 deaths.** A 2012 Virginia Commonwealth University report for the city found a 33-year gap between the highest tract (Back Bay, 91.9 years) and the lowest (Roxbury, 58.9). Which Roxbury tract had 58.9: UNCONFIRMED (the 2012 report itself was not found online; BPHC names only "Roxbury"). Using 2015-2021 deaths, BPHC found 68.8 years in tract 803 and 91.6 in Back Bay tract 107.02, a 22.8-year gap; median household income $41,211 against $141,250. BPHC report pp. 52-53 (link in Section 1); WBUR, May 11, 2023, https://www.wbur.org/news/2023/05/11/boston-life-expectancy-gap-back-bay-roxbury (WBUR prints the Roxbury income as $42,211; BPHC's table says $41,211). BPHC's 2025 *Live Long and Well* report repeats the 23-year gap and describes the two tracts as 2.3 miles apart. https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2025/01/BPHC%20Live%20Long%20and%20Well%20Report_Jan2025_FINAL.pdf

## 3. How the cuts compound

The 1938 map graded these blocks red, and the timing lines up with a fast change in who lived in the D9 area between 1950 and 1970 (a secondary count, to be checked against our census history). In the 1960s the state cleared land at the tracts' north edge for two highways that were never built, and the city's renewal agency planned to clear Lower Roxbury for a school and the Inner Belt; Next City and the *Globe* tie these takings to empty land that sat for decades. When the El closed in 1987, Next City reports that sales at the square's remaining businesses fell 30%, and the Ferdinand Building stayed empty for about 40 years. Much of the cleared land was later filled with subsidized housing, so most homes here now have income limits, and the timing lines up with low incomes and very low home ownership (under 20% in 803 and under 7% in 804.01 in 2020). BPHC links its tract life expectancy gap to "structural, social, and environmental factors" in general, but it does not tie the gap to any one of these events.

## 5. What makes it distinct from Grove Hall

Grove Hall is mainly a lending story, where a bank group's loan line and FHA foreclosures emptied one avenue. Nubian Square is a story of public projects: a red-graded area cleared for highways that were never built, an elevated train taken away in 1987 and replaced by a bus, and cleared land filled with subsidized housing, with BPHC's own lowest life expectancy tract at its center.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **The square itself is in a different tract.** The square's commercial core and bus station are across Washington Street in tract 806.01, which holds about 2,500 college dorm residents and should not be added. The story can say "the east side of Nubian Square and Lower Roxbury," not "Nubian Square" alone.
- **Subsidized housing sets incomes by design.** Roughly two-thirds of homes in each tract appear to be public housing, HUD-assisted or tax-credit units. Low median income partly measures that policy choice. The trend line can still show what came before the subsidized housing, but read today's income ranks with this in mind.
- **Unknown group quarters in 804.01.** 387 people (11%) live in "other noninstitutional" quarters, which could be a shelter or treatment residence. Identify it before publishing.
- **The life expectancy numbers do not agree and are noisy.** BPHC puts tract 803 at 68.8 for 2015-2021; CDC's USALEEP puts it at 76.9 for 2010-2015 (standard error about 2 years). With about 2,200 residents, a few deaths can move a tract figure by years. BPHC itself called the 2023 numbers provisional. Do not join BPHC and USALEEP numbers into one trend line.
- **The 33-year tract is not confirmed.** BPHC says only "Roxbury." Do not say tract 803's life expectancy rose from 58.9 to 68.8 unless BPHC or VCU confirms it is the same tract.
- **Some cuts are next door, not inside.** The 1967 unrest was in Grove Hall. The Washington Park renewal area lies mostly south and west. The Dudley Triangle (DSNI) is about a mile east, in tract 906. Use them as neighborhood context only.
- **Sources disagree on counts.** Washington Park displacement ranges from 1,500 families (1958 plan) to 2,200 (1961 plan) to about 2,600 (Segregation by Design); DSNI's founding is given as 1984 and 1985; Orchard Park's opening as 1941 and 1942.
- **There is renewal, too.** Orchard Gardens replaced a failing complex, the city put a 500-worker school headquarters on the square, and the cleared parcels are now being built on. Rising land values bring displacement pressure, but they also mean the "decline" story should end in a mixed present, not only loss.
