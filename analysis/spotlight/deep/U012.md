# U012 deep dive: Seventh Ward, St. Roch, Florida Area and Desire Area, New Orleans, LA

Prepared 2026-11-19. Facts below were checked against the URL given with each one. Anything not checked against a source is marked UNCONFIRMED. Numbers labeled "our overlay" or "our check" were computed for this note and still need a tie-out.

**What we cannot add but the story needs.** The Agriculture Street landfill and Gordon Plaza sit in 2020 tract 137.01 (our check of the landfill point and 3400 Louisa St), which is not a candidate tract. ## 2. Timeline

1. **1909.** The city starts dumping on swampy ground at Agriculture Street. It becomes a 95-acre city landfill. Wikipedia (dump start): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_Street_Landfill ; EPA (size): https://cumulis.epa.gov/supercpad/SiteProfiles/index.cfm?fuseaction=second.Cleanup&id=0600646
2. **1918 to 1923.** The Industrial Canal is dredged from June 6, 1918, and opens May 5, 1923, at a cost of $19 million, cutting the Upper Ninth Ward off from the Lower Ninth and the land that became New Orleans East. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Canal . The Wikipedia dates carry no inline citation; UNCONFIRMED against a primary source (Port of New Orleans or Army Corps records).
3. **Late 1930s.** HOLC grades the area. Our overlay of Mapping Inequality polygons on 2020 tracts: tracts 28 and 30 are 100% D ("hazardous"), 14.01 is 81% D, 22 is 41% D and 53% C, 23 is mostly D and C. The Desire tract (137.02) was not graded at all, only a small "Industrial" patch. https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/ . Map year (1939 is often cited) UNCONFIRMED.
4. **1946.** The Florida public housing development opens: 734 units in 47 buildings on 18.5 acres, bounded by Florida Ave and N Dorgenois, Mazant and Gallier streets. It was built for white residents and was mostly Black by the 1970s. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Projects (citing the Los Angeles Times, 1995).
5. **1952 to 1956.** The Desire development is built on a "cypress swamp and dumping ground" bordered by rail lines, the Industrial Canal and industrial plants. It opens May 21, 1956, with about 1,860 units in 262 buildings, at a cost of $23 million. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desire_Projects (citing Germany, "New Orleans After the Promises," 2007).
6. **September 1965 to 1966.** Hurricane Betsy puts Desire under about six feet of water. The Agriculture Street dump, closed in the 1950s, is reopened for Betsy debris and officially closed again in 1966. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desire_Projects ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_Street_Landfill
7. **1966 to 1968.** I-10 opens in stages through and next to the cluster: Franklin Ave to the Industrial Canal in April 1966 (this is the stretch over tracts 23, 30 and 137.02), St. Bernard Ave to Franklin Ave on February 27, 1968, and Orleans Ave to St. Bernard Ave, the oak-lined stretch of Claiborne Avenue, on March 14, 1968. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_10_in_Louisiana . The Claiborne Avenue page says nearly 500 homes were destroyed and businesses closed (citing The Guardian, 2022): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claiborne_Avenue . Counts of oaks removed and businesses lost are UNCONFIRMED.
8. **1970s to late 1980s.** About 47 acres of the old landfill are built on: the Gordon Plaza subdivision, Housing Authority of New Orleans (HANO) townhomes, an apartment complex, small businesses and Moton Elementary School. In 1990 about 1,137 people lived on the site. EPA: https://cumulis.epa.gov/supercpad/SiteProfiles/index.cfm?fuseaction=second.Cleanup&id=0600646
9. **1986 to 1994.** EPA begins investigating in 1986 after complaints about trash surfacing (Wikipedia, above). EPA proposes the Agriculture Street Landfill for the Superfund list on August 23, 1994 and lists it on December 16, 1994, with a Hazard Ranking score of 50.00. EPA (above).
10. **1994.** A HUD audit finds all 150 randomly sampled Desire units fail basic standards, with 21,000 work orders outstanding; the Florida development records 26 homicides that year. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desire_Projects ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Projects (citing the New York Times, December 25, 1995).
11. **1995 to 2004.** Desire wins a $44.2 million HOPE VI grant (approved February 1995) and is torn down. Wikipedia gives conflicting finish dates (razed by 1999 vs. "completely torn down by 2003"); exact dates UNCONFIRMED. Florida is partly demolished from 1998 to 2004. Same Wikipedia pages.
12. **1997 to 2002.** EPA's cleanup at the landfill removes 24 inches of soil from residential areas and replaces it, excavating 69,031 tons of soil and debris; 58 of 67 single-family homes take part. EPA declares no further action needed in April 2002. EPA (above).
13. **August 29, 2005.** Hurricane Katrina floods the area; a floodwall breach on the Upper Ninth side of the Industrial Canal is reported near Florida Ave (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Canal, no inline citation, UNCONFIRMED). The Florida development's census population falls from 1,604 (2000) to 6 (2010) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Projects, citing the Greater New Orleans Community Data Center). Our census numbers for the cluster: 24,425 (2000) to 12,952 (2010), down 47%.
14. **March 2018.** A Civil District Court judge orders the Orleans Parish School Board to pay a $12 million judgment to 1,433 former Moton Elementary students. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_Street_Landfill (citing the Louisiana Weekly: http://www.louisianaweekly.com/opsb-must-pay-12m-to-students-who-attended-school-atop-hazardous-waste-site/)
15. **2024 to 2025.** The city buys out and demolishes the last nine Gordon Plaza area properties whose owners had declined the 1990s cleanup, then finishes the soil removal there. EPA's August 12, 2025 five-year review finds the site protective of human health. EPA (above). The size of the city's relocation fund and the solar farm plan for the site are UNCONFIRMED (triage cited https://lailluminator.com/2024/10/31/superfund-solar-farm/, which blocked our fetch).

**Check against our census numbers (history_long.csv, rows with fit 0.8 or more, original eight tracts):** population fell from 30,005 (1990) to 24,425 (2000), down 19% before Katrina, then to 12,952 (2010). Poverty was 44.7% in 1990, fell to 33.4% in 2000 and rose to 48.1% in 2020-2024. Unemployment was 17.9% in 1990 and 12.7% in 2000. The 1990-2000 drop in poverty and rise in owner share (43.6% to 48.4%) line up in time with the Desire and Florida demolitions, which removed many of the poorest renter households; we do not have a source that tests this. The 1970 and 1980 rows have fit near 0.18 and are left out, so the census chart cannot show the 1960s and 1970s decline the triage note describes. Life expectancy (USALEEP 2010-2015): 71.3 (14.01), 71.7 (22), 70.8 (23), 68.3 (28), 68.4 (30), and 66.0 for 2010 tract 137 (which holds both 137.01 and 137.02).

## 3. How the cuts compound

The land at the back of the Upper Ninth Ward was swamp, dump and industrial edge, walled off by the Industrial Canal in 1923 and mostly graded "hazardous" by HOLC in the late 1930s. The city then placed two of its largest public housing developments there, Desire on a former swamp and dumping ground, and later built homes and a school on top of the old city dump. I-10 opened over the area and along Claiborne Avenue in 1966-1968; Wikipedia's Claiborne page says the overpass is commonly linked to the decline of the area, but we have not checked a scholarly source for that link. Population was already falling before 2005 (down 19% from 1990 to 2000), and Katrina then cut it nearly in half. The timing lines up for each cut, but the sources here do not measure how much each one added.

## 5. What makes it distinct

Here the neighborhood was boxed in by public works one layer at a time: a shipping canal, a city dump turned into a housing site, two very large public housing developments, and an elevated interstate, all before Katrina. Katrina is the last cut in this story, and our census numbers show the population already falling in the 1990s.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Several key sites are next door, not inside.** The landfill and Gordon Plaza are in tract 137.01, the Claiborne business strip and the Circle Food Store are in tract 34 and nearby tracts, and part of the Florida development may be in tract 16. None are candidate tracts. Either the editor allows adding 137.01 (and maybe 16), or the story must say "next door."
- **Wikipedia does a lot of the work here.** Many dates (Industrial Canal, Desire, Florida, Betsy, the Katrina breach) come from Wikipedia, some with no inline citation. Before publishing, check them against Germany (2007), Arend (2009), HANO and HUD records, the Army Corps, and Times-Picayune archives.
- **Health study.** Wikipedia reports that a 1997 ATSDR review found higher breast cancer rates in the area were not related to the landfill, and EPA's 2025 review calls the site protective. The story should not say the landfill caused the area's poor health.
- **The 1990-2000 poverty drop is likely a population effect.** Tearing down Desire and part of Florida moved out many low-income renters, so falling poverty in that decade does not mean residents got better off.
- **Our history numbers have problems.** (a) The "standardized to 2010 tracts" rows use whole 2010 tract 137, which includes 137.01 (the landfill and Gordon Plaza), so they cover more than our kept tracts. That is why the 2020 standardized population (16,205) is 25% above the 2020-2024 ACS count for the eight 2020 tracts (12,970). (b) The 2010 to 2020 population rise (12,952 to 16,205) may partly reflect this geography mix and rebuilt housing; check before charting. (c) Owner share drops from 48.4% (2010) to 38.0% (2020), and vacancy swings from 33.7% (2010) to 14.5% (2020) to 21.5% (2020-2024 ACS); the census and ACS measure vacancy differently, so do not mix them on one line. (d) 1970 and 1980 rows (fit about 0.18) are unusable, which leaves the pre-1990 story without tract numbers. (e) All rows still include the dropped tracts 17.20 and 17.51. Rebuild on the six kept tracts.
- **Boundaries after Katrina.** 2020 tracts 17.20 and 17.51 match 2010 tracts exactly, and 137.02 is the western 3.1 of the 5.0 square km in 2010 tract 137 (Census 2020-2010 relationship file). For 2000 and earlier, tract 137 and the 17.xx series were drawn differently; use the NHGIS crosswalks and expect noise.
- **I-10 inside the cluster is mostly the Franklin Ave to Industrial Canal stretch (opened April 1966), not the oak-lined Claiborne stretch.** Only tract 28 touches N Claiborne Ave.
