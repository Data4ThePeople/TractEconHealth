# U047: West Dayton, Ohio (deep dive)

Status: the editor has confirmed West Dayton for the series. This file checks the triage notes (research/U047.md), fixes what was wrong, and sets the tract list.

Method notes for this pass:
- HOLC shares were recomputed here by overlaying the Mapping Inequality national GeoJSON (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/static/mappinginequality.json) on the 2020 Census cartographic tracts (data/raw/tiger/cb_2020_us_tract_500k.shp), area-weighted in an equal-area projection.
- City and CDP shares come from overlaying Census TIGERweb place polygons on the tracts.
- 2020 population, group quarters, and housing units come from the 2020 PL 94-171 file via the Census API.
- Neighborhood names come from City of Dayton plans (linked below) and Census geocoder lookups of street addresses. The Dayton Daily News (DDN) website blocked automated reads (HTTP 429) during this pass, so facts that rest only on DDN articles are marked as secondhand or UNCONFIRMED. Web search was also unavailable for part of this pass.

**Westwood check.** The 2025 Westwood Neighborhood Plan gives Westwood's 2020 population as 4,433 (https://www.daytonohio.gov/DocumentCenter/View/22894/Westwood-Neighborhood-Plan). Tracts 38 and 39 add up to exactly 4,433 in the 2020 census (2,481 + 1,952). So these two tracts are Westwood.

**Recommended tract list (6):** 39113165100, 39113003800, 39113003900, 39113004100, 39113000200, 39113070300. The 2020 population is 14,032.

**Drops:**
- **39113060200 (Drexel).** It is outside Dayton and outside the area HOLC graded. It is the least like the rest of the cluster. In ACS 2020-2024 its owner share is 55%, its vacancy rate is 6%, and its obesity rate (41%) is about 10 points below the core tracts. The West Dayton history (HOLC, 1966, US-35) does not apply to it.
- **39113070201 (Trotwood).** 99% of it is in the city of Trotwood. Only a 13% sliver falls inside a HOLC polygon. Trotwood was mostly a township until it merged with Madison Township in 1996 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotwood,_Ohio), so its history differs from the West Dayton story. Its health numbers do match the core, so this is a judgment call about place, not data.

**Institution check.**
- None of the six kept tracts has a prison, jail, college dorm or base. Group quarters total 17 people across the six kept tracts in 2020 (PL table P5).
- **Tract 41 public housing flag.** DeSoto Bass Courts is a Greater Dayton Premier Management public housing site. It was built in 1945 and had 350 units in 2018, down from more than 524 (Renew Miami Chapel plan, https://www.daytonohio.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5305/Miami-Chapel-Choice-Transformation-Plan, pp. 19 to 21). The plan places its redevelopment at Germantown St. and James H. McGee Blvd. The biggest 2020 census block in tract 41 sits on Germantown St. near that corner, with 183 housing units and 251 people. A nearby address (1300 Danner Ave.) geocodes to tract 35. So the complex is at or on the eastern edge of tract 41, and it may be split between tracts 41 and 35 (UNCONFIRMED). Even if all 350 units were in tract 41, that would be 28% of its 1,242 housing units, not most of them. Keep the tract, but run the cluster numbers with and without tract 41 as a check. The plan also calls for replacing these units over five phases. If that happens, tract 41's numbers will shift for reasons that have nothing to do with the people who live there now.
- **Tract 703.** One 2020 block holds 350 housing units and 383 people. That is likely one large apartment complex (name UNCONFIRMED). It is 14% of the tract's people, so it does not drive the numbers.
- The former Good Samaritan Hospital site (2222 Philadelphia Dr.) geocodes to tract 5, which is not in the cluster. The Delphi Home Avenue plant (2701 Home Ave.) geocodes to tract 42, which borders tract 41 but is not in the cluster.

**Crossing lines.** Tract 703 is split almost evenly between Dayton and Trotwood. Tract 2 sits at the city's northwest edge, near Wolf Creek. The City's West Dayton plan defines West Dayton as west of the Great Miami River and south of Wolf Creek. The Northwest Dayton plan starts north of Wolf Creek. Tract 2 may sit on that seam (UNCONFIRMED). The story should say "West Dayton and its edge with Trotwood."

**Our history numbers need a rerun.** history_long.csv was built on all eight tracts. Dropping Drexel and the Trotwood tract will change the 1970 baseline the most. The 1970 Black share of 65.7% looks low next to 80% in 1980. The likely reason is that the suburban tracts were mostly white in 1970, but that is UNCONFIRMED at the tract level.

## 3. How the cuts compound

The City's own plans make the first link. Redlining and segregation packed Black families into West Dayton by the 1950s, and the area held little but older homes and public housing such as DeSoto Bass. A 1966 DDN article listed the lack of home loans as one grievance before the unrest. Afterward many west side businesses moved or never reopened, according to the DDN series as summarized on Wikipedia. The City says the US-35 freeway, finished in 1996, cut West Dayton in half and removed hundreds of homes along one stretch. It also says the loss of thousands of factory jobs left West Dayton with more poverty and joblessness than the rest of the city. For the later cuts (Delphi and GM closings in 2008, NCR leaving in 2009, the overdose rise, and the hospital closing in 2018), no source we found ties one to the next, so we can only say the timing lines up with the rise in vacancy (about 10% in 1990 to about 25% in 2008-2012) in our census numbers.

## 5. What makes it distinct

West Dayton is a small factory city's Black west side. Its losses are separate, dated events a reader can follow in order, from a 1930s loan map to a 1966 shooting on a street that runs through the cluster, a 1990s freeway, the auto and NCR exits, and the 2018 loss of the nearby hospital. Unlike the big-city stories, almost every layer can be tied to a named street or site in or next to these six tracts.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Several big cuts happened outside the tracts.** GM Moraine, NCR's headquarters, and Good Samaritan Hospital (tract 5, north of Wolf Creek) were not in the cluster. Their effect on these tracts is through jobs and access, which we can show only at the county level. Only the Delphi Home Avenue plant borders the cluster.
- **Reinvestment in tract 1651.** The Dayton Aviation Heritage National Historical Park, set up on Oct. 16, 1992, includes the Wright Cycle Company site in Wright-Dunbar, and a new visitor center opened in 2003 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Aviation_Heritage_National_Historical_Park). Wright-Dunbar has had decades of restoration. The DDN series reported some new west side investment since 2016 (secondhand via Wikipedia). The planned DeSoto Bass rebuild and the Gem City Market grocery co-op (324 Salem Ave., tract 10, outside the cluster; opening date UNCONFIRMED) are also new.
- **Health has a mixed record.** Our USALEEP life expectancy (69.5) is a single 2010-2015 point. We have no earlier tract health data to show decline. County overdose rates rose sharply, but they are county-wide, not West Dayton-only.
- **Disputed numbers.** The Moraine closing headcount differs (2,400 on Wikipedia vs. about 1,100 in the triage's DDN source). Arrest counts for 1966 range from about 100 to over 500. The claim that Montgomery County led the nation in overdose deaths in 2017 was not confirmed by the DDN itself, per the triage; the CDC model rate kept rising through 2021, so "2017 was the peak" is not safe either.
- **Good Samaritan.** The civil rights complaint said Good Sam was the only hospital serving the west side. HHS found no violation, and Premier said the hospital was "financially unsustainable." Present both.
- **Census quirks.** Tract 38 shows 0.0% unemployment in ACS 2020-2024 next to 32% poverty, which suggests a small-sample problem. The cluster's unemployment swings from 28.4% (ACS 2008-2012) to 10.7% (ACS 2020-2024), and those are noisy survey estimates. Decennial 2020 vacancy (19.5%) and ACS 2020-2024 vacancy (26.6%) differ by method. The 1970 values carry a fit of 0.847 and include the dropped suburban tracts.
- **DeSoto Bass in tract 41.** Its share of tract 41 is UNCONFIRMED (see Boundary), and its planned rebuild could move tract numbers.
