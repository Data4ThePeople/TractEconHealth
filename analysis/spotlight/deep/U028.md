## 1. Boundary

**Local names.** El Segundo Barrio (the "Second Ward," South El Paso), with Chihuahuita at its western edge, and the Chamizal neighborhood to the east. The National Register nomination puts the historic Segundo Barrio between Paisano Drive on the north and South Santa Fe Street on the west ([NR nomination, Segundo Barrio Historic District, 2021](https://atlas.thc.texas.gov/NR/pdfs/100006994/100006994.pdf)). The city defines the Chamizal neighborhood as bounded by Alameda Avenue (north), Gateway South Boulevard (east), Paisano Drive and the Cesar Chavez Border Highway (south), and Laurel Street and the rail line (west). The city's 2008 plan says it falls in 2000 census tracts 21, 28 and 29 ([City of El Paso, Chamizal Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy, January 2008](https://www.elpasotexas.gov/assets/Documents/CoEP/Community-Development/Neighborhood-Associations/Neighborhood-Revitalization/CHAMIZAL-NRS.pdf)).

**Where tracts sit (checked with the Census geocoder, https://geocoding.geo.census.gov):**
- 48141001800: Sacred Heart Church (602 S. Oregon) and Chihuahuita (Charles Road). West Segundo Barrio.
- 48141001900: the "Six Hells" tenement block (600 S. Ochoa), Lydia Patterson Institute, and Father Pinto Apartments (1001 S. Ochoa). Core Segundo Barrio.
- 48141002000: East Segundo Barrio (1100 E. Seventh Ave., 300 S. Cotton).
- 48141002800: Bowie High School and the Chamizal National Memorial area (800 S. San Marcial). Chamizal neighborhood.
- 48141002900: Alameda Avenue at about 3700. Chamizal neighborhood.
- 48141003000: Alameda Avenue at about 4100 to 4800, east of Gateway South. Next to the city's Chamizal boundary but outside it. Local name UNCONFIRMED.

**Recommended tracts (6), 2020 population 15,195 (2020 PL 94-171, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl):**
48141001800, 48141001900, 48141002000, 48141002800, 48141002900, 48141003000

**Dropped (2):**
- **48141002100.** 352 of its 2,084 residents (17%) live in "other noninstitutional" group quarters (2020 PL 94-171). Two shelters geocode into this tract: the Opportunity Center for the Homeless (1208 Myrtle Ave.) and Annunciation House, a migrant shelter (1003 E. San Antonio Ave.). Which of them the Census counted is UNCONFIRMED. A 17% shelter population would drive this tract's poverty, health and life expectancy numbers. The tract's western half is also the Magoffin area north of Paisano, not Segundo Barrio. Trade-off: the city counts part of this tract as the Chamizal neighborhood, so the story loses a slice of Chamizal.
- **48141003602.** The El Paso County Juvenile Detention Center (6400 Delta Drive, [El Paso County Juvenile Probation](https://epcounty.com/jvprobation)) geocodes into this tract. The 2020 count shows 47 people in juvenile facilities and 107 in "other institutional" quarters. The tract is also about 2 miles east of the Chamizal neighborhood, near Ascarate Park, and is not part of either named neighborhood.

**Note:** all `history_long.csv` numbers are for the original 8 tracts. They need to be rebuilt for the 6-tract outline before use.

- The Levi Strauss source says the closing was *announced* by early 2002. It does not say the plant closed in 2002.

## 3. How the cuts compound

The neighborhood started crowded and poor: it was the first stop for families coming from Mexico, and landlords built tenements to house them. Then public projects kept taking land. The 1916 cleanup, the 1946-1947 Paisano widening, and the Chamizal river channel each removed housing. The National Register nomination says that from 1960 to the early 1970s the barrio lost about one-third of its land and more than half of its housing. It also says renters pushed out by the Chamizal clearance crowded into the blocks that were left, with almost no new housing built for them. The garment plants that hired many residents then shrank: El Paso apparel jobs fell 92% from 1990 to 2019. The Dallas Fed says NAFTA "likely accelerated" the move of apparel work to lower-cost countries. In our census numbers, the timing lines up: unemployment in these tracts rose from 19.7% in 1990 to 22.9% in 2000, and the population kept falling.

## 5. What makes it distinct

This was a main front door for families coming from Mexico, and here an international treaty, not just a highway, took homes: the river itself was moved and more than 5,600 people had to leave. It then lost much of the garment work its residents held, in a city whose apparel jobs fell 92% from 1990 to 2019.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Poverty was already high before most of the timed cuts in our data.** The 8-tract poverty rate was 45.1% in 1970. It peaked at 63.4% in 1990, before NAFTA, and fell to 55.6% in 2000 while apparel jobs were collapsing. So the NAFTA period does not show up as rising poverty. Unemployment did rise from 1990 to 2000 (19.7% to 22.9%). Our data cannot show the 1916-1967 cuts at all; the series starts in 1970.
- **Life expectancy is not low.** USALEEP gives 76.3 years for the 8-tract cluster (fit 1.0), compared with 69.9 for the Flint finalist (U013). Hispanic residents often show longer life spans than income would predict. The "poor health" in this cluster comes from chronic disease rates in CDC PLACES (for example, diabetes 30.6%), which are modeled estimates, not counts. The story should not claim early death.
- **The jobs link to the neighborhood is indirect.** We have county apparel jobs, not where garment workers lived. The NR nomination says many garment workers crossed daily from Juárez by the 1960s, so some lost jobs were held by people who did not live in these tracts.
- **Two sources for El Paso apparel jobs do not match.** The Comptroller says 1,130 jobs in 2019; QCEW (private, county) shows 1,310. The difference may be the area (metro vs. county) or the data release. Check before charting.
- **History numbers need rebuilding.** `history_long.csv` uses all 8 tracts, including the two we drop. The 1990 population (32,451.11) is a crosswalk estimate, not a count. Vacancy jumps around: 6.5% in the 2010 census but 11.5% in ACS 2008-2012. The census and ACS measure vacancy differently, so do not put them on one line without a note.
- **The population drop is not all decline.** Some of the loss since 1970 reflects smaller households and the city's own housing removal. It is not proof that people left because of the economy.
- **Tract 3000's name is unconfirmed.** It lies just east of the city's Chamizal boundary.
- **Shelter identities in tract 2100 are unconfirmed.** The 352-person group quarters count is confirmed; which shelter it is, is not.
