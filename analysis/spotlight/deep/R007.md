## 3. How the cuts compound

McDowell's economy rested on one product, coal, and for much of the county one buyer, U.S. Steel. The e-WV encyclopedia says mine mechanization after 1950 caused widespread job losses, and the population fell by about half between 1950 and 1970. When U.S. Steel left Gary in the 1980s, the World Socialist Web Site reported that county personal income "plunged by two-thirds" that year. That is one outlet's claim and has not been checked against BEA data. The e-WV entry on Welch links the town's population loss to out-migration, lost mining jobs and floods. The timing of the 2001 and 2002 floods lines up with a further drop in population and housing. WV Public Broadcasting reported that the 2016 Walmart closing took away the main grocery store and a key donor to the local food pantry. The heavy pill shipments of 2006-2012 and the county's very short life spans happened after decades of job loss. The timing lines up, but no source we read connects them directly.

## 5. What makes it distinct

McDowell was once the top coal county in the country. Then it lost more than four of every five residents over 70 years, as mechanization, a steel company's exit, repeated floods, the pill wave and the loss of its only big store came one after another. Unusually, almost every step can be shown with a public county series running back a century.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Dropping the prison tract also drops downtown Welch and Kimball.** The tract story then covers outlying camps rather than the county seat. The Walmart was in Kimball, so tie that event to county data, not the tract numbers.
- **The U.S. Steel exit has conflicting dates (1982, 1984, 1986) and job counts (550 vs. more than 1,200).** None of the sources is primary. U.S. Steel annual reports, MSHA records or the 2013 book reviewed in the Gazette would settle it.
- **The 1950 and 1960 miner counts come from a magazine with no named source.** Mechanization layoff counts for 1957-1960 are UNCONFIRMED. The state production drop in 1957-58 is confirmed, but production is not jobs.
- **The state production file covers only underground mines through 2008.** The 2009-2021 sheet appears to include surface mines too (the jump from 2.97M tons in 2008 to 4.62M in 2009 suggests this). Do not join the two sheets without checking.
- **Coal jobs have not kept falling since 2000.** MSHA shows about 1,000 miners in both 2000 and 2024. The coal loss mostly happened before 2000.
- **Health.** West Virginia figures in our screen come from the 2025 CDC PLACES release. The 2025 release left Kentucky out, so the Kentucky comparison county (R015) uses the 2024 release. Comparisons across the state line mix two releases. The often-quoted figure of 141 drug deaths per 100,000 in 2015 is cited on Wikipedia to a CDC MMWR that does not contain it: **UNCONFIRMED**. Pull it from CDC WONDER.
- **Tract life spans are noisy.** Tract 9542's USALEEP value of 82.1 has a standard error of 3.7 years.
- **ARCOS rank is our own calculation:** dosage units per resident from WaPo's county file, averaged over 2006-2012. WaPo did not publish that rank.
- **Overlap.** R003 (Mingo) and R019 (Martin) are the same kind of story. Pick only one central Appalachian coal place.
