## 3. How the cuts compound

Owsley never had a big employer. Families lived on small farms, a tobacco allotment and wages earned in other counties, mostly coal. The population fell by more than two fifths between 1940 and 1970 as people left for work. The federal quota system gave small burley farms a steady cash crop from 1938 on. The 2004 law ended it and paid holders out over ten years. The county ag council says the loss of tobacco income accounts for most of the county's lost farm income. By 2017, the county's farms together lost money. A resident told Al Jazeera that Walmarts in nearby counties closed local stores. The rise in pill shipments, the cancer death rate and reliance on public benefits came during the same decades. The timing lines up, but no source we read ties each one to tobacco.

## 5. What makes it distinct

Owsley is a farm-and-tobacco place, not a coal camp. Its people lived on small burley plots and on work in other counties, and federal tobacco policy shaped the cash economy from 1938 to 2004. It shows decline coming from out-migration, the end of a protected crop, distant retail and public benefits replacing farm income.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **The "suffering started in the 80s" frame only partly fits.** Owsley's population peaked in 1940 and lost 44% by 1970, long before the farm crisis. It was already among the poorest U.S. counties (55.4% poverty in 1989). The 1980s show a fresh drop (-12%), but no Kentucky-specific source confirms a farm crisis there. The tobacco story turns on 2004, not the 1980s.
- **The kept tract is not the sickest part of the county.** Tract 9301's life expectancy (77.3) is close to the U.S. average for 2010-2015. The tract with the short life span (9302, 66.5) is outside the candidate.
- **Kentucky health data.** Kentucky's health figures in our screen come from the 2024 CDC PLACES release (2022 survey year), because the 2025 release left Kentucky out. They cannot be compared directly with 2025-release numbers for other states.
- **Coal is part of this story too.** Men commuted to mines in Breathitt and other counties. Coal job losses next door likely hit Owsley household income, but we have no county commuting series to show it.
- **Tobacco numbers are the weakest link.** The acreage and farm series that would carry the chart were not pulled. That pull has to happen before approval.
- **Small numbers.** With about 4,000 people, county death rates swing a lot from year to year. Use multi-year averages.
- **ARCOS rank is our own calculation**, from WaPo's county file, 2006-2012. WaPo did not publish that rank. Shipments are counted by pharmacy county, so pills bought by people from nearby counties land in Owsley's total.
