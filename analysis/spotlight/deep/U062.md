## 1. Boundary

**Local names.** Watts, a neighborhood of the City of Los Angeles since 1926. The *Los Angeles Times* Mapping L.A. boundary runs along Alameda Street (east), Imperial Highway (south), Central Avenue up to 103rd Street and then Success Avenue up to 92nd Street (west), with Florence-Firestone to the north. Inside it are the Watts Towers and 103rd Street area, the Grape Street and Jordan Downs area, Nickerson Gardens, Imperial Courts and the blocks of small houses between them.

**Method.** I overlaid the *Los Angeles Times* Watts polygon (LA GeoHub, https://services5.arcgis.com/7nsPwEMP38bSkCjy/arcgis/rest/services/LA_Times_Neighborhoods/FeatureServer) on 2020 tract shapes from TIGERweb (https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). Ten tracts make up 100% of the polygon. Nine lie 99% to 100% inside it; tract 2421 lies 60% inside. Landmarks were placed with the Census geocoder (https://geocoding.geo.census.gov): 9800 Grape St (Jordan Downs) in 2421, 1590 E 114th St (Nickerson Gardens) in 2426, 11541 Croesus Ave (Imperial Courts) in 2431, 1761 E 107th St (Watts Towers) in 2427. Unit counts are from HUD's Public Housing Developments layer (https://services.arcgis.com/VTyQ9soqVukalItT/arcgis/rest/services/Public_Housing_Developments/FeatureServer) and Wikipedia. Population, group quarters and homes are from the 2020 Census DHC (https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/dhc).

## 2. Timeline

1. **April 2, 1926 (effective June 1).** Watts voters chose to join Los Angeles, 1,338 to 535, adding about 23,000 people to the city. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts,_Los_Angeles (citing *Los Angeles Times*, April 3, 1926)
2. **1920s to 1940s.** Racial covenants kept Black residents out of most of Los Angeles except the Central Avenue district and Watts, and Watts became mostly Black during the 1940s. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts,_Los_Angeles (citing Richard Rothstein, *Los Angeles Times*, August 20, 2017). A covenant dataset for Watts deeds: UNCONFIRMED (none found).
3. **1939.** The Home Owners' Loan Corporation map graded every graded part of the 10 Watts tracts "D" (red), all in area D61, with a sliver of D60. The graded share of each tract runs from 22% to 89%; the rest was ungraded. Computed for this note from the Mapping Inequality polygons, https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining . The words of the D61 area description: UNCONFIRMED (the site would not load in this session). This settles the triage's open question on the Watts grade.
4. **May 1944.** Jordan Downs (512 homes in 111 buildings on 34 acres when built) and Imperial Courts (498 units) opened as housing for war workers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Downs (citing *Los Angeles Times*, May 28, 1944) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Courts
5. **1954 and 1955.** Nickerson Gardens (1,066 units) opened in March 1954, and Jordan Downs reopened as public housing in 1955. By the mid-1960s the projects were close to all Black. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickerson_Gardens ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Downs
6. **August 11 to 17, 1965.** The Watts uprising left 34 dead, 1,032 injured, nearly 4,000 arrested and more than $40 million in property damage. The McCone Commission named unemployment, poor housing and poor schools as long-standing grievances. https://csudh.edu/watts/history . (Correction: triage gave 3,438 arrests; CSUDH says "nearly 4,000." Pick one source and cite it.)
7. **1966 to 1972.** The hardest-hit blocks, 103rd Street between Compton and Wilmington Avenues, were cleared under a 1966 urban renewal plan by 1972. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts,_Los_Angeles (citing *Christian Science Monitor*, 1990)
8. **1970 to 1980.** In the four Watts tracts that keep the same names over time (2422, 2423, 2427, 2431), the Black share went from 91.4% to 84.1%. Computed for this note from the project's NHGIS nominal tract file (`data/raw/nhgis/extract/nhgis0010_csv`), https://www.nhgis.org . Boundary match for 1970 and 1980: UNCONFIRMED; 1990 to 2010 the four are whole-tract matches.
9. **1972 to 1993.** The Century Freeway (I-105), just south of Watts, was held up by the *Keith v. Volpe* lawsuit (filed 1972), with a consent decree in 1979 (amended 1981). It opened in 1993. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Anderson_Freeway ; https://clearinghouse.net/case/43962 . The triage figure of 4,200 replacement homes: UNCONFIRMED.
10. **1975 to 1986.** Eight major plants in South Los Angeles closed, putting 12,000 people out of work (SurveyLA citywide historic context, as excerpted at https://scalar.sparcinla.org/great-wall-institute/labor-and-union-organizing). GM's South Gate assembly plant, about 2 miles east, ended production on March 23, 1982 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Gate_Assembly). Bethlehem Steel closed its Maywood mill in 1982 (https://memorywork.irle.ucla.edu/archives/4956). Job counts for South Gate, and closing years for the Goodyear and Firestone plants: UNCONFIRMED.
11. **1980 to 1990.** In the same four tracts, the Black share fell from 84.1% to 57.1%, and to 37.1% by 2000. NHGIS, as in entry 8. The shift to a Latino majority was well under way before 1992.
12. **April 26, 1992.** Four Watts gangs signed the "Watts truce" days before the 1992 unrest. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts,_Los_Angeles (citing Jeff Chang, *Can't Stop Won't Stop*)
13. **2000.** Watts was 61.6% Latino and 37.1% Black, with median household income of $25,161 (2008 dollars) and 67% of homes rented. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts,_Los_Angeles (citing *Los Angeles Times* Mapping L.A.)
14. **August 2007 to July 2015.** King-Harbor hospital in nearby Willowbrook closed in 2007 after failing a federal review, leaving South Los Angeles without a major public hospital until the new Martin Luther King Jr. Community Hospital opened on July 7, 2015. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr._Community_Hospital
15. **2010 to 2015 deaths, and 2020.** Life expectancy in the Watts tracts ranged from 74.1 to 77.9 years, against an average of 80.6 across Los Angeles County tracts (USALEEP, https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nvss/usaleep/usaleep.html ; project copy `data/raw/usaleep/US_A.CSV`). In 2020 the 8 kept tracts had 34,955 people: 75.8% Hispanic and 21.2% non-Hispanic Black (2020 Census DHC). Jordan Downs redevelopment (master plan said to be approved in 2013, phases opening since about 2019): UNCONFIRMED (both source sites failed to load).

## 3. How the cuts compound

Covenants elsewhere in Los Angeles left Watts as one of the few places Black families could live, as Rothstein describes, and the 1939 map graded all of its graded land red. Wartime and 1950s public housing added more than 2,000 units in a neighborhood of about two square miles. The McCone Commission tied the 1965 uprising to joblessness, housing and schools, and the hardest-hit blocks were cleared and not rebuilt for years. The SurveyLA history ties the 1975 to 1986 plant closures to 12,000 lost jobs in South Los Angeles; for Watts itself the timing lines up, but no source here measures the job loss for Watts residents. The freeway fight, the 2007 hospital closure and the change from a mostly Black to a mostly Latino neighborhood came on top of that; the timing lines up with low life expectancy today, but no cited source links them directly.

## 5. What makes it distinct

Watts holds nearly every cause on our list in one small area, each with a date: covenants and a red HOLC grade, three wartime and 1950s housing projects, the 1965 uprising, nearby plant closures, a freeway fight and a hospital closure. It is also a place where the people changed, from more than 90% Black in 1970 to about three-quarters Latino in 2020, so the "decline" story has to be told across two communities.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Demographic turnover.** The Black share in the stable four tracts went from 91% (1970) to 23% (2020). A chart of income or health over time compares different populations. Show race next to every trend line, and avoid saying "the same neighborhood got poorer" without that context.
- **The Latino shift started before 1992.** Wikipedia's claim that it "accelerated after the 1992 riots" is marked "citation needed." The NHGIS numbers show the biggest drop in Black share between 1980 and 1990.
- **Rebuilt Watts is less extreme than the candidate.** Dropping Jordan Downs and Nickerson Gardens moves the area from about the 2nd to about the 25th income percentile. Tell the editor this is a true neighborhood picture, not a screen-bottom one.
- **Public housing is part of the Watts story.** Dropping the two project tracts follows the rule, but about one in five Watts homes is public housing. Show the project tracts as a separate line rather than leaving them out of the story.
- **History numbers for U062 should not be used.** The 1970-1990 rows have fit 0.597 and 0.588, under the 0.8 cutoff. Other rows look off: the owner share for 2020-2024 is 3.05% while the 2020 census shows 1 owned home out of 684; the 2008-2012 owner share and vacancy are both exactly 0.0; and the 2020-2024 Black share (16.5%) is half the 2020 census (33.6%), likely because of redevelopment moves and small ACS samples. Rerun the history builder on the 8 kept tracts.
- **USALEEP smoothing.** Tracts 2420, 2421 and 2426 all show exactly 75.3, which suggests pooled or modeled estimates. Treat tract differences of a year or two as noise.
- **The hospital and freeway are just outside Watts.** MLK hospital is in Willowbrook and I-105 runs along Watts' southern edge. Say "nearby," not "in Watts."
- **Compton (U067) for comparison.** Compton's triage story (a white suburb that became a Black middle-class suburb, then mostly Latino) is real, but its tract is a weak screen fit (17th income, 15th health percentile) with no stable 1990 match. Watts is the stronger LA pick.
