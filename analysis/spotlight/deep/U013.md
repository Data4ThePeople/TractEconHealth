## 1. Boundary

**Local names.** The cluster rings the former Buick City complex on Flint's north side and runs east along the I-475 corridor toward Davison Road. Neighborhood names used locally for this area include the north side around North Saginaw Street and the east side off Davison Road; exact names by tract are UNCONFIRMED. The St. John neighborhood was on the near north side, "wedged between Buick factories, the rail line and the Flint River" ([Michigan Civil Rights Commission, *The Flint Water Crisis: Systemic Racism Through the Lens of Flint*, 2017](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mdcr/mcrc/reports/2017/flint-crisis-report-edited.pdf)).

**Where tracts sit (Census geocoder, https://geocoding.geo.census.gov, and 2020 TIGER boundaries):**
- 26049013600: North Saginaw Street from about 1000 to 2200 N., including 902 E. Hamilton Ave., the old Buick headquarters address. The south and west edge of the Buick City complex.
- 26049002000: North of 136, around 4100 N. Saginaw.
- 26049002200: East of 136. Contains the I-475 corridor at Davison Road (1300 Davison) and E. Hamilton Ave. near Broadway.
- 26049002300 and 26049002400: East side, between Davison Road and the north city line.
- 26049001800 and 26049001900: Northeast side, north of 22 and 23.
- 26049000800: North side, just west of North Saginaw, near tract 20.
- Not in the cluster: tract 26049980100 (zero population; the Buick City industrial land, including 1100 E. Stewart Ave.) and tract 26049002600 (between the river and Hamilton Ave., 2,249 people in 2020). Tract 26 likely held much of the St. John core between the river and the Buick plants (UNCONFIRMED). It belongs to a different screened neighborhood (cluster 260), so we cannot add it.

**Recommended tracts (all 8), 2020 population 11,704 (2020 PL 94-171, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl):**
26049000800, 26049001800, 26049001900, 26049002000, 26049002200, 26049002300, 26049002400, 26049013600

**Dropped:** none. Tract 136 has 108 people in group quarters (73 in college housing, 35 other noninstitutional) out of 1,357, or 8%. No other tract has more than 32. No shelter, prison or single institution drives the numbers.

**Recommendation on framing: tell it as a north-central and east Flint story, with St. John as the first chapter, not as St. John's story alone.** Reasons:
1. The St. John core was cleared in 1965-1977, and most of its land is now highway, industrial land (tract 980100, no residents) or tract 26, which is not in this cluster.
2. Our census series for this cluster starts in 1990 (1970 and 1980 have fit 0.795, under our 0.8 cutoff), well after the clearance. The data can show what happened to the neighborhoods around the cleared land, not to St. John itself.
3. The cluster today is 47% Black (2020), a mixed area. St. John was the center of Black life in Flint. Calling this cluster "St. John" would mislabel the people who live there now.

**Corrections to the triage notes:**
- The triage dated the St. John clearance to "early 1970s." Per the MCRC report (citing Highsmith), buying and relocation ran from 1965 to 1977, after an announcement in 1958.
- The triage said I-475 was built in the early 1970s. The central section did not open until 1981.
- The triage gave Buick City's mid-1980s peak (28,000) and closing workforce (1,200) from WSWS only. We could not confirm them in a second source.
- The state took over Flint's finances first in May 2002 (not just "2002").
- The triage listed HOLC grades as unconfirmed. They are now overlaid (see 1937 entry).

## 3. How the cuts compound

Federal grading in 1937 marked every Flint district where Black families lived as "hazardous," and covenants kept them out of most new housing. The Michigan Civil Rights Commission report, drawing on historian Andrew Highsmith, says the city then picked the St. John area for highway and renewal clearance partly because land there was cheap and poor. It says a 1965 ban on repairs drove home values down before buyouts, and that displaced Black families had few places to go. Highsmith writes that the I-475 and St. John projects "ultimately increased Black poverty and residential segregation." GM jobs in Flint fell from about 80,000 in 1978 to under 8,000 by 2010, and Buick City, next to this cluster, closed in 1999. The timing lines up with our census numbers: the cluster lost half its people from 1990 to 2020, and poverty rose from 39% to about 50%. Last, the state's emergency managers, who ran the city because of its money troubles, made the 2014 water switch, and a 2016 study tied the switch to a rise in children's blood lead levels.

## 5. What makes it distinct

These neighborhoods sit around land that was cleared of homes to serve the auto industry and then emptied when the plant closed in 1999. The water crisis was the last of many cuts here, and it came while state-appointed managers, not elected officials, ran the city's finances.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **The data starts after the biggest early cuts.** 1970 and 1980 fall under our 0.8 fit cutoff, so our numbers begin in 1990, after redlining, the St. John clearance and most of the GM job loss. The early cuts must be told from documents, not our tract numbers.
- **This cluster is not mostly St. John.** See the framing note in section 1. The core cleared area is mostly outside the 8 tracts.
- **Homeownership did not fall in our data.** It held near 48% to 50% from 1990 to 2024. Highsmith's 50%-to-15% drop is for Black residents of St. John during the clearance era, not for this cluster.
- **Vacancy fell after 2010** (24.2% to 16.7% in the 2020 census), mostly because vacant houses were demolished, not because the area recovered. That is our reading; we did not confirm it with Land Bank counts by tract. ACS 2020-2024 shows 20.2%, and census and ACS vacancy are measured differently.
- **Poverty dipped from 1990 to 2000** (39.2% to 34.6%) before rising to 47% (ACS 2008-2012) and 49.5% (2020-2024). The 1990s auto recovery may explain the dip; that is not confirmed.
- **Black share rose** from 32% (1990) to 47% (2020). This likely reflects white residents leaving faster, but we have not tested that.
- **Water crisis effects were citywide, not targeted at this cluster.** The highest water lead was in Wards 5, 6 and 7; we have not matched wards to these tracts.
- **Key job numbers rest on secondary sources.** GM's 80,000 (1978) and under 8,000 (2010) come from Wikipedia. Buick City's peak and closing workforce come only from WSWS. These need a primary source, such as GM statements in newspapers of the time or Highsmith's book, before publication.
- **Our timeline relies heavily on one report (MCRC 2017),** which itself relies on Highsmith. That is a strong source, but it is one line of evidence.
- **Overlap with U055 (west Flint).** Both share GM's decline and the water crisis. U013 has the lower income and the clearer link to a cleared neighborhood; U055 has Civic Park, GM's covenanted worker housing. Pick one.
