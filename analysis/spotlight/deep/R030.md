## 2. Timeline

1. **1900 to 1920.** Phillips County grew from 26,561 to a peak of 44,530 people; Lee County grew from 19,409 to a peak of 28,852. Both were cotton counties worked largely by sharecroppers and tenants. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_County,_Arkansas ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/lee-county-783/
2. **September 30 to October 1919.** About 100 Black sharecroppers met with the Progressive Farmers and Household Union at a church in Hoop Spur, about three miles north of Elaine, to seek better payment for their cotton. Violence followed. Estimates of Black deaths "have ranged into the hundreds" (the Equal Justice Initiative count is at least 237); five whites died. More than 500 troops came from Camp Pike. On October 31 a grand jury charged 122 Black residents, and 12 were sentenced to death. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/elaine-massacre-of-1919-1102/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas
3. **February 19, 1923.** In *Moore v. Dempsey*, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled 6 to 2 for the Elaine defendants, finding their trials violated due process. The Moore defendants were released January 13, 1925. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/moore-v-dempsey-5200/ ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/elaine-massacre-of-1919-1102/
4. **1920 to 1927.** Helena was second only to Memphis in the U.S. in hardwood products. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/phillips-county-797/
5. **1927 and 1937.** Major floods hit Phillips County farms and towns. In 1927 about 20,000 people around Marianna needed relief. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/phillips-county-797/ ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/marianna-918/
6. **1940s and 1950s.** Black workers from the rural Delta moved into Helena for jobs, and by mid-century the city was 70% Black (Robert Palmer, *Deep Blues*, cited at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena%E2%80%93West_Helena,_Arkansas). Phillips County reached 46,254 people in 1950. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas
7. **1960s.** Farm mechanization, the decline of the timber business, and labor disputes at several plants "eventually cost the two communities thousands of jobs." No exact count is given. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/helena-west-helena-phillips-county/
8. **July 27, 1961.** The Helena Bridge opened as a toll bridge carrying U.S. 49 to Lula, Mississippi. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Bridge
9. **1960, 1967 and 1983.** Lee County gained an auto seat frame plant (Douglas and Lomason, later Camaco) and a clothing plant (P. M. Manufacturing) in 1960 and 1967. By 1983 Camaco was the only manufacturer left in the county; it closed in March 2007. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/lee-county-783/
10. **1966 to 1970.** Marvell Academy opened in Phillips County as a segregation academy in 1966. Lee Academy opened in Marianna the year before Lee County dropped its "freedom of choice" plan in fall 1970; almost 200 white students left the newly unified Marianna district for it. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/lee-county-783/ ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/marianna-boycotts-of-1971-1972-14057/
11. **March 1970 and June 1971 to July 1972.** The Lee County Cooperative Clinic opened in Marianna in March 1970 with help from the federal VISTA program. Black residents boycotted white-owned Marianna stores for 13 months; twelve stores closed. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/marianna-918/ ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/marianna-boycotts-of-1971-1972-14057/
12. **1990.** The census ranked Phillips County among the 16 poorest counties in the U.S. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/phillips-county-797/
13. **2000 to 2009.** The Helena-West Helena area lost 20.89% of its people, the largest drop of any U.S. metro or micro area. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas . The Lake View district (2004) and the Elaine district (2006) were merged into neighboring districts. Same source; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine,_Arkansas
14. **2020 and December 5, 2023.** Helena Regional Medical Center stopped delivering babies in 2020, and the median drive to a delivery hospital for Phillips County mothers rose from 6 to 51 minutes. The hospital became a rural emergency hospital, which ends inpatient care, effective December 5, 2023. https://talkbusiness.net/2024/06/loss-of-labor-and-delivery-services-at-rural-hospitals-raises-risk-for-arkansas-moms/ ; https://www.shepscenter.unc.edu/programs-projects/rural-health/rural-emergency-hospitals/
15. **2020.** Phillips County had 16,568 people, 64% fewer than in 1950; Lee County had 8,600, 70% fewer than in 1920. County life expectancy (2020 to 2022 deaths) was 67.4 in Phillips and 68.9 in Lee, against 77.1 for the U.S. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/lee-county-783/ ; County Health Rankings 2025 (project copy `data/raw/chr/analytic_data2025_v3.csv`), https://www.countyhealthrankings.org

## 3. How the cuts compound

In 1919 Black sharecroppers near Elaine organized to get fair payment for their cotton, and the violence that followed ended that effort; the Supreme Court later threw out the convictions, but the farm system stayed in place. Phillips and Lee counties both peaked by 1920 or 1950 and have lost people in every census since 1950. In the 1960s, the Encyclopedia of Arkansas says, farm machines, a shrinking timber business and plant labor disputes cost Helena and West Helena thousands of jobs. Factories that came to Lee County in the 1960s were mostly gone by 1983, and the last one closed in 2007. When schools integrated around 1970, white families moved to private academies, and a 13-month boycott in Marianna closed a dozen stores. The area then had the steepest population loss in the country in the 2000s, and its hospital stopped delivering babies in 2020; the timing lines up with each earlier loss, but no cited source ties them in one chain.

## 5. What makes it distinct

It is the site of the 1919 Elaine massacre, which began when sharecroppers organized for fair cotton payments and led to a landmark Supreme Court due process ruling. In the 2000s the area lost a larger share of its people than any other U.S. metro or micro area, and in 2020 its hospital stopped delivering babies.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Not the Mississippi Delta.** The editor asked for a Mississippi Delta story. This is the Arkansas side of the river. It is part of the same farm region, but it does not match the request as worded.
- **Spread out.** The 7 tracts run about 50 miles from Marianna to Elaine across two counties. That is a region, not a neighborhood, and the story may read as three towns.
- **Our history numbers include the Mississippi tract.** Every R030 row in `history_long.csv` includes 28027950100. The series must be rebuilt for the 7 kept tracts before charting. The 1980 rows (fit 0.108) must not be used; the 1980 to 1990 jump from 18,745 to 31,294 people is a coverage artifact, not growth.
- **2020 census and ACS disagree.** The 2020 census (on 2010 tracts) gives 17,522 people and 17% vacancy; the ACS 2020 to 2024 gives 18,534 people and 27% vacancy. In a shrinking place, the ACS being 6% higher and 10 points apart on vacancy points to sampling error and different tract versions. Use the census counts for the headline.
- **Poverty went up and down.** Tract poverty was 49% in 1990, 37% in 2000 and 39% in 2020 to 2024. Not a steady decline.
- **City numbers break in 2006.** Helena and West Helena merged, so city counts jump from 6,323 (Helena, 2000) to 12,282 (merged city, 2010). Use tracts or counties.
- **Lee County's prison.** After 1992 about 1,500 incarcerated people are counted in Lee County, which hides part of the county's loss. Subtract group quarters when charting Lee County.
- **Mechanization's role is debated.** Historian Donald Holley argues migration also drove mechanization, not only the reverse (https://eh.net/encyclopedia/mechanical-cotton-picker/).
- **Elaine death toll.** The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says estimates "have ranged into the hundreds"; 237 is one count. Do not present a single number as settled.
- **Factory history is thin.** Mohawk Rubber and the Chrysler plant rest on single sources.
