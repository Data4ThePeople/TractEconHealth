## 2. Timeline

1. **September 17, 1851. Fort Laramie (Horse Creek) Treaty.** The United States recognized Lakota and other nations' territory in exchange for safe passage on the Oregon Trail. It promised $50,000 a year for 50 years; the Senate cut that to 10 years. Text: [Kappler, Indian Affairs: Laws and Treaties, vol. 2 (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20140812163225/http://digital.library.okstate.edu/kappler/Vol2/treaties/sio0594.htm). Summary of the Senate change: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fort_Laramie_(1851)).
2. **April 29, 1868. Fort Laramie Treaty.** It set apart the Great Sioux Reservation "for the absolute and undisturbed use and occupation" of the Lakota and allied nations ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/fort-laramie-treaty)). Article XII said no land cession would be valid without the signatures of three-fourths of adult men ([United States v. Sioux Nation, 1980](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/448/371)). The reservation held about 25 million acres ([Kneip](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/430/584)).
3. **February 28, 1877. Black Hills Act (19 Stat. 254).** Congress took the Black Hills, about 7.5 million acres, without the three-fourths signatures the treaty required ([Sioux Nation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/448/371); acreage from [Kneip](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/430/584)).
4. **March 2, 1889. Act breaking up the Great Sioux Reservation (25 Stat. 888).** Congress split what was left into six reservations and returned about half of the land to the public domain for settlers ([Kneip](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/430/584)). Pine Ridge was one of the new reservations ([SD State Historical Society](https://history.sd.gov/Archives/forms/indian%20archives/Indian%20Archives%20Project/greatsioux.pdf), which lists five, not six). The act also set up allotment of land to individuals; Kneip notes its section 12 borrowed language from the 1887 General Allotment Act. Pine Ridge acres lost to allotment sales: UNCONFIRMED.
5. **December 29, 1890. Wounded Knee Massacre**, in today's tract 46102941100. U.S. Army soldiers killed Lakota people camped at Wounded Knee Creek. Estimates of the dead range from about 150 to 300 ([Wikipedia summary of sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wounded_Knee_Massacre)). Exact toll: UNCONFIRMED from a primary source.
6. **May 27, 1910. Bennett County opened to settlers.** An act directed the Interior Secretary to sell reservation land in Bennett County. The county grew from 96 people in 1910 to 1,924 in 1920 and 4,590 in 1930 ([Wikipedia, Bennett County, citing census counts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_County,_South_Dakota)). Courts later split on whether the county is still reservation land: the Eighth Circuit said yes in 1957 ([Putnam v. United States](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/248/292/328382/), holding as summarized by Wikipedia; opinion not opened), the Eighth Circuit said no in 1975 (Cook v. Parkinson, 525 F.2d 120; [link](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/525/120/169809/), not opened in this pass, UNCONFIRMED), and a 2004 Interior Board of Indian Appeals decision said yes (UNCONFIRMED, from the Wikipedia page only). The statute citation (36 Stat. 440) is UNCONFIRMED.
7. **1942. Aerial gunnery range.** The War Department took 341,725 acres of the reservation for a bombing range, and 125 families were moved; a 1968 law (P.L. 90-468) returned 202,357 acres ([Wikipedia, Pine Ridge Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Ridge_Indian_Reservation)). UNCONFIRMED: no primary source opened.
8. **1952-1973. Federal relocation program.** The BIA moved an estimated 100,000 Native people from reservations to cities such as Denver, Chicago and Los Angeles. By the late 1990s Rapid City had 10,000 to 12,000 Native residents, mainly Lakota ([Donald Fixico, Encyclopedia of the Great Plains](https://plainshumanities.unl.edu/encyclopedia/doc/egp.na.122.html)). Counts for Pine Ridge itself: UNCONFIRMED. The Oglala Sioux Tribe was not terminated in the 1950s (UNCONFIRMED in this pass).
9. **February 27 to May 8, 1973. Occupation of Wounded Knee.** About 200 Oglala Lakota residents and American Indian Movement members held the village for 71 days. They protested the tribal government of the time and the failure to honor treaties. Two occupiers, Frank Clearwater and Lawrence "Buddy" Lamont, were killed ([Wikipedia, with sources including United States v. Means, 8th Cir. 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wounded_Knee_Occupation); [court opinion](https://turtletalk.files.wordpress.com/2012/10/us-v-means-ca8.pdf)).
10. **June 30, 1980. United States v. Sioux Nation.** The Supreme Court held that the 1877 act was a taking that required just compensation, and upheld an award of $17.1 million plus 5% yearly interest from 1877 ([opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/448/371)). That the Lakota nations have declined the money and that the fund now tops $1 billion: UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
11. **1980 to 2014. Life expectancy.** IHME estimates put life expectancy in Oglala Lakota County at 61.3 years in 1980 and 66.8 years in 2014, the lowest of any U.S. county in 2014 ([Indian Country Today, citing IHME](https://ictnews.org/archive/life-expectancy-falling-us-tribal-communities-not-much/); method: [Dwyer-Lindgren et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5543324/)). The U.S. figure was 73.8 in 1980 and 79.1 in 2014 (same JAMA paper). That the county was lowest in every year since 1980 is a quote from the lead author in news reports, not checked against the data file (UNCONFIRMED until the IHME file is pulled).
12. **2000 Census. Poverty.** In Shannon County (now Oglala Lakota County), 52.3% of residents were below the poverty line and median household income was $20,916 ([Wikipedia, citing Census 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oglala_Lakota_County,_South_Dakota)). Re-pull from Census 2000 SF3 before use.
13. **2010. Whiteclay beer sales.** Four stores in Whiteclay, Nebraska, a village of fewer than 15 people in the 2000-2020 censuses on the reservation's southern edge, sold 4.9 million cans of beer, about 13,000 a day. Alcohol is banned on the reservation by tribal law ([Lincoln Journal Star via Rapid City Journal, 2012](https://rapidcityjournal.com/news/oglala-sioux-tribe-sues-whiteclay-stores-beer-makers-distributors/article_38c945c0-539e-11e1-ad82-0019bb2963f4.html)).
14. **April 30, 2017. Whiteclay sales end.** The Nebraska Liquor Control Commission denied all four license renewals, citing a lack of law enforcement; licenses ran out at midnight April 30 ([KETV](https://www.ketv.com/article/license-renewal-denied-for-whiteclay-liquor-stores/9527984)). Date of the Nebraska Supreme Court ruling that ended the store owners' appeal: UNCONFIRMED.
15. **2016 and 2017. Indian Health Service funding.** IHS spent $2,834 per patient in 2016, compared with $9,990 per person for federal health spending nationwide; in 2017 it was $3,332 compared with $9,207 ([U.S. Commission on Civil Rights, Broken Promises, 2018, pp. 66-67](https://www.usccr.gov/files/pubs/2018/12-20-Broken-Promises.pdf)). That is about 72% less than the national federal figure in 2016.

## 3. How the cuts compound

The land base shrank in steps: the 1868 reservation lost the Black Hills in 1877, about half of what remained in 1889, and then settler land sales in Bennett County after 1910. The Supreme Court itself found that the 1877 taking required payment, so that link is on the record. Courts and the Census give dates and counts for each loss, but no source opened here measures how much each step cut income on Pine Ridge, so for income the honest phrase is that the timing lines up. The U.S. Commission on Civil Rights says the IHS has been underfunded for years, and the life expectancy gap between this county and the nation stayed near 12 years from 1980 to 2014; the timing lines up, but neither source proves one caused the other. Whiteclay alcohol sales and the relocation program added pressure in later decades, and the tribe's own alcohol ban and its 2012 lawsuit show it pushed back on both.

## 5. What makes it distinct

The poorest, shortest-lived county in IHME's 1980-2014 series sits on a reservation whose land loss can be dated step by step through a treaty, two acts of Congress, a settler opening and a Supreme Court ruling. The Oglala Lakota Oyate has answered with its own college, a tribal alcohol ban, a lawsuit against beer makers and community groups such as Thunder Valley, so the story can show both the cuts and the response.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **Not a decline from a high point.** The record shows Pine Ridge poor at every point with data, not a place that fell from prosperity. Life expectancy rose 5.5 years from 1980 to 2014, close to the U.S. gain of 5.3 years. The story is about a gap that persisted, not a recent drop.
- **Census undercount.** The 2020 Post-Enumeration Survey found a 5.64% net undercount of American Indian and Alaska Native people living on reservations, after 4.88% in 2010 ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022/2020-census-estimates-of-undercount-and-overcount.html)). Rates and counts here may be off by that much or more. The county count drop from 11,323 in 1980 to 9,902 in 1990, then up to 12,466 in 2000, looks more like a counting problem than a real loss (UNCONFIRMED).
- **Differential privacy (2020).** The 2020 census added statistical noise to protect privacy ([Census disclosure avoidance](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/decennial-census/decade/2020/planning-management/process/disclosure-avoidance.html)). Small areas such as single tracts and villages are the most affected, so do not chart 2020 block or village counts. ACS tract estimates also carry large margins (Kyle's income: ± $38,147).
- **Geography changes.** Shannon County became Oglala Lakota County with a new FIPS code (46113 to 46102) on May 1, 2015 ([Census county changes](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/geography/technical-documentation/county-changes.2010.html)). Any series must join both codes. The reservation part of Jackson County was once a separate county (Washabaugh), merged in 1983 (UNCONFIRMED).
- **Tract history.** Our current history_long rows are for the old 7-tract cluster that includes Rosebud. They must be rebuilt for the 5 recommended tracts. The USALEEP 2010-2015 life expectancy row (74.6) has fit 0.529 and should not be used.
- **Wikipedia figures.** Several numbers above (Bennett and Oglala Lakota population, the 2000 poverty rate, the gunnery range) come from Wikipedia tables. Re-pull them from NHGIS or primary records before publishing.
- **Framing risk.** Pine Ridge is often covered only through poverty numbers. Leading with tribal institutions and the treaty record, not just deficits, matters here.
