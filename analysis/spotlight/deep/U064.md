## 1. Boundary

**Local names.** Grove Hall, the business district where Blue Hill Avenue, Warren Street, Washington Street and Geneva Avenue meet. Tract 821 is the Roxbury side (Elm Hill and Schuyler Street area, west of Blue Hill Avenue). Tract 901 is the Dorchester side (Columbia Road, Wolcott Street, Quincy Street area). Franklin Park lies just west. Both tracts are in ZIP 02121, with part of 821 in 02119.

## 2. Timeline

1. **1920s to early 1950s.** Blue Hill Avenue was the spine of Boston's largest Jewish district. Peak Jewish population across Roxbury, Dorchester and Mattapan is given as about 90,000; Gerald Gamm's *Urban Exodus* (Harvard University Press, 1999) gives 70,000 in Roxbury and Dorchester in the early 1950s and dates the start of the shift to the 1920s. https://www.timesofisrael.com/what-happened-to-the-jews-of-bostons-jew-hill-avenue/ ; https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.4159/9780674037489/html
2. **1938.** The Home Owners' Loan Corporation map graded both tracts entirely "C" (yellow, "definitely declining"), in area C10, a long strip along Blue Hill Avenue. Red area D9 (Roxbury, to the north) comes within about 15 meters of tract 821. Computed for this note by overlaying the Mapping Inequality polygons on the 2020 tract shapes. https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining . The words of the C10 area description: UNCONFIRMED (the site would not load in this session). This supports the triage line that the corridor "just barely escaped redlining."
3. **1950s.** Middle-class Jewish families and, by the mid-1950s, major Jewish institutions moved to Brookline and Newton. By 1960 more than half of the peak Jewish population had left the city. https://www.timesofisrael.com/what-happened-to-the-jews-of-bostons-jew-hill-avenue/ . The count of five institutions moving in the early and mid-1950s comes from a review of Gamm at https://muse.jhu.edu/article/616 , which could not be opened here: UNCONFIRMED.
4. **1962 or 1963.** A consortium of savings banks, later called the Boston Banks Urban Renewal Group (B-BURG), was formed with city urban renewal officials and lent in the Washington Park urban renewal area just north of Grove Hall. Sources differ on the year (1962 or 1963) and the amount ($1 million or $2.5 million). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_White_(politician) (citing Levine and Harmon, pp. 167-168) ; https://ejournals.bc.edu/index.php/orac/article/download/13495/10843/29739 (Boston College *Oracle*, a student history journal)
5. **June 2 to 4, 1967.** Mothers for Adequate Welfare held a sit-in at the welfare office at 515 Blue Hill Avenue in Grove Hall. Police cleared the building, and three nights of unrest followed along a 15-block strip of Blue Hill Avenue. About 1,000 police were on the street by early Saturday, two buildings burned, and officials estimated damage at $500,000. https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1967/6/15/roxbury-quiet-in-past-finally-breaks/ ; https://whenandwhereinboston.org/?p=68071 . The triage figure of "about three dozen businesses" looted or burned: UNCONFIRMED.
6. **May 13, 1968.** The mayor announced that Boston banks and insurers would provide $50 million for low-income housing. B-BURG, described as "moribund," was revived that spring and lent only FHA-insured mortgages at the FHA rate of 6.75%, almost all to Black families, inside "a very narrow swatch" of Dorchester and Mattapan. J. Anthony Lukas, review in *Partisan Review* 59:3 (1992), p. 429, https://www.bu.edu/partisanreview/books/PR1992V59N3/HTML/files/assets/basic-html/page97.html
7. **July and August 1968.** B-BURG, then 22 institutions holding $4 billion in assets, drew the area for a $29 million loan program along Blue Hill Avenue in Mattapan and Roxbury. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_White_(politician) , citing Hillel Levine and Lawrence Harmon, *The Death of an American Jewish Community: A Tragedy of Good Intentions* (Free Press, 1992), pp. 167-180. Book page numbers were not checked against the book itself. Whether the line took in tracts 821 and 901: UNCONFIRMED. One account says it ran "from the South End, through Roxbury and part of Dorchester, into Mattapan" (Boston College *Oracle*, citing *Boston Globe*, April 5 and 7, 1972), which would include Grove Hall, but Levine and Harmon and Lukas center it on Mattapan and south Dorchester.
8. **By March 31, 1970.** More than 1,300 minority families had bought homes with B-BURG mortgages, most of them in Mattapan. A Model Cities study found 65% of homes bought under the program from 1968 to 1970 needed major repairs at purchase. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_White_(politician) (footnotes 67 and 68; the underlying sources could not be read in this session)
9. **1970.** The two Grove Hall tracts had 11,900 people and were already 94.5% Black. Poverty was 21.6% and unemployment 6.5%. `history_long.csv` (NHGIS, fit 0.987)
10. **September 13, 1971.** A U.S. Senate subcommittee opened three days of hearings on the B-BURG program. https://whenandwhereinboston.org/entry/u-s-senate-subcommittee-begins-hearings-on-the-boston-banks-urban-group-program
11. **1970s.** More than 1,200 B-BURG buyers lost their homes to foreclosure, and more than 1,000 buildings stood abandoned in parts of Dorchester and Mattapan (Lew Finfer, *Dorchester Reporter*, May 29, 2019, https://www.dotnews.com/columns/2019/how-redlining-dashed-dreams-hurt-neighborhoods). From July 1977 through June 1978, 91% of government-insured foreclosures in Boston were in Dorchester, Mattapan and Roxbury (Levine and Harmon, pp. 276-277, as cited at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_White_(politician)). Author Jim Vrabel says about half of buyers lost their homes within five years (https://www.wbur.org/radioboston/2023/06/14/hidden-history-housing-mattapan-jewish). Counts for tracts 821 and 901 alone: UNCONFIRMED.
12. **1970 to 1990.** The two tracts lost 25% of their people (11,900 to 8,968). Unemployment rose from 6.5% to 10.8% (1980) to 16.5% (1990). In 1990, 15.7% of homes were vacant and 17.5% were owner-occupied. `history_long.csv`
13. **1987 to 1990, then 1996.** Youth homicides in Boston rose from 22 to 73 a year, concentrated in Roxbury, Dorchester and Mattapan. Operation Ceasefire began in 1996. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ceasefire , citing Kennedy, Braga and Piehl, *Reducing Gun Violence* (National Institute of Justice, 2001), https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/188741.pdf . Grove Hall figures: UNCONFIRMED.
14. **1992.** A Federal Reserve Bank of Boston study of 1990 mortgage applications found Black and Hispanic applicants in the Boston area about 60% more likely to be turned down than white applicants with the same financial traits (17% vs. 11%). https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/research-department-working-paper/1992/mortgage-lending-in-boston-interpreting-hmda-data.aspx
15. **2008 to 2012, and 2010 to 2015 deaths.** Unemployment in the two tracts reached 22.5% (ACS 2008-2012), and home ownership stayed near 16% to 20%. The 2007 to 2012 foreclosure wave in Dorchester and Roxbury is noted by Finfer (link above); tract counts: UNCONFIRMED. Life expectancy was 76.9 in tract 821 and 80.1 in tract 901 (78.4 combined), against 81.5 in Back Bay tract 107.02 and an average of 79.6 across Suffolk County tracts. USALEEP 2010-2015, https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nvss/usaleep/usaleep.html (project copy `data/raw/usaleep/US_A.CSV`)

## Life expectancy reporting the editor recalled

- **The "33-year gap."** It comes from a 2012 report for the Boston Public Health Commission by the Center on Human Needs at Virginia Commonwealth University: Zimmerman, Evans, Woolf and Haley, *Social Capital and Health Outcomes in Boston* (2012). It used 2003 to 2007 deaths. The highest tract was in Back Bay (91.9 years) and the lowest in Roxbury (58.9). BPHC, *Health of Boston 2023 Provisional Mortality and Life Expectancy Report*, p. 52, https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2023/05/HOB_Mortality_LE_2023_FINAL_May12.pdf . BPHC later described the two areas as "2.3 miles apart." *Live Long and Well* (January 2025), https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2025/01/BPHC%20Live%20Long%20and%20Well%20Report_Jan2025_FINAL.pdf
- **Correction to the brief.** The 2013 Robert Wood Johnson Foundation and VCU "short distances to large gaps" maps covered Washington, D.C., New Orleans, Kansas City, the San Joaquin Valley and others; the announcement lists no Boston map (https://news.vcu.edu/article/a_matter_of_miles). The Boston number comes from the separate 2012 VCU report above. A Boston map in that series: UNCONFIRMED.
- **The 2023 update.** Using 2015 to 2021 deaths, BPHC found 68.8 years in Roxbury tract 803 (Nubian Square, median household income $41,211) and 91.6 years in Back Bay tract 107.02 ($141,250), a 22.8-year gap. By neighborhood, Back Bay and nearby areas were 82.8, Roxbury 77.8, Dorchester ZIPs 02121 and 02125 (which include Grove Hall) 77.8, and Mattapan 77.3: about a 5-year gap. Same BPHC report, pp. 51-53; WBUR, May 11, 2023, https://www.wbur.org/news/2023/05/11/boston-life-expectancy-gap-back-bay-roxbury
- **What it means for Grove Hall.** Neither the 33-year nor the 23-year headline is about Grove Hall. The Roxbury tract in both is near Nubian Square, about 1.5 to 2 miles north. BPHC has not published 2015 to 2021 figures for tracts 821 and 901: UNCONFIRMED; ask BPHC for its tract table. On USALEEP, Grove Hall's gap to Back Bay is 1.4 to 4.6 years by tract, much smaller than the headlines. The story can use the citywide gap as context but should not say Grove Hall is the low end of it.

## 3. How the cuts compound

The 1938 map graded the Blue Hill Avenue corridor as declining, with a red-graded area right next door, and the timing lines up with the Jewish middle class and its institutions moving out in the 1950s. Levine and Harmon argue that B-BURG's drawn loan area, FHA-only lending and blockbusting real estate agents sped up white departure and left Black buyers in homes many could not keep; Lukas and the federal official quoted in the *Oracle* article make the same link between the line and panic selling. Finfer ties the FHA foreclosures to abandoned and burned buildings in Dorchester and Mattapan. In Grove Hall's own tracts, the timing lines up: population fell 25% from 1970 to 1990 while unemployment rose from 6.5% to 16.5% and one home in six stood empty. The 1992 Boston Fed study shows mortgage denials still differed by race two decades later, and the timing lines up with home ownership in these tracts staying below 20% from 1990 to today.

## 5. What makes it distinct

A bank group drew a line on a map and steered FHA-insured loans for Black buyers inside it, so this is a lending story more than a highway or plant-closing story. It plays out on one avenue that also saw a 1967 welfare protest turn into three nights of unrest.

## 6. Counter-evidence and caveats

- **The turnover in these two tracts came before B-BURG.** The tracts were already 94.5% Black in 1970, two years into the program. B-BURG's heaviest effect was in Mattapan and south Dorchester, south of Grove Hall. In Grove Hall, B-BURG is part of the corridor story, not the start of it. Pull 1950 and 1960 NHGIS tract data to show when the change happened here.
- **Scholars disagree on the cause of the Jewish exodus.** Gamm argues that the way Jewish institutions were organized (portable, member-run) mattered more than bankers and real estate agents, and that the shift began in the 1920s. Levine and Harmon put more weight on B-BURG and blockbusting. The story should present both.
- **B-BURG figures differ by source.** Founding year (1962 or 1963), the early Washington Park amount ($1 million or $2.5 million), and the program total ($29 million planned, while *Boston Globe* figures cited by *Oracle* show about $5 million lent from August to December 1969) do not line up. Cite Levine and Harmon page numbers from the book itself before publishing.
- **Life expectancy gap is smaller here than the headlines.** USALEEP puts Grove Hall at 76.9 and 80.1, and Back Bay tract 107.02 at 81.5, while BPHC puts 107.02 at 91.6 for 2015-2021. USALEEP gives the same 81.5 to four Back Bay tracts and the same 76.9 to several Roxbury tracts, which suggests heavy smoothing for small tracts. Do not compare USALEEP and BPHC numbers directly.
- **Demographic change.** The Black share fell from 91.6% (1990) to 59.0% (2020) as Latino and Caribbean residents moved in (2020 census: 35.7% Hispanic in the two tracts). Comparisons over time mix different groups of residents.
- **History numbers check out.** 2020 owner share of 15.8% matches the 2020 census tenure count (617 of 3,897 occupied homes). One oddity: the 2020-2024 ACS Black share (46.6%) is well below the 2020 census (59.0% in the file, 53.7% non-Hispanic Black in DHC). This is likely ACS sampling plus a definition difference; use census counts for the trend line.
- **Health ranks.** The candidate sits at the 2.7th income and 12.1st health percentile. That is a strong screen fit, but the life expectancy rank is middling within Boston.
