## History (dated bullets with sources)

- **1935 to 1940: HOLC grades.** Dayton's HOLC map is dated 1935 in a Dayton Daily News caption (https://www.daytondailynews.com/news/lasting-scars-the-legacy-race-based-redlining/SID35gjyuwblkQtjLeKkbK, page now returns 404; year UNCONFIRMED against Mapping Inequality). Our own overlay of the Mapping Inequality polygons (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining, national GeoJSON) on the 2020 tracts finds: tract 1651 is 77% grade D (area D11, "Hazardous"); tract 41 is 47% grade D (D11) and 17% industrial; tracts 2, 38 and 39 are 49% to 86% grade C ("Definitely Declining", areas C12 and C14); the Trotwood and Drexel tracts were outside the graded city. So the core of the cluster was rated C or D, none A or B.
- **About 1966: segregation.** About 60,000 Black residents, about 96% of Dayton's Black population, lived on the west side, and a 1966 Dayton Daily News article listed lack of access to home loans as a grievance (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Dayton_race_riot, citing the Dayton Daily News).
- **Sept. 1, 1966: unrest.** Lester Mitchell, a Black man, was shot in a drive-by on West Fifth Street about 3 a.m.; unrest lasted about 24 hours, the Ohio National Guard sent 1,000 troops, 1 died and 30 were hurt, arrests are reported as 100 to over 500, and damage was about $250,000, mostly to Black-owned west side businesses. Two more disturbances followed in 1967 and one in 1968. Businesses moved or never reopened, and white flight sped up (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Dayton_race_riot; https://daytondailynews.com/data/special-projects/lasting-scars; https://www.blackpast.org/african-american-history/the-1966-dayton-ohio-uprising/). The killing was never solved.
- **1960s to 1990s: I-75 and US-35.** US-35 was planned and built through West Dayton in the 1960s, and more westward expansion in the 1990s took more homes and businesses (Congress for the New Urbanism, https://www.cnu.org/node/10848). A 1.4-mile stretch of the US-35 extension removed 283 homes, 17 businesses and 5 nonprofits (year of that count not given), and whole blocks along West Third Street were razed; the connected McGee Blvd. project took about 30 years and opened in pieces around 1990 to 1991 (https://daytonvistas.com/james-h-mcgee-boulevard-history-road-widenings-35-west-and-neighborhoods/, citing Dayton Daily News, 1990 to 1992). In 1940, West Fifth Street from Bank St. to Broadway ("the Nickel") had more than 70 businesses, and many blame I-75 in part for its decline (https://www.daytondailynews.com/local/deconstructing-segregation-how-highways-contributed-and-what-can-be-done-about-it-today/LIAY4RKK3JBSPCLZ6XAQPRZZNA/). I-75 construction years in Dayton: UNCONFIRMED (likely late 1950s to 1960s). A former NAACP president cited highway land taking on the west side as a cause of decline (Wikipedia riot page).
- **1960s to 1980s: urban renewal.** Clearance of West Third St. blocks is documented above; the formal federal urban renewal project names, acreage and dates for West Dayton are UNCONFIRMED. Check the "Renewing Inequality" urban renewal dataset (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/renewal) for Dayton projects.
- **1960 to 2020: population loss.** Dayton city peaked at 262,332 in 1960 and fell to 137,644 in 2020 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio, from decennial census).
- **1999 to 2008: GM and Delphi.** Delphi had about 15,000 local jobs in 1999 and shrank over the next decade (https://www.daytondailynews.com/data/news/jobs-wins-and-losses/). Delphi filed for bankruptcy in October 2005 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi_Automotive). The Delphi Home Avenue plant, on West Dayton's southwest side, closed in 2008 with about 400 workers (https://daytonvistas.com/james-h-mcgee-boulevard-history-road-widenings-35-west-and-neighborhoods/). GM announced in June 2008 that its Moraine truck plant would close; it had peaked at about 4,000 workers and had about 1,100 when it closed in 2008 (https://daytondailynews.com/local/closure-of-gm-moraine-plant-ended-local-manufacturing-era-15-years-ago/IJKOIX7MH5D2ZBBNC2ALBKOEC4/). Earlier GM closures in Dayton (Frigidaire, Delco plants, Inland) and their job counts: UNCONFIRMED.
- **June 2009: NCR leaves.** NCR moved its headquarters from Dayton to the Duluth, Georgia area in 2009 after more than 125 years in Dayton and sold most Dayton property in June 2009 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCR_Corporation; https://daytonchamber.org/?p=13746). Job counts differ: NCR said 1,250 Dayton jobs, while its state layoff notice listed 644 (as reported by the Dayton Daily News; exact article UNCONFIRMED, see the jobs dashboard above). NCR's Dayton peak headcount: UNCONFIRMED.
- **2005: retail.** Salem Mall in Trotwood (opened 1966) lost its Lazarus and JCPenney anchors in 1998 and closed in early 2005; Sears there closed in January 2014 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Mall).
- **2008: foreclosure wave.** The Dayton article on Wikipedia ties recent population loss to "the national housing crisis that began in 2008" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio). Specific foreclosure filing counts for Montgomery County or West Dayton: UNCONFIRMED (web search budget ran out). Use HMDA and county filing data below.
- **2017: overdose deaths.** Montgomery County had a record year. The Ohio Department of Health counted 521 accidental overdose deaths (320 in 2016), the county reported 566, and the county's age-adjusted rate led Ohio; the claim that it led the nation was widely repeated but the Dayton Daily News found the national ranking could not be confirmed (https://www.daytondailynews.com/news/state-cites-progress-despite-2017-record-for-opioid-deaths/Fn5wUk2rug9esaIjF6pahP; https://www.daytondailynews.com/news/crime--law/montgomery-county-the-nation-leader-for-drug-ods-one-knows/3Sg775iI6VnVXntb6aDmtM). The editor's phrase "one of the highest" is safe; "the highest in the nation" is not.
- **July 2018: hospital closure.** Premier Health announced on Jan. 17, 2018, that Good Samaritan Hospital (opened 1932, 577 beds, 3,300 employees) would close; final patients left July 20 and it closed July 23, 2018. A civil rights complaint said it was the only hospital on the largely Black west side; the HHS Office for Civil Rights later found no violation (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Samaritan_Hospital_(Dayton); https://www.wyso.org/news/2018-07-19/good-samaritan-emergency-department-shuts-doors-amid-protest-federal-civil-rights-investigation).
- **May 27, 2019: tornadoes.** An EF4 tornado (170 mph, 20-mile path, up to 0.6 mile wide) crossed eastern Trotwood between Shiloh Springs and Westbrook roads, then Northridge, Old North Dayton and Riverside; 19 tornadoes hit the region that night (https://www.weather.gov/iln/20190527_trotwood; https://www.weather.gov/iln/20190527). Trotwood reported 500 homes damaged and 59 destroyed (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotwood,_Ohio). Whether the path touched these specific tracts is UNCONFIRMED; by our rough line from the NWS endpoints the path ran about 1 to 2 km north of the Trotwood tracts' centers. Check the NWS KMZ track before using this as a cause for these tracts.
- **Grocery closures.** A West Dayton food desert and the co-op Gem City Market on Salem Ave. are widely discussed, but store names and closing dates were not confirmed in this pass: UNCONFIRMED.
