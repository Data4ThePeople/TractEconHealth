# U052: West and southwest side of Rockford, Illinois (plus one tract on the south side east of the river)

## Name and place
- Eight Winnebago County tracts in the city of Rockford: 17201001000, 001100, 001200, 001800, 002100, 002600, 002900, 003100. 2020 population about 19,700 (2020 PL 94-171); candidate file pop 20,550.
- Seven tracts lie west of the Rock River, from the near west side by downtown (tract 002600) south through the southwest side (tracts 001000, 001200, 002100) and north (002900, 003100). Tract 001800 lies east of the river on the south side (TIGERweb extents, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). Local usage: "the West Side" and "southwest Rockford" (City of Rockford planning documents, https://cdn.rockfordil.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1030). Exact neighborhood names by tract: UNCONFIRMED.
- The Barber-Colman campus, a former major employer, sits in southwest Rockford in or next to the cluster.

## Rule-out flags
- Tract 002600 holds 579 people in adult correctional facilities (very likely the Winnebago County Jail at 650 W. State St., UNCONFIRMED) out of 2,736 in the tract and about 19,700 in the cluster (2020 PL 94-171 table P5, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl). Small at cluster level, but consider dropping tract 002600 for the health and income comparison.
- Stable since 1990 (0.974) with tract data back to 1970.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1930s: HOLC graded Rockford; grades for west side blocks need a check (Mapping Inequality, https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining). A reporter's account describes the Rock River dividing a historically white East Side from the West Side, with Black buyers steered west (Chicago Defender, https://chicagodefender.com/blackflightmeetsracialresistance). Rockford HOLC coverage: UNCONFIRMED.
- Mid-1960s: First attempts to desegregate Rockford schools (Federal Bar Association outline of People Who Care, https://www.fedbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/1.-People-Who-Care-outline.pdf).
- Mid-1970s: Barber-Colman employs up to 3,000 at its Rockford campus (City of Rockford, Barber-Colman page, https://rockfordil.gov/497/Barber-Colman-Campus; the cdn host returned a certificate error, so this is from the search summary).
- 1982: Rockford metro has the nation's highest jobless rate in July 1982, nearly one in five; the city rate hits a record 26% in November; about 30,000 residents out of work. The machine tool and fastener industries were tied to autos and construction (Illinois Issues, NIU archive, https://ipo.lib.niu.edu/1982/ii821004.html and https://ipo.lib.niu.edu/1983/ii830318.html; BLS Monthly Labor Review 1982, https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/1982/08/art1full.pdf).
- May 11, 1989: People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education is filed over a school reorganization plan that would close or break up schools serving the west side; it became a class action finding intentional segregation; the remedial order ended in 2001 (Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse, https://www.clearinghouse.net/case/1052/; EdWeek 1993, https://www.edweek.org/education/educational-bias-is-issue-in-ill-district-desegregation-case/1993/04). A founder said the group set out "to fight for the rights of west and old Rockford."
- About 2001-2021: The Barber-Colman campus sits vacant for two decades until the city sells it in October 2021 (City of Rockford page, above; Rock River Current, https://www.rockrivercurrent.com/tag/barber-colman/page/2/).
- 2020: City brownfields framework names the Barber-Colman site and areas to its west as the key reuse area for southwest Rockford; city plans cite years of deferred investment on the West Side (https://cdn.rockfordil.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1030).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. The 1982 job collapse and the 1989 school segregation case are well dated and the river line is a clear geography, but west-side specific sources on lending and plant closures are thinner than for bigger cities.

## What makes it distinct
A machine-tool city that led the nation in unemployment in 1982, where a river split the town by race and a federal court later found the school district had segregated the west side on purpose.
