# U024: Highland Park and the Davison corridor (Dexter-Fenkell, Oakman Blvd, Chalfonte, Bethune), Detroit area, MI

## Name and place
Eight tracts in a band along the Davison/Fenkell corridor, population about 12,926. Two tracts (5538 and 5598) are in the separate city of Highland Park (Census geocoder, incorporated place). The other six are in Detroit; the City of Detroit neighborhood layer puts them in Dexter-Fenkell (5303), Oakman Blvd Community (5304, 5317), Chalfonte (5364) and Bethune Community (5367, 5371) (https://services2.arcgis.com/qvkbeam7Wirps6zC/arcgis/rest/services/Current_City_of_Detroit_Neighborhoods/FeatureServer/0). The west end (Bethune) is about 5 miles from the east end (Highland Park), so this is a corridor, not one neighborhood.

## Rule-out flags
- No prison, college, base or single large public housing complex. The HUD public housing layer shows only Downes Manor (38 units) in tract 5538 (https://services.arcgis.com/VTyQ9soqVukalItT/arcgis/rest/services/Public_Housing_Developments/FeatureServer/0).
- Mixed geography: the strongest story (Highland Park) covers only 2 of 8 tracts. Recommend re-cutting the cluster around Highland Park's tracts plus the adjoining Detroit tracts if this is chosen.
- Boundaries stable (stability 0.996).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1907 to 1913: Ford buys 160 acres in Highland Park, finishes the plant in 1909 and opens the first moving assembly line in 1913 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_Michigan).
- 1930: Highland Park population 52,959, its peak decade (same source, census table).
- 1939: HOLC map grades most of this corridor "C" (71% of sampled area), with "D" pockets in 5317 and 5598 (Mapping Inequality GeoJSON, our own point sample: https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining).
- 1942: The Davison Freeway opens through Highland Park, described as the first depressed urban freeway in the US; about 130 homes were demolished for it (https://detroithistorical.org/learn/encyclopedia-of-detroit/davison-freeway; https://www.loc.gov/item/mi0509; demolition count from https://americascanceledhighways.com/tag/m-8, UNCONFIRMED by a primary source).
- 1950s to 1967: Dexter-Linwood and Dexter-Davison shift from the center of Jewish Detroit to a Black neighborhood as white families move to Oak Park and Southfield (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Metro_Detroit; https://detroit1967.org/learn/online-research/collection/archive/document-digital-dwight-stackhouse-july-31st-2015).
- Late 1950s to 1973: Ford demolishes large parts of the Highland Park plant and ends tractor work there in 1973 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_Michigan).
- 1991 to 1993: Chrysler moves its headquarters to Auburn Hills; it was 25% of the city's tax base and 50% of its budget, and the move cost 6,000 jobs (same source).
- 2000 to 2009: State-appointed emergency financial manager runs Highland Park (same source).
- August 2011: More than two-thirds of residential streetlights are removed over an unpaid electric bill (same source).
- 2013: Detroit's water department sues Highland Park for $17.7 million in unpaid water and sewer charges (same source).
- Population: 1930 52,959; 1960 38,063; 1990 20,121; 2020 8,977 (same source).

4. Highland Park has a clear, dated chain (plant, freeway, HQ exit, state takeover, streetlights, water debt) with public records for each, but the Detroit half of the cluster dilutes it.

## What makes it distinct
A small city that the auto industry built and then left, where the loss of one company's tax base led to a state takeover and the removal of streetlights.
