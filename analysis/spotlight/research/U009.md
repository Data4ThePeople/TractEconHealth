# U009: Skid Row, downtown Los Angeles, CA

## Name and place
Two tracts, 06037206301 and 06037206302 (Census Tracts 2063.01 and 2063.02), city of Los Angeles, together about 0.1 square miles, population about 3,218. TIGERweb extent: lat 34.0407 to 34.0461, lon -118.2504 to -118.2439, which is in the core of Skid Row east of downtown (Census geocoder: Central City PUMA, ZIP 90013). Residents and media call it Skid Row (Central City East).

## Rule-out flags
- **Institutional concentration by policy.** Since the 1976 "containment" plan, the city steered missions, shelters and homeless services into Skid Row, and SRO hotels were preserved there (https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/279-the-containment-plan/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skid_Row,_Los_Angeles). The numbers mostly measure who those shelters and SROs serve, not a residential neighborhood that declined.
- **Not a decline story.** The area has been a low-income district for more than a century, not a place that slid over decades.
- Boundaries: piece of a larger 2010 tract; no 1990 match.
- **Recommendation:** rule out.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- Late 1960s: Skid Row had about 15,000 housing units, mostly SROs; less than a decade later half had been demolished, driven by stricter fire and safety codes (https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/279-the-containment-plan/).
- 1975 to 1977: The "Blue Book" plan, adopted by the City Council in 1976, created a "policy of containment" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skid_Row,_Los_Angeles). Sources differ on 1975, 1976 and 1977.
- After 1976: The Community Redevelopment Agency founded SRO Housing Corporation to buy and fix SRO hotels (https://srohousing.org/about/).
- 2008: Los Angeles passed a permanent ordinance to preserve SRO hotels (https://therealdeal.com/la/2024/03/20/la-wants-to-demolish-sro-hotel-rooms-for-the-homeless).
- 2024: City leaders proposed replacing some Skid Row SRO rooms (same source).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
2 as a compounding-decline story; the containment policy is well documented, but this is a services district by design, not a neighborhood worn down by many causes.

## What makes it distinct
The only candidate whose numbers were set by a deliberate plan to concentrate homeless services in one place.
