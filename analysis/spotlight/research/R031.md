# R031: Rocky Ford, Otero County, Colorado

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1877-1928: George Swink plants melons from 1877; Rocky Ford becomes the "Sweet Melon Capital"; melon shipping peaks in 1928 at 3,000 rail carloads (search summary of local history sources: https://corf.specialdistrict.org/welcome-to-rocky-ford; https://magazine.csusystem.edu/?p=1829). Peak figure should be checked against a primary source.
- 1899: American Crystal Sugar builds a beet sugar plant in Rocky Ford; the Rocky Ford Ditch Company dates from 1888 (Colorado Sun, 2025, https://coloradosun.com/2025/10/21/rocky-ford-arkansas-river-watermelons-ditches/).
- 1960: Town reaches its peak census population, 4,929 (Wikipedia, above).
- 1974: The Sugar Act of 1934 expires and sugar import tariffs are lifted, which the Colorado Sun says "killed the Lower Arkansas Valley's largest industry" (Colorado Sun, above).
- 1979: Crystal Sugar closes the Rocky Ford plant; local farmers cannot raise money to buy it; an investor group buys it for its water rights (Colorado Sun, above).
- 1980-1987: Resource Investment Group buys 58% of Rocky Ford Ditch shares in 1980 and in 1987 sells the water, about 8,200 acre-feet a year, to the city of Aurora for $25.5 million (Colorado Water Conservation Board record, https://dnrweblink.state.co.us/CWCB/0/doc/50691/Page2.aspx). The water district's manager says Aurora "dried up around 7,500 acres on the Rocky Ford Ditch" (Colorado Sun).
- 1970s-2009: Basin-wide, more than 150,000 acre-feet were severed from farmland by 2009, and cities held rights to more than 102,000 irrigated acres in the Arkansas Basin (Colorado Sun, citing Water Education Colorado).
- 1990-2020: Town falls from 4,162 to 3,876; 2003 sale of more Rocky Ford Ditch shares on record (CWCB, https://dnrweblink.state.co.us/CWCB/0/doc/167406/Page1.aspx).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. A clear chain of dated events (Sugar Act expiry, plant closure, water sale with acre-feet and price) with state water records, though the local health and income link to the tract is less documented.

## What makes it distinct
A farm town whose water was sold to a growing city 150 miles away, so the land that made its melons famous was taken out of production by contract rather than by drought.
