## History (dated bullets with sources)
- Before 1930: Cotton plantations and sharecropping. The county had 44,530 people in 1920 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas).
- September 30 to October 2, 1919: The Elaine massacre. About 100 Black sharecroppers met with the Progressive Farmers and Household Union at Hoop Spur, near Elaine. White mobs and U.S. troops followed. Black deaths are estimated in the hundreds, and 122 Black residents were charged (https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/elaine-massacre-of-1919-1102/). Wikipedia cites at least 237 killed (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas). Moore v. Dempsey later overturned the convictions; the 1923 ruling date was not in the fetched text and is UNCONFIRMED.
- 1940s to 1950s: Mechanization cut farm labor. Black families from the surrounding Delta moved into Helena, which was 70% Black by mid-century (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena%E2%80%93West_Helena,_Arkansas).
- 1950 to 2020: Phillips County fell from 46,254 to 16,568, a 64.2% drop. The losses were 17.7% from 2000 to 2010 and 23.8% from 2010 to 2020 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas). From 2000 to 2009, the Helena-West Helena area lost 20.9% of its people, the largest drop of any statistical area in the U.S. (same source).
- 1970s: Mohawk Rubber (a Yokohama subsidiary) closes in Helena, and unemployment rises (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena%E2%80%93West_Helena,_Arkansas). Year and job count are UNCONFIRMED.
- 1960 to 2000: Helena falls from 11,500 to 6,323 people, a 45.0% drop (same source).
- 1966: Marvell Academy opens as a private segregation academy (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_County,_Arkansas). In 2004 and 2006, the Lake View and Elaine school districts are merged away (same source).
- January 1, 2006: Helena and West Helena merge (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena%E2%80%93West_Helena,_Arkansas).
- River-town decline: Helena's port still runs barge traffic, but the downtown riverfront decline, the hospital's status and the Helena Regional Medical Center service cuts are UNCONFIRMED. No Helena or Marianna entry appeared on the Sheps closure list as fetched (https://www.shepscenter.unc.edu/programs-projects/rural-health/rural-hospital-closures/).

## Story strength (1-5)
5. The causes are layered and well documented: the 1919 massacre, mechanization, mid-century migration into Helena, factory loss, and the steepest population loss of any U.S. metro or micro area in the 2000s, all backed by long census series.

## What makes it distinct
It is the site of one of the deadliest racial massacres in U.S. history, tied directly to sharecroppers organizing. The county then lost nearly two-thirds of its people after 1950.
