# U027: North St. Louis (The Ville, JeffVanderLou, St. Louis Place, Carr Square/Columbus Square, Fairground area) plus two East St. Louis, Illinois tracts

## Name and place
- Six St. Louis city tracts: 29510110100 and 110300 (around Natural Bridge and Fair avenues, likely the Fairground or O'Fallon area, UNCONFIRMED), 121200 (JeffVanderLou), 126600 (Carr Square/Columbus Square to the riverfront, roughly -90.205 to -90.178), 127100 (St. Louis Place, which includes the NGA West campus site), 127700 (The Ville/Greater Ville). Two St. Clair County, Illinois tracts in East St. Louis: 17163504502 and 504600 (Census geocoder, https://geocoding.geo.census.gov; TIGERweb, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov).
- 2020 population about 15,350 (2020 PL 94-171).
- The Pruitt-Igoe site sits at or just outside the southern edge of tract 127100/126600; exact tract UNCONFIRMED.

## Rule-out flags
- The cluster crosses the Mississippi River and a state line. North St. Louis and East St. Louis have different histories, governments and data. Recommend splitting: use the six St. Louis tracts as the candidate and drop the two East St. Louis tracts (or treat East St. Louis as its own candidate).
- Group quarters: 206 people in adult correctional facilities in tract 126600 (facility UNCONFIRMED) out of about 15,350. Not large enough to drive the numbers.
- Stability 0.838, oldest year 1990 per file, though St. Louis city has tracts back to 1940 and NHGIS series should allow older comparisons.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1930s: HOLC graded St. Louis; grades for these blocks need a check (Mapping Inequality, https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining). UNCONFIRMED.
- 1937-1979: Homer G. Phillips Hospital, the city's hospital for Black patients and a center of The Ville, opens in 1937; on August 17, 1979 the city closes all but a small outpatient unit, which runs until 1985 (BlackPast, https://www.blackpast.org/african-american-history/homer-g-phillips-hospital-1937-1979; Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer_G._Phillips_Hospital). No hospital was rebuilt in The Ville.
- 1947: City planners map "obsolete and blighted" districts covering much of the near north side, which steered later clearance (St. Louis Magazine, https://www.stlmag.com/history/architecture/1947-city-planners-obsolete-blighted-districts-st-louis-downtown/).
- 1954-1960s: Mill Creek Valley, a 465-acre Black neighborhood south of this cluster, is cleared; thousands of Black residents are displaced, many north and west into areas like JeffVanderLou and The Ville (KSDK, https://www.ksdk.com/article/news/local/what-mill-creek-valley-st-louis-citypark-brickline-greenway/63-5ca18972-e138-427e-9721-f4bbdeb41b66; Colin Gordon interview, nextSTL, https://nextstl.com/?p=903).
- 1954-1976: Pruitt-Igoe public housing opens (first tenants November 1954) and is demolished from 1972 to 1976 (St. Louis Public Radio, https://black-white.stlpr.org/pruitt-igoe; KSDK, https://www.ksdk.com/article/features/vintage-ksdk-tearing-down-pruitt-igoe-apartments/63-bb34a931-f6e6-42a6-a03d-e222f60c8a61).
- 1950s-2000s: White flight and then Black middle-class flight from The Ville (BlackPast, above). Population loss by tract can be shown in NHGIS and in Colin Gordon's Mapping Decline maps (https://mappingdecline.lib.uiowa.edu/, URL UNCONFIRMED).
- 2016-2022: The city uses eminent domain to clear land in St. Louis Place for the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency's NGA West campus next to the Pruitt-Igoe site (KSDK piece above notes the adjacency). Dates and number of households: UNCONFIRMED in what I found.
- East St. Louis (if kept): 1917 massacre, more than 300 Black homes and businesses burned (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_St._Louis_massacre); population fell by more than three-fifths from 1950 to 2000 (Britannica, https://www.britannica.com/print/article/176786).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
5 for the six St. Louis tracts. Clearance, public housing siting, the closing of the neighborhood's hospital and later eminent domain are all dated and well documented, with maps and tract data.

## What makes it distinct
A neighborhood shaped by repeated rounds of public planning: displaced families from Mill Creek, the Pruitt-Igoe towers and their demolition, the closing of Homer G. Phillips, and a federal campus built on cleared blocks.

## Data that could show it (source, link, what it shows, years covered)
- NHGIS tract series, https://www.nhgis.org: population, race, vacancy, income, 1940-2020 (St. Louis was tracted early).
- Mapping Inequality, https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining: 1937 St. Louis HOLC grades.
- Mapping Decline (Colin Gordon), https://mappingdecline.lib.uiowa.edu/ (URL UNCONFIRMED): dated maps of race, deed restrictions, renewal areas, 1900s-2000.
- HUD Picture of Subsidized Households, https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/assthsg.html: public housing units by tract, 1977 on.
- St. Louis city vacant parcel and LRA (Land Reutilization Authority) data, https://www.stlouis-mo.gov/data/: vacancy and city-owned land, 2000s on.
- Missouri hospital licensing records and AHA guides: hospital beds in the area over time.
- USALEEP, https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nvss/usaleep/usaleep.html: tract life expectancy 2010-2015.
- HMDA, https://ffiec.cfpb.gov/: lending by tract, 1990 on.
