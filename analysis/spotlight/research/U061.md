# U061: Englewood and Fuller Park, Chicago (editor seed)

## Name and place

South Side of Chicago, Cook County, Illinois. The cluster runs north to south along the Dan Ryan Expressway:
- 17031680900, 17031681000, 17031681200, 17031681300: Englewood (community area 68), roughly 59th to 69th streets. Tract 6809 holds Kennedy-King College (commuter campus, no dorms; 2020 group quarters 11).
- 17031834600: north Englewood near 55th St.
- 17031835500, 17031835600: Fuller Park (community area 37), the narrow strip between the Dan Ryan and the rail embankment.
- 17031340600: Armour Square / Bronzeville edge. It contains the Wentworth Gardens public housing complex (344 units, opened 1947; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wentworth_Gardens) and the White Sox ballpark. 2020 population 1,151.

2020 census population of the eight tracts: 16,671. Group quarters are tiny (under 70 total).

## Rule-out flags

- Tract 3406 is largely one public housing complex (Wentworth Gardens), but it is about 7% of cluster population, so it does not drive the result. Consider dropping it so the cluster reads as "Englewood and Fuller Park."
- The cluster mixes two community areas with different stories (Fuller Park: highway and stockyards; Englewood: retail and housing collapse). Fine for one story, but name both.
- Story overlaps with Woodlawn (U063) and West Garfield Park (U065). Pick one Chicago story.

## History (dated bullets with sources)

- **About 1940: HOLC grades.** Our overlay of Mapping Inequality polygons (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining) on the 2020 tracts: all four Englewood tracts are 83% to 100% grade D (area D83); the 55th St. and Fuller Park tracts are 69% to 100% grade D (D72), with the rest industrial; tract 3406 is 52% D, 48% industrial.
- **1950s: Dan Ryan Expressway.** Built through Fuller Park in the 1950s, dividing it and displacing a third of its residents (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuller_Park,_Chicago). Opened 1962 (year from general knowledge; UNCONFIRMED here).
- **1960: Englewood peak.** 97,595 residents, 69% Black (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englewood,_Chicago).
- **1971: stockyards close.** The Union Stock Yards, next to Fuller Park, declined in the 1960s and closed in 1971 (Fuller Park Wikipedia page).
- **1978 onward: retail loss.** The 63rd and Halsted shopping district lost anchors including Sears after 1978 (Englewood Wikipedia page).
- **1969 to 2004: no new housing.** Fuller Park saw no new housing built and only 12 commercial permits; in the 1980s it got the fewest bank home-improvement loans in Chicago (Fuller Park Wikipedia page).
- **1960 to 2020: population loss.** Englewood fell from 97,595 to 24,369; Fuller Park from 17,174 (1950) to 2,567 (both Wikipedia pages, from census).
- **2013: hardship.** Fuller Park had the highest hardship score among Chicago community areas, with 40% unemployment (Fuller Park Wikipedia page). Englewood school closings in 2013 and 2018: UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5)

5. One of the most studied neighborhoods in the country, with HOLC, highway, stockyard, retail and population records all dated.

## What makes it distinct

Fuller Park shows a highway and a dying stockyard cutting one small neighborhood apart, while Englewood shows a 1960s shopping hub emptying out. As a national story it is the most familiar of the three Chicago seeds.
