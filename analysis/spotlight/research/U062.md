# U062: Jordan Downs, Watts, Los Angeles, CA (editor seed: Watts)

## Name and place
Single tract 06037242100 (Census Tract 2421), city of Los Angeles, Watts, about 0.2 square miles, population about 2,167. The tract box runs from about 97th Street to 103rd Street and from Grape Street to Alameda Street (TIGERweb extent: lat 33.9431 to 33.9483, lon -118.2368 to -118.2275). That matches the boundaries Wikipedia gives for the Jordan Downs public housing project: Grape Street (west), 97th Street (north), Alameda Street (east), 103rd Street (south) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Downs). The Census geocoder places 9800 Grape St in tract 06037242100, and a New Markets Tax Credit project page lists the Jordan Downs Plaza address at tract 6037242100 (https://nmtccoalition.org/project/jordan-downs-redevelopment/). Residents call it "Jordan Downs" or "the Downs."

## Rule-out flags
- **Single public housing complex.** This tract is essentially Jordan Downs, a roughly 700-unit Housing Authority of the City of Los Angeles (HACLA) complex (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Downs). Its income and health numbers mostly reflect who public housing admits, not the wider neighborhood. That is a reason to rule out this tract as drawn.
- **Redevelopment in progress.** A roughly $1 billion rebuild is replacing the complex and expanding it from about 700 to about 1,500 units, with a master plan approved in 2013 (https://kcrw.com/news/shows/greater-la/housing-broad-museum-worker-safety/jordan-downs-watts; https://www.clpha.org/news/2023/inside-redevelopment-transforming-watts-los-angeles-housing-authority-city-los-angeles). Recent census years capture a population in transition (mixed-income phases, relocations), which breaks the time series.
- Boundary stability 0.588 against 1990 (judgment call).
- **Recommendation:** Watts has the strongest compounding story of the three LA candidates (see "What makes it distinct"). Rebuild the Watts candidate from several adjacent Watts tracts, not this one alone, and report Jordan Downs as one part of it.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1926: Watts residents voted 1,338 to 535 to join Los Angeles; annexation took effect June 1, 1926 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts,_Los_Angeles).
- 1939: HOLC issued its Los Angeles map; race was a key factor in grades, and PBS SoCal names Watts among mixed areas that lost private investment (https://www.laalmanac.com/history/hi727.php; https://pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/segregation-in-the-city-of-angels-a-1939-map-of-housing-inequality-in-la). The grade of the specific Watts polygon is UNCONFIRMED; check Mapping Inequality.
- 1940s: Racial covenants confined Black residents mostly to the Central Avenue district and Watts; Watts became mostly Black in the 1940s (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts,_Los_Angeles).
- May 1944: Jordan Downs opened as housing for war workers; it opened as public housing in 1955 and was close to all-Black by the mid-1960s (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Downs).
- August 11, 1965: The Watts uprising began; 34 deaths, 1,032 injuries, 3,438 arrests and more than $40 million in property damage (https://csudh.edu/watts/history). The McCone Commission report, December 2, 1965, pointed to unemployment, poor schools and poor living conditions (same source). Jordan Downs was one of the flashpoints (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Downs).
- 1975 to 1986: Eight major plants in South LA closed, leaving 12,000 people out of work, including GM South Gate (1982), Bethlehem Steel (1982), Goodyear and Firestone (https://scalar.sparcinla.org/great-wall-institute/labor-and-union-organizing; https://memorywork.irle.ucla.edu/archives/4956; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Gate_Assembly). Exact closing years for Goodyear and Firestone: UNCONFIRMED.
- 1972 to 1993: The Century Freeway (I-105) was held up by the Keith v. Volpe suit (filed 1972); a 1979 consent decree created a program to replace 4,200 homes lost to construction (https://clearinghouse.net/case/43962; https://scalar.usc.edu/works/century105-freeway-history/introduction). The freeway runs just south of Watts; opening in 1993 is UNCONFIRMED.
- Mid-1980s: Jordan Downs was known as a Crips stronghold (Grape Street) during the crack era (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Downs).
- April 26, 1992: Watts gangs signed the "Watts truce" just before the 1992 unrest; after 1992 the shift from Black to Latino majority sped up. In 2000 Watts was 61.6% Latino and 37.1% Black (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts,_Los_Angeles).
- 2013 to now: Redevelopment master plan approved; phases opening since about 2019 (https://urbanize.la/tags/jordan-downs). Phase dates UNCONFIRMED.

## What makes it distinct
Watts packs nearly every urban cause on the list into one square mile, in a dated order: covenants and HOLC, wartime public housing, 1965, plant closures, a freeway, the crack era, 1992, and a Black-to-Latino shift.

## LA comparison (Watts vs. Compton U067 vs. U009)
- **Watts (U062):** strongest documentable compounding story. Covenants, HOLC, 1944/1955 public housing, 1965, South LA plant closures, I-105, crack era, 1992 and the population shift are all dated and sourced above. Problem: the tract is Jordan Downs only.
- **Compton (U067):** a distinct story (a white suburb that became a Black middle-class suburb, then lost that middle class, then became mostly Latino), but the tract is only at the 17th income and 15th health percentile and is a piece of a 1990 tract.
- **U009:** Skid Row. A documented policy story (the 1976 containment plan), but a services district, not a neighborhood that declined. Rule out.
- **Pick:** Watts, rebuilt from several Watts tracts so that no single complex drives the numbers.
