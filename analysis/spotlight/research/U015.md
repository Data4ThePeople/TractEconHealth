## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1890s: White families moved from Russell to what became Old Louisville and the East End, and Black families moved in. By the 1940s Russell was called "Louisville's Harlem." (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell,_Louisville)
- 1937 to 1938: HOLC graded Louisville's neighborhoods. Parkland and Park DuValle fell in C and D zones, and Shawnee and Chickasaw were first graded B, per a secondary source (https://loupolitical.org/2021/10/01/how-racism-is-built-into-louisvilles-infrastructure). The grades for these specific tracts are UNCONFIRMED; check Mapping Inequality (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining).
- 1957: Louisville voters approved a $5 million bond issue to begin urban renewal (Louisville Magazine timeline, https://loumag.epubxp.com/i/111400/41/). In the 1960s, urban renewal razed Russell's business districts, including the Walnut Street Black business and entertainment district, and public housing was built (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell,_Louisville; https://louisville.edu/cepm/westlou/west-end-archive-description).
- 1960s: I-264 (the Shawnee Expressway) was built along the western West End, dividing Shawnee and Chickasaw from Parkland and Park DuValle (https://history.ky.gov/markers/highland-park). Exact construction year UNCONFIRMED.
- May 27 to 29, 1968: Unrest centered at 28th and Greenwood in Parkland; 472 arrests, 2 deaths, about $200,000 in damage. Most white business owners and residents left the West End afterward (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Louisville_riots). Louisville Public Media reports about 15,000 white residents left the West End in the 1960s (https://lpm.org/news/2021-09-24/louisville-is-more-diverse-than-ever-but-remains-largely-segregated).
- 1975: Court-ordered busing in Jefferson County. UNCONFIRMED in this pass (no source pulled).
- 2008 onward: Foreclosure wave and vacant properties. UNCONFIRMED for these tracts; Louisville Metro demolitions data FY2003 to FY2020 exists (https://services1.arcgis.com/79kfd2K6fskCAkyg/arcgis/rest/services/Louisville_Metro_KY_Jefferson_County_Demolitions_Demolished_from_FY2003_to_FY2020/FeatureServer).
- 2010s: Beecher Terrace demolished and rebuilt as mixed-income housing under a HUD Choice Neighborhoods grant. Dates and grant amount UNCONFIRMED in this pass.

## Story strength (1-5)
5. Redlining, urban renewal of Walnut Street, the 1968 unrest, white flight, public housing and later disinvestment are each dated and each has a public data series.

## What makes it distinct
The textbook urban sequence in one Southern border city: a thriving Black business district erased by urban renewal, then the 1968 unrest and white flight that turned the whole West End into a segregated, disinvested area.
