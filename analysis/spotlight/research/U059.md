# U059: Gifford, Indian River County, FL

## Name and place
Indian River County tract 503.03 (1.1 sq mi, ZIP 32967), in the Gifford CDP just north of Vero Beach (Census geocoder). Gifford is a historically Black community that residents and local media call "Gifford." U.S. 1 runs through it. Population 2,815 (ACS); 3,423 in 2020, 85% Black.

## Rule-out flags
- **Boundary:** a piece of a larger 2010 tract, so no tract match to 1990. Gifford CDP has census data in 2000 (and likely earlier; 1990 CDP status UNCONFIRMED), which could serve as the long series.
- No institution: group quarters 10.
- The 2020 Gifford CDP lost the area east of U.S. 1, so CDP counts are not comparable 2010 to 2020 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifford,_Florida)).

## History
- **1880s-1893:** Settled by Black families; Black laborers on Flagler's Florida East Coast Railway joined in 1893 ([Wikipedia, citing Indian River County Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifford,_Florida)).
- **1900s:** Residents worked in citrus groves and fields owned by white growers, in domestic service and in lumber ([Florida Historical Marker](https://markers.flheritage.com/home/details/1112)).
- **1901-1969:** A school for Black children began in 1901 and became Gifford High School, which graduated its last class in 1969 at integration; Joe Idlette Jr. sued the school district in 1964 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifford,_Florida); [Indian River Guardian](https://indianriverguardian.com/2019/01/21/why-is-martin-luther-king-jr-s-birthday-celebrated-here-in-indian-river-county/)).
- **1970s:** A CBS report exposed brown, unsafe tap water from a small nonprofit system; federal officials pushed the county to take over, and the county hooked Gifford to its water and sewer in the late 1970s ([TCPalm via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/feared-life-gifford-thank-whistleblower-100033855.html); [Indian River Guardian, 2014](https://indianriverguardian.com/2014/03/23/gifford-neighborhood-plan-update-still-waiting-to-be-updated/)). Air date UNCONFIRMED.
- **1998-2010s:** Indian River County went from 20 citrus packinghouses in 1998 to about 8, with hundreds of jobs lost, as canker and greening spread ([The Packer](https://www.thepacker.com/markets/fruit/growers-feel-squeeze-countys-citrus-industry-drops); [Indian River Guardian, 2015](https://indianriverguardian.com/2015/01/07/is-there-a-future-for-indian-river-citrus/)).
- **1999-2002:** County formed a task force on Gifford's problems (1999) and adopted the Gifford Neighborhood Plan (2002) ([Indian River Guardian, 2014](https://indianriverguardian.com/2014/03/23/gifford-neighborhood-plan-update-still-waiting-to-be-updated/)).
- **October 31, 2011:** A storm flooded homes waist-deep; one resident's flood insurance went from $600 to $1,500 a year. In 2013 average income was cited at $23,000 in Gifford against $47,000 countywide ([Indian River Guardian, 2014](https://indianriverguardian.com/2014/03/23/gifford-neighborhood-plan-update-still-waiting-to-be-updated/)).
- **2017:** Hurricane Irma destroyed up to half the Indian River citrus crop ([Vero News](https://vb32963online.com/STORIES%202017/SEPTEMBER%202017/VB32963_Irma_Destroyed_Up_To_Half_Of_Indian_River_Citrus_Issue039_092817.html)).

## Story strength
3. Several dated causes with records (segregated schools, unsafe water, citrus collapse, flooding), but the tract cannot be traced to 1990 and the citrus link to Gifford's jobs is not yet documented directly.

## What makes it distinct
A Black citrus-labor town beside a wealthy beach city that waited until the 1970s for safe tap water and then watched the groves it worked in die.
