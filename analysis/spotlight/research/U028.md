# U028: Segundo Barrio and the Chamizal neighborhood, South-Central El Paso, Texas

## Name and place
- Eight tracts in the city of El Paso, 2020 population about 19,900, 89% to 97% Hispanic in every tract (2020 PL 94-171, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl).
- Tracts 48141001800, 001900, 002000, 002100 sit between downtown and the border, roughly -106.49 to -106.47 longitude (TIGERweb, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). This is El Segundo Barrio, bounded by Paisano Drive, the Border Highway, Cotton Street and South Mesa Street (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Segundo_Barrio). Tract 001800, the westernmost, may include part of Chihuahuita (UNCONFIRMED).
- Tracts 002800, 002900, 003000 sit east of Cotton Street around -106.46 to -106.44: the Chamizal neighborhood, which the city calls one of its oldest and poorest (City of El Paso, Chamizal Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy, https://www.elpasotexas.gov/assets/Documents/CoEP/Community-Development/Neighborhood-Associations/Neighborhood-Revitalization/CHAMIZAL-NRS.pdf).
- Tract 003602, further east near -106.41, 31.755: neighborhood name UNCONFIRMED (possibly near Ascarate or the lower Chamizal area). Consider dropping it.

## Rule-out flags
- Group quarters: tract 002100 has 352 people in "other noninstitutional" group quarters (17% of the tract), likely a homeless shelter or migrant shelter (identity UNCONFIRMED). Tract 003602 has 154 (47 juvenile, 107 other institutional). Not big enough to drive an 8-tract cluster, but note them.
- Tract boundaries mostly stable since 1990 (stability 0.869); tract data goes back to 1970 per the batch file.
- Strong story, no single institution.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1880s on: Segundo Barrio is the first stop for thousands of families arriving from Mexico; by the 1930s it is overcrowded with tenements ("presidios") (Wikipedia, El Segundo Barrio, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Segundo_Barrio).
- 1916: City demolition squads enter the barrio to "clean up South El Paso" (UTEP, Politics of Preservation, https://utep.edu/liberalarts/public-and-oral-history/_Files/docs/Politics_of_Preservation_1.pdf).
- 1946: Paisano Drive is widened through the neighborhood (National Register nomination, Segundo Barrio Historic District, https://atlas.thc.texas.gov/NR/pdfs/100006994/100006994.pdf; UTEP paper above).
- Late 1950s: Interstate 10 cuts the Chamizal area off from neighborhoods to the north (community blog, https://deepinsideelpaso.blogspot.com/2011/04; secondary source, check against TxDOT records).
- 1963-1968: The Chamizal Convention (signed August 29, 1963) shifts the border; nearly 5,600 South El Paso residents of neighborhoods such as Rio Linda and Cordova Gardens are moved out, and the new river channel opens in 1968 (National Park Service, https://www.nps.gov/cham/learn/historyculture/implementation.htm).
- 1990-2019: El Paso apparel jobs fall from 16,150 to 1,130, a 92% drop, after NAFTA and automation (Texas Comptroller, https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/economic-data/manufacturing/2020/rio-grande.php). Farah had once employed 7,000 in El Paso and was down to just over 700 by 1990 (Texas Observer, https://www.texasobserver.org/archive_page/ustxtxb_obs_1990_10_12_50_00008-00000_000pdf18306/); Levi Strauss had six plants and about 6,000 workers around 1992, and its last El Paso sewing plant (780 workers) was set to close by 2002 (Dallas Fed via FRASER, https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/frbdal_bus/frbdal_bus_2002_iss1.pdf).
- 2000: Chamizal poverty rate 58.7%, versus 23.8% for the metro (San Francisco Fed case study, https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20121019134331/http:/www.frbsf.org/cpreport/docs/el_%20paso_tx.pdf).
- 2006 to present: Residents push back on downtown redevelopment and eminent domain proposals (City revitalization summary, https://elpasotexas.gov/assets/Documents/CoEP/Community-Development/Neighborhood-Associations/Neighborhood-Revitalization/SegundoBarrio_NeighRevitalization-SPANISH.pdf; Texas Public Radio, https://www.tpr.org/show/fronteras/2018-11-30/fronteras-extra-el-pasos-segundo-barrio).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. Many dated, compounding causes (tenement crowding, 1916 demolitions, Paisano 1946, I-10, the Chamizal move, then the collapse of garment jobs), each with a record or data series.

## What makes it distinct
The immigrant front door of the United States on the Mexican border, cut by roads and an international boundary change, then hit by the loss of the garment jobs its residents held.
