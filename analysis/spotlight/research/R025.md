# R025: Meadville and Bude, Franklin County, MS

## Name and place
One tract, 28037950101. It touches the towns of Meadville (the county seat, 448 people in 2020) and Bude (780 people) in southwest Mississippi, on the Homochitto River near the Homochitto National Forest (Census TIGERweb; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadville,_Mississippi; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bude,_Mississippi). The 2020 census counted 1,877 people (54% white, 44% Black); the batch file gives 2,302. This is not in the Delta.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1900: The county had 13,678 people, about half Black. Only 19% of Black farmers owned their land (https://mississippiencyclopedia.org/entries/franklin-county/).
- 1910 to 2020: Population fell by about half (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_County,_Mississippi). It was 7,675 in 2020.
- 1912: Bude was founded (Bude article, above).
- 1930: About 87% of Black farmers were tenants (Mississippi Encyclopedia, above).
- 1960: The county had 9,286 people. Furniture manufacturing now employed more people than farming, and the county had 31 producing oil wells (Mississippi Encyclopedia, above).
- May 1964: Klan members kidnapped and murdered Henry Dee and Charles Moore of Meadville. James Ford Seale was convicted in 2007, and the county settled the families' civil suit in 2010 (Mississippi Encyclopedia; Wikipedia, above).
- Today: American Industrial Transport (AITX) runs a large railcar maintenance shop in Bude (Bude article, above). The decline of furniture work and timber after 1960 is UNCONFIRMED.

## What makes it distinct
It is a hill-country timber and furniture county rather than a cotton plantation county, with a documented history of Klan violence.
