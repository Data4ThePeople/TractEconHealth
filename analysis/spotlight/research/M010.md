# M010: Eastern Navajo Nation chapters, New Mexico (Tohatchi, Navajo/Red Lake, Crownpoint area, Naschitti, Nageezi area and others), McKinley, San Juan and Sandoval counties, NM

## Name and place
Eight tracts across three counties, population about 20,317. The batch labels this "Gallup," but the city of Gallup is not in the cluster. Tract internal points (Census geocoder): 9437.01 Tohatchi Chapter; 9438.01 Red Lake Chapter (the town of Navajo, NM); 9438.03 Fort Defiance Chapter area (NM side); 9431 Naschitti Chapter; 9440 Crownpoint CCD (Eastern Navajo Agency checkerboard, off the main reservation); 9460.03 Red Rock CCD (south of Crownpoint; likely Thoreau and Prewitt area, UNCONFIRMED); 9409 Sandoval County, Cuba CCD (likely Torreon, Ojo Encino and Counselor chapters, UNCONFIRMED); 9432.01 Napi Headquarters CCD, San Juan County (Navajo Agricultural Products Industry area and Nageezi, UNCONFIRMED). The people are Diné (Navajo).

## Rule-out flags
- **Scattered cluster.** The tracts run from the Arizona line to Sandoval County. Two distinct stories sit here: the Navajo, NM timber mill (west) and the uranium belt and checkerboard (east). Consider splitting.
- Boundaries: stability 0.342, shifted since 1990; tracts start in 1990.
- Name label "Gallup" is misleading.
- No prison, college or base found. Navajo Technical University is in Crownpoint (tract 9440 or nearby); its size is unlikely to drive the numbers but check (UNCONFIRMED).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1944 to 1986: Uranium mining on and near Navajo lands; 3,000 to 5,000 Navajo miners; a 1962 Public Health Service report linked uranium mining to cancer (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium_mining_and_the_Navajo_people).
- 1962 to 1994/1995: Navajo Forest Products Industries ran a sawmill at Navajo, NM; it closed in 1994 per High Country News or 1995 per EPA, amid charges of overcutting and mismanagement, owing more than $16 million (https://www.hcn.org/issues/issue-42/navajo-nation-bails-out-timber-mill/; https://response.epa.gov/NFPI). The 103-acre site still needs cleanup (https://navajotimes.com/biz/costs-of-cleaning-up-many-issues-plague-former-navajo-forest-products-industry-site/).
- 1968 to 1982: Northeast Church Rock mine operated (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Rock_uranium_mill_spill).
- July 16, 1979: The United Nuclear Church Rock tailings dam failed, releasing about 93 to 94 million gallons of acidic, radioactive water into the Puerco River (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Rock_uranium_mill_spill; https://newmexicohistory.org/?p=2072). Church Rock is west of this cluster; the spill is context, not in the tracts.
- 1990: The Radiation Exposure Compensation Act passed (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium_mining_and_the_Navajo_people).
- 2005: The Navajo Nation banned uranium mining and processing (same source).
- 2008 to now: EPA cleanup plans for 523 abandoned uranium mines; the Mariano Lake Mine (Eastern Agency) was listed for cleanup in EPA's 2011 report; a 2017 settlement funds cleanup of 94 mines (same source).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. Uranium mining, the timber mill closure and the checkerboard land pattern are each dated and backed by EPA, court and census records, but the cluster is scattered and tract data start in 1990.

## What makes it distinct
The uranium legacy: decades of mining for the nation's weapons and power plants left hundreds of open mines near homes, and a tribal sawmill that was supposed to be the alternative closed in the 1990s.
