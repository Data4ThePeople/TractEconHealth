# R010: Many and Fisher, Sabine Parish, Louisiana

## Name and place
- One tract, 22085000500 (Census Tract 5, Sabine Parish). About 27 square miles of land, 2020 population 3,174.
- The tract's bounding box takes in the parish seat of Many (town), the old company sawmill village of Fisher, and Fort Jesup CDP (TIGERweb place layers, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). The population-weighted centroid (31.546, -93.434) sits in and around Many.
- 2020 census: 1,488 of 3,174 residents (47%) were Black alone, far above the parish share. This looks like the Black side of Many plus nearby rural land. Name residents use for the specific streets: UNCONFIRMED.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1899-1901: Louisiana Long Leaf Lumber Company ("4L") builds Fisher as a company sawmill town (Wikipedia, Fisher, Louisiana, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher,_Louisiana; National Register nomination, https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/9b1bc3c8-fd12-4d49-bf59-eb3654540f6a).
- 1905-1920s: Sabine Parish is a longleaf pine sawmill region. Loring, near Many, had about 1,000 people in 1905; the Peason mill ran double shifts in the mid-1920s (All Things Sabine, https://allthingssabine.com/posts/2017-07-08-peavy-wilson-lumber-company-of-peason-louisiana/). Neighboring cutover mill towns such as Fullerton (Vernon Parish) closed by 1927 once the timber was gone (64 Parishes, https://64parishes.org/lumber-king).
- 1928-1934: A short oil and gas boom, which declined around 1934 (parish history summaries; specific source UNCONFIRMED).
- 1963-1969: Land buying for the Toledo Bend Reservoir begins in May 1963; the reservoir fills from 1966 and the dam's power plant runs from 1969. Riverbank communities were flooded, including Robinson Bend, a mostly Black settlement, and members of the Choctaw-Apache Tribe were displaced (Texas Highways, https://texashighways.com/culture/history/the-lost-underwater-towns-of-texas/; 64 Parishes, https://64parishes.org/?p=60067; Toledo Bend article, https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Toledo_Bend_Dam).
- 1966: The Fisher mill is sold to Boise Cascade, ending the company-town era; rail service to Fisher ends in 1968 (Wikipedia, Fisher, Louisiana, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher,_Louisiana).
- 2010-2024: Parish population falls from 24,233 (2010 census) to about 22,000 (ACS); figures from a secondary site, check against Census (https://www.populationreview.org/county/sabine-parish-la).
- Later mill closures, timber job counts and the fate of the Fisher mill after Boise Cascade: UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
2. The timber company-town arc and the Toledo Bend displacement are documented, but the link from those events to this specific Black neighborhood of Many is not yet shown, and the job data for later decades is thin.

## What makes it distinct
A cutover longleaf pine parish where a state-built reservoir (financed without federal money) flooded riverbank farms in the 1960s, layered on a company-town economy that ended when the mill was sold.
