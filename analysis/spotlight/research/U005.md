# U005: South Memphis / Soulsville, Memphis, TN

## Name and place
- Shelby County, TN. 8 tracts, pop 12,622. Centroid 35.1113, -90.0424.
- Tract checks with the Census geocoder (https://geocoding.geo.census.gov):
  - 011600 holds the Stax Museum (926 E McLemore Ave) and LeMoyne-Owen College (807 Walker Ave). That is the heart of "Soulsville USA."
  - 005000 holds Mason Temple (930 Mason St).
  - 005500 holds 1500 S Third St. 005900 holds 700 E Trigg Ave. 011500 holds 1200 Kerr Ave.
  - 004500 is the northernmost tract, at 35.129, -90.039. It sits near the Vance Avenue area, where Foote Homes stood. The exact match is UNCONFIRMED. The geocoder put 600 Vance Ave in tract 011401, outside the cluster.
- Residents and local media say "South Memphis," "Soulsville" and "38106/38126."
- Institutions: LeMoyne-Owen College is in 011600, but the 2020 count shows only 35 people in college housing there (PL 94-171 table P5). No prison. The college does not drive the numbers.

## Rule-out flags
- None from boundaries (stability 0.997).
- Shares Memphis-wide causes with U011. Its civil-rights and music history is a different story.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1940: Foote Homes public housing is built. The site later stood abandoned, about 30 acres of blight (https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/environment/remediation/documents/successstories/west/rem_success_foote-homes.pdf). Planners call Foote and Cleaborn the largest concentration of public housing in Memphis (https://pauldavidoff.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Prelim-Vance-Plan-1.pdf).
- About 1937: Memphis is HOLC-mapped. Most Black neighborhoods were graded D (https://dailymemphian.com/article/35310). South Memphis's grades are UNCONFIRMED.
- 1950s-1960s: Streetcar removal and suburban flight start the business decline (https://www.highgroundnews.com/features/the-soul-of-memphis-on-the-ground.aspx).
- April 3, 1968: Dr. King gives his last speech at Mason Temple (tract 005000), during the sanitation strike. This is widely documented, but no source was pulled in this session. UNCONFIRMED here.
- 1975: Stax Records goes bankrupt and closes. The building was later demolished. The 1989 date is UNCONFIRMED (https://www.highgroundnews.com/features/the-soul-of-memphis-on-the-ground.aspx).
- 1980 to 2000: Soulsville lost 26% of its population from 1980 to 1990 and 34% from 1990 to 2000, as cited by High Ground News (https://www.highgroundnews.com/features/the-soul-of-memphis-on-the-ground.aspx). Recheck these figures against NHGIS.
- 1980s and 1990s: The Memphis Housing Authority was twice on HUD's troubled-agency list. In the 1980s, "revitalization" cleared older buildings (https://pauldavidoff.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Prelim-Vance-Plan-1.pdf).
- 2016-2018: Foote Homes, the last traditional public housing project in Memphis, is demolished for the South City mixed-income project (https://dailymemphian.com/article/362/Foote-Homes-to-South-City-continues-story-of-change).

## What makes it distinct
This is where Memphis's music and civil-rights history (Stax, Mason Temple) sits next to its largest public-housing concentration. Distinct from North Memphis, but its causes are closer to a standard urban-renewal story. U011 is the more distinct Memphis pick.
