# U051: Indianapolis northeast side, 30th to 38th Street corridor (Forest Manor, Avondale Meadows, Arlington Woods, north Martindale-Brightwood)

## Name and place
Eight Indianapolis tracts in Marion County between about 30th and 42nd streets, from near Keystone Avenue east toward Arlington Avenue. From tract centroids, this takes in Forest Manor (30th to 38th, Hawthorne Lane to Parker Avenue), the Avondale Meadows area near 38th Street, Arlington Woods, and the north edge of Martindale-Brightwood ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis, Forest Manor](https://indyencyclopedia.org/forest-manor/); [Avondale Meadows](https://indyencyclopedia.org/avondale-meadows)). Neighborhood assignment by tract is approximate and should be checked against the city's neighborhood map. Population 19,948.

## Rule-out flags
- No prison, college or base (group quarters near zero in all eight tracts, 2020 PL file).
- Hawthorne Place, a 176-unit public housing complex, opened in Forest Manor in 1968 ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/forest-manor/)). It is too small to drive the numbers for eight tracts by itself.
- oldest_tract_year is 1980 even though Indianapolis was tracted earlier. Check why; it may limit the long series.

## History
- **1921:** Forest Manor opened as a middle-class streetcar-and-auto suburb ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/forest-manor/)).
- **1937:** HOLC graded Indianapolis neighborhoods. The grades for these areas are UNCONFIRMED; check Mapping Inequality.
- **1947-1956:** The Meadows was built, with about 1,236 apartments in 1947 and 1953 and the city's first suburban shopping center in 1956 ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/avondale-meadows)).
- **1960s:** Black families began buying in Forest Manor and many white residents left within a few years. Census data show the Meadows area's racial makeup changed sharply from 1950 to 1980 as white families moved to neighboring counties ([Forest Manor](https://indyencyclopedia.org/forest-manor/); [Avondale Meadows](https://indyencyclopedia.org/avondale-meadows)). Interstates I-65 and I-70, built from the 1960s into the 1970s, cut through Martindale-Brightwood and displaced residents ([Polis Center](https://polis.indianapolis.iu.edu/?p=393)).
- **1970:** Unigov merged the city and county governments but left the township school districts separate. Indianapolis now has 11 school districts in one city ([Chalkbeat, 2016](https://www.chalkbeat.org/indiana/2016/8/3/21098810/how-racial-bias-helped-turn-indianapolis-into-one-city-with-11-school-districts/)).
- **1981-2016:** Under a federal court order, IPS bused Black students one way to suburban township schools. By the end, a larger share of IPS schools were segregated than before busing began ([Chalkbeat, 2016](https://www.chalkbeat.org/indiana/2016/6/30/21100652/the-end-of-busing-in-indianapolis-35-years-later-a-more-segregated-school-system-calls-it-quits/)). The Polis Center ties busing to the decline of neighborhood schools in Martindale-Brightwood ([Polis Center](https://polis.indianapolis.iu.edu/?p=393)).
- **1980s:** The Meadows declined into high crime and neglect and became known as one of the most dangerous areas in the Midwest. A Cub Foods grocery opened in 1995 and closed in 1997 ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/avondale-meadows)).
- **2009:** Forest Manor Middle School closed as enrollment fell ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/forest-manor/)). Redevelopment at Avondale Meadows began after 2009, and homicides there fell sharply in 2019.
- **2008:** Foreclosure wave. Local foreclosure counts UNCONFIRMED; HMDA and county records can show them.

## Story strength
3. White flight, school-district lines, the interstates and crime are well documented. Plant closures and HOLC grades are not yet confirmed for these tracts, and the cluster blends several neighborhoods with different histories.

## What makes it distinct
A postwar suburb inside the city limits fell behind when the city merged with the county but kept its school districts separate. The story is about where the lines were drawn, and it does not need a factory closure to explain the decline.
