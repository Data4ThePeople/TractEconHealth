# U022: Grier Heights, Charlotte, NC

## Name and place
Single tract 37119002300 (Mecklenburg County), 0.7 square miles, population about 3,520, southeast of Uptown off Monroe Road and Randolph Road. An OpenStreetMap reverse lookup of the centroid lands in Grier Heights. Residents and media call it Grier Heights (historically "Grier Town").

## Rule-out flags
- None found. No prison, college or base. Public housing role UNCONFIRMED.
- Tract stable since 1970 (stability 1.0).
- Gentrification is underway (https://www.axios.com/local/charlotte/2017/06/29/grier-heights-nearing-tipping-point-will-another-black-community-vanish-95774), so current numbers may shift fast.
- Overlaps in theme with U054 (also Charlotte). Pick one.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- Late 1800s: Founded as a settlement of freed Black families outside the city limits (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grier_Heights).
- 1930s: Developed further by Black businessman Arthur S. Grier (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grier_Heights; oral history https://repository.charlotte.edu/islandora/object/uncc:3040).
- 1960 to 1977: Charlotte's urban renewal cleared the Black Brooklyn neighborhood; Grier Heights families were tied to Brooklyn through schools and businesses (https://brooklyn-oral-history.charlotte.edu/historic-sources/brooklyn-time-line/). Whether displaced Brooklyn residents moved to Grier Heights is UNCONFIRMED.
- Annexation into Charlotte: date UNCONFIRMED.
- 1990s: Homes turned to rentals, incomes fell and drug crime rose; police made it a site of a full community policing effort after a 1994 ranking of inner-city neighborhoods (CMPD study, https://live-cpop.ws.asu.edu/sites/default/files/library/awards/goldstein/1999/99-05.pdf).
- 2008 to 2013: A church founded CrossRoads Corporation in 2008; it tore down 23 run-down duplexes and began mixed-income housing in 2013 (https://self-help.org/updates/sh-blog/blog/2016/08/02/making-a-difference-together-a-church-a-city-and-a-community-developer). Violent crime 2011 to 2013 was reported at five times the city average (same source).
- 2017: Rising prices around the neighborhood raised fears of displacement (Axios link above).

## What makes it distinct
A Black town that grew outside a fast-growing Sun Belt city, lost its owner base to rentals and drugs, and now sits in the path of one of the hottest housing markets in the South.
