# U030: East and South Wheeling, Benwood, and Bellaire (Upper Ohio Valley steel towns)

## Name and place
An urban cluster on both banks of the Ohio River. In Wheeling, WV (Ohio County): tracts 4 and 7 (East Wheeling and the north end of downtown), 6 and 27 (downtown and Centre Market area), and 26 (South Wheeling). Benwood city, Marshall County, WV: tract 205. Bellaire village, Belmont County, Ohio: tracts 116 and 117. Neighborhood names come from tract centroids and are approximate. Population 8,582.

## Rule-out flags
- No single institution drives the numbers. Tract 27 (downtown) has 225 in group quarters, 71 of them in correctional facilities (likely a county holding facility; check). Tract 7 has 29 in juvenile facilities. Both are small.
- Two states and three counties, joined by the river. That is how the steel valley worked, so it may be a strength, but county series must be shown for both sides.
- Small population (8,582) for an urban candidate.

## History
- **1930:** Wheeling's population peaked at 61,659 ([WV Public Broadcasting via Weelunk](https://weelunk.com/?p=31098)). Bellaire peaked earlier, at 15,061 in 1920 ([Wikipedia, Bellaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellaire,_Ohio)).
- **1960s:** Building the Wheeling Tunnel, I-70, I-470 and the Route 2 changes "dramatically altered" Wheeling. The highway work cleared the core of the city's Black business district in East Wheeling, including Wayman A.M.E. Church ([Archiving Wheeling](https://www.archivingwheeling.org/blog/right-of-way); [Weelunk oral history](https://weelunk.com/history-words-memories-black-community/)). Exact demolition years UNCONFIRMED.
- **July 1983:** Wheeling-Pittsburgh's Benwood Works closed for good, citing weak markets and imports ([e-WV](https://www.wvencyclopedia.org/entries/1179); [abandonedonline](https://abandonedonline.net/location/wheeling-steel-benwood-works)). One source says 1984.
- **1984:** Imperial Glass in Bellaire, once called the world's largest glass factory under one roof, closed on August 31 after a bankruptcy filing ([Wikipedia, Bellaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellaire,_Ohio)).
- **1985 and 2000:** Wheeling-Pittsburgh Steel bankruptcies. UNCONFIRMED in this search; the company's 1985 Chapter 11 is widely reported but not sourced here.
- **2012-2013:** RG Steel, the last owner of the Wheeling-Pittsburgh mills, went bankrupt, with mass layoffs from June 2012 ([e-WV](https://www.wvencyclopedia.org/entries/1179)).
- **September 2019:** Ohio Valley Medical Center in Wheeling and East Ohio Regional Hospital in Martins Ferry, OH both closed. About 1,100 workers lost jobs ([CBS Pittsburgh](https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/hospital-workers-lost-jobs-ohio-west-virginia); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/2-hospitals-to-close-in-ohio-west-virginia.html)). The OVMC building was torn down in October 2023.
- **2010s:** The opioid epidemic. Ohio County and Belmont County overdose rates are available from CDC WONDER; not pulled here.

## Story strength
4. Each step has a date and a data series: highways in the 1960s, steel and glass closures from 1983 to 2013, and a hospital closure in 2019. The cluster is small and split across two states.

## What makes it distinct
A river valley that lost its steel mills, its glass works and then its hospital. In East Wheeling, the interstate cut through the Black business district first. This is the only candidate in the batch where a hospital closure lands in the last chapter.
