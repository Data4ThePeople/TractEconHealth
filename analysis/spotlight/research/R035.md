# R035: Elizabethtown, Dublin and northern Bladen County, NC

## Name and place
Three rural tracts in Bladen County, NC, population about 8,543: 9504.01 (Elizabethtown, the county seat; 11.1 square miles), 9503.01 (Dublin area west of Elizabethtown; centroid 34.66, -78.72 matches Dublin, UNCONFIRMED) and 9502 (139 square miles north of Elizabethtown). The Smithfield Foods pork plant at Tar Heel (15855 NC Hwy 87 W) geocodes to tract 9503.02, which is next to but not in this cluster.

## Rule-out flags
- No prison found. Bladen Community College is in Dublin (commuter college, no dorms, UNCONFIRMED), which should not skew the numbers.
- Stability 0.806 since 1990: usable, but check tract 9503 and 9504 splits.
- Documentation of specific mill closures is thin so far.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- Mid-1900s: Farming (tobacco, cotton) and timber economy (general; county source needed, UNCONFIRMED).
- 1992: Smithfield opened the Tar Heel plant, later the largest pork plant in the world (opening year UNCONFIRMED; plant is the county's largest employer per https://bladenonline.com/?p=110432).
- 1990s to 2000s: Plants that operated in the county included Harriet and Henderson Yarns, Sportee, WestPoint Pepperell and Elizabethtown Manufacturing (https://bladenonline.com/?p=110432). Closing years and job counts UNCONFIRMED. Harriet and Henderson closed its Henderson plant to protect its Bladen operation (https://www.wral.com/news/local/story/105938/).
- 2002 to 2006: Bladen County lost about 1,000 jobs, per the NC Rural Center as cited by Bladen Online (https://bladenonline.com/?p=110432).
- 1994 to 2000s: North Carolina lost about 138,000 textile jobs after 1994 (statewide context, https://www.ncpedia.org/node/14260).
- 2017: State regulators found GenX and other PFAS from the Chemours Fayetteville Works plant on the Bladen/Cumberland line in private wells (NC DEQ: https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/key-issues/genx-investigation). Whether wells in these tracts were affected is UNCONFIRMED.

## What makes it distinct
Of the two textile candidates in this batch, this is the better fit: a stable-tract county seat that lost its mills and traded them for a giant pork plant and a chemical plant upstream.
