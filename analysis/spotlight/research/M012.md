# M012: South-central Hutchinson, Reno County, Kansas (just south of downtown)

## Name and place
- One tract, 20155000600 (Census Tract 6, Reno County), about 0.95 square miles of land. 2020 population 2,613 (2020 PL 94-171); candidate file pop 2,759.
- Bounding box about 38.047 to 38.061 N and -97.941 to -97.922 W: from the south edge of downtown Hutchinson south toward the Arkansas River (TIGERweb, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). Local neighborhood name: UNCONFIRMED.
- Housing: median year built 1938, about 1,506 units, about 784 renter households (ACS 2019-2023, https://api.census.gov/data/2023/acs/acs5).

## Rule-out flags
- Group quarters: 117 people in adult correctional facilities (likely the Reno County jail or a community corrections site, UNCONFIRMED) out of 2,613 (4.5%) (2020 PL 94-171 P5). The state's Hutchinson Correctional Facility is not in this tract (it would show far more inmates).
- Stable since 1990 (1.0).
- Thin documentation: Hutchinson as a whole grew from 1930 to 2010 (27,085 to 42,080), so the decline story is specific to this old core area and I found little written about it.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1887 on: Salt discovered in 1887; Carey (later the mine), Morton and Cargill operate salt plants in Hutchinson; Carey opens the city's salt mine in 1923 (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutchinson,_Kansas; Kansas Historical Society, https://www.kansashistory.gov/kansapedia/reno-county-kansas/15333). Salt plant locations relative to this tract: UNCONFIRMED.
- 1946: The Santa Fe Harvey House hotel (Bisonte) closes as rail passenger service declines (search summary of local history; source UNCONFIRMED).
- 1980: The Rock Island Railroad, which ran through Hutchinson, shuts down (Wikipedia, Hutchinson).
- January 17-18, 2001: Gas leaking from ONEOK's Yaggy storage field, 7 miles northwest, travels underground and surfaces through old brine wells; explosions destroy two downtown businesses and damage 26 others; two people die in a mobile home park the next day (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Hutchinson_gas_explosions; Kansas Geological Survey, https://www.kgs.ku.edu/Hydro/Hutch/Background/index.html). Whether the downtown blast sites are inside this tract: UNCONFIRMED (they are at or near its north edge).
- June 2007: Eaton moves 155 hydraulics assembly jobs from Hutchinson to Reynosa, Mexico, a year after city and state incentives (Wikipedia, Hutchinson).
- 2010-2020: City population falls 4.9% (Wikipedia, Hutchinson).

## What makes it distinct
An old core neighborhood in a salt and rail town where a century of brine wells left a path for a gas leak to explode under downtown in 2001.
