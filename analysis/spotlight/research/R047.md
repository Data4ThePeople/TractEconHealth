# R047: Belzoni, Humphreys County, MS

## Name and place
This is one tract, 28053950300, which covers the city of Belzoni. It is the county seat and calls itself the "Farm-Raised Catfish Capital of the World" (Census TIGERweb; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belzoni,_Mississippi). The 2020 census counted 3,654 people, 87% of them Black. The batch file gives 3,211.

## Rule-out flags
- Tract stability is 0.995, so it can be compared over time.
- No institution drives the numbers. The tract has 71 people in group quarters, including 21 in a correctional facility (2020 PL, table P5).
- Data check: the batch file's median household income ($45,238) and ACS 2020 to 2024 poverty (15.7%) are far better than the 41.6% poverty in 2008 to 2012 (history_long.csv). This may be ACS sampling noise in a small tract. Recheck it before choosing this place.
- Overlap: the cotton, sharecropping and outmigration history overlaps with Clarksdale (M016). The catfish chapter is the distinct part.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1930: The county had 24,729 people, 69% Black, and more than 5,600 farms, most worked by tenants and sharecroppers (https://mississippiencyclopedia.org/entries/humphreys-county/).
- 1940 to 2020: County population peaked at 26,257 in 1940 and was 7,785 in 2020, a 70% drop (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphreys_County,_Mississippi). Belzoni peaked at 4,142 in 1960 and had 1,938 people in 2020, a 53% drop (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belzoni,_Mississippi).
- 1953 to 1955: George Lee and Gus Courts organized an NAACP chapter in 1953. Lee was murdered in 1955 and no charges were filed. Courts was driven from the area (Mississippi Encyclopedia, above).
- 1960: Agriculture was more than half of all jobs. About 300 people worked in furniture and textile plants (Mississippi Encyclopedia, above).
- 1965: Voter registration was still so hard that after a month of daily work only seven people had registered (Margot Adler, https://www.crmvet.org/vet/adler.htm, cited at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belzoni,_Mississippi).
- 1976: The governor named Humphreys County the Farm-Raised Catfish Capital of the World. It stayed the top catfish county until changes in the industry in the early 2000s (Mississippi Encyclopedia, above). Catfish took the labor freed by cotton mechanization (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Delta_Pride_strike).
- 2000 to 2015: Imported basa and tra fillets took nearly a quarter of the U.S. fillet market within 18 months. Mississippi catfish acreage fell from 112,000 acres in 2001 to 37,000 in 2015, a 67% drop that cost the state more than 5,000 jobs (https://mississippiencyclopedia.org/entries/catfish-farming/).
- 2013: Patient's Choice Medical Center of Humphreys County closed (Sheps Center rural hospital closures list, https://www.shepscenter.unc.edu/programs-projects/rural-health/rural-hospital-closures/).

## Story strength (1-5)
4. A boom and bust in catfish sits on top of the cotton story, and catfish acreage and the hospital closure have dated public records. The current income reading needs a check.

## What makes it distinct
Catfish replaced cotton as the Delta's work, then imports cut it back, and the county lost its hospital. It is a second collapse layered on the first.
