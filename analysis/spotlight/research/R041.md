# R041: Ohio River "Bend" towns of Meigs County, Ohio, plus Ravenswood, West Virginia

## Name and place

Five rural tracts on both banks of the Ohio River (place names from OpenStreetMap features inside each TIGERweb tract polygon):
- 39105964300: Salem and Rutland townships, Meigs County, Ohio (Rutland village, many hamlets; 129 sq mi).
- 39105964400: Middleport and part of Pomeroy (county seat), Meigs County.
- 39105964500: Minersville area just upriver of Pomeroy (Sutton Township); likely includes Syracuse (UNCONFIRMED).
- 39105964600: Sutton and Letart area: Racine village and river hamlets.
- 54035963300: Ravenswood and North Ravenswood, Jackson County, West Virginia (ZIP 26164).

2020 census population: 18,402. Group quarters: two nursing homes (89 and 59 residents) and 5 in correctional quarters. No prison, college or base.

## Rule-out flags

- The cluster crosses a state line. Ravenswood's story (aluminum) differs from the Meigs coal-and-salt story. Fine if told as "both banks of the river," but state-level series will need to be split.
- Stable tracts back to 1990 only; earlier history must use place and county data.

## History (dated bullets with sources)

- **1819 to 1848: coal and salt.** Coal mining in Pomeroy began in 1819; salt was found in deep wells in 1848 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeroy,_Ohio).
- **1860 to 1880: peak.** Pomeroy peaked at 6,480 in 1860 and Meigs County at 32,325 in 1880 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeroy,_Ohio; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meigs_County,_Ohio).
- **1920s to 1940s: first decline.** Strikes in the 1920s and the Depression cut coal and salt; a World War II boom faded (Pomeroy Wikipedia page). Major floods hit in 1913 and 1937 (same page). Middleport peaked at 3,772 in 1920 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middleport,_Ohio).
- **1957: aluminum across the river.** Kaiser Aluminum opened a large plant six miles south of Ravenswood; Ravenswood grew from 1,175 (1950) to 3,410 (1960) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenswood,_West_Virginia).
- **1975: salt ends.** Excelsior Salt Co. closed when the EPA ordered it shut over smoke and brine pollution (Pomeroy Wikipedia page).
- **1993: mine water spill.** Emergency pumping at Southern Ohio Coal Co.'s Meigs No. 31 mine released millions of gallons of mine water, killing all life in Parker Run and the lower 16 miles of Leading Creek; mining had largely ended in the county by the 1990s (Meigs County Wikipedia page). Mine closure year and job counts: UNCONFIRMED.
- **2009 to 2015: last mine and smelter.** The Yellowbush mine near Racine opened in 2009 (expected 120 to 150 miners) and closed in 2015 (Meigs County Wikipedia page). Century Aluminum announced the permanent closure of the Ravenswood smelter in 2015; it had employed 650 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_Aluminum). A 1990 to 1992 lockout at Ravenswood: UNCONFIRMED.
- **Opioid deaths** in Meigs and Jackson counties: UNCONFIRMED.

3. A long, documented arc from the 1800s with dated closures, but the cross-river cluster splits the story, and local job counts are missing.

## What makes it distinct

A 150-year slide in two river towns built on coal and salt, with the last lift (a 1957 aluminum plant across the river) closing in 2015.
