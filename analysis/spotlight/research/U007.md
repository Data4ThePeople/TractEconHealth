# U007: Central, Kinsman, Fairfax and Hough, Cleveland's East Side, OH

## Name and place
Eight tracts, population about 18,130, on Cleveland's East Side between East 30th and East 90th. Tract centroids (TIGERweb) and the public housing in them place them in Central (1087.01 Cedar-Central, 1093.01, 1098.01 Outhwaite, 1984 King Kennedy), Kinsman (1148 Garden Valley/Heritage Homes, 1979), and Fairfax/Hough (1972, 1987). Neighborhood assignment for 1972, 1979 and 1987 is from coordinates only (UNCONFIRMED against the city's planning areas).

## Rule-out flags
- Public housing is very heavy but spread across four estates in four tracts: Cedar Central (604 units), Outhwaite (858), King Kennedy (1,016), Heritage Homes on the old Garden Valley site (207) (https://services.arcgis.com/VTyQ9soqVukalItT/arcgis/rest/services/Public_Housing_Developments/FeatureServer/0). No single complex drives the whole cluster, but tracts 1098.01 and 1984 may be dominated by one.
- Overlaps U025 (tract 1097.01, next door). U007 is the stronger version.
- Boundaries stable (0.965).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1910 to 1920: Cleveland's Black population rises from 8,448 to 34,451, mostly settling in Central (https://case.edu/ech/articles/c/central-neighborhood).
- 1935 to 1937: The federal PWA builds Outhwaite and Cedar-Central, among the first public housing in the US (same source).
- 1939/1940: HOLC grades 86% of the sampled area "D" (Mapping Inequality, our point sample: https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining).
- 1942 and 1971: Carver Park (1942) and King-Kennedy (1971) make Central "the public housing capital of Cleveland" (https://case.edu/ech/articles/c/central-neighborhood; https://teachingcleveland.org/?p=1158).
- Mid-1950s to 1960: Urban renewal clearance elsewhere pushes displaced Black residents into Hough, which goes from 5% non-white in 1950 to 74% in 1960 (https://case.edu/ech/articles/h/hough).
- July 18 to 23, 1966: Hough uprising; 4 killed, about 50 injured, about 275 arrested; HUD then cuts Cleveland's urban renewal funding and cancels a $10 million grant (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hough_riots).
- 1960 to 2000: Central falls from 52,675 to 12,107 people; Hough from 65,694 (1950) to 16,409 (2000) and housing units from about 23,000 (1966) to 8,409 (2000) (https://case.edu/ech/articles/c/central-neighborhood; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hough_riots).
- 2000s to now: Central's poverty rate reported at 71.5%, highest in the city (https://case.edu/ech/articles/c/central-neighborhood; year UNCONFIRMED). Foreclosure and lead-poisoning chapters not yet sourced.

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
5. Nearly every layer, from the Great Migration and New Deal housing to the HOLC "D" grade, 1966 unrest, cut renewal funds and 75% population loss, is dated and has a public data series.

## What makes it distinct
The birthplace of American public housing, where the first New Deal estates and every later wave of public housing stacked up in one place.
