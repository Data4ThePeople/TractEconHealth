# U031: East and central North Little Rock, plus a piece of Little Rock east of I-30 and the airport tract, Pulaski County, AR

## Name and place
Eight tracts on both sides of the Arkansas River (Census TIGERweb):
- North Little Rock: 05119002600, 05119002700, 05119002800, 05119002900, 05119003002 and 05119003100. These cover the east side and the central area north of downtown (Argenta).
- Little Rock: 05119000500, south of Roosevelt Road and east of I-30, and 05119980400, a special-use tract around Clinton National Airport with 532 residents.

Resident and media names are UNCONFIRMED in this pass. Likely names include Dixie, Rose City and Glenview on the NLR east side and the Hanger Hill area for tract 5. North Little Rock's Wikipedia article lists "Baring Cross" and "Dixie" among its neighborhoods (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Little_Rock,_Arkansas). The 2020 census counted 15,991 people, 75% Black.

## Rule-out flags
- The cluster crosses the river and two cities, and it includes an airport tract. It is not one neighborhood.
- No institution: group quarters total 420, none in prisons, colleges or the military (2020 PL, table P5).
- Tract stability is good (0.863, with 1970 data).
- Documentation in this pass is thin. The web search budget ran out.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1871 to 1917: Argenta was founded as the railroad side of the river and fought annexation by Little Rock. It became North Little Rock in 1917 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Little_Rock,_Arkansas).
- 1909 to 1970: Scipio Jones High School served Black students in North Little Rock under segregation. It closed in 1970 (same source).
- Late 1930s: HOLC graded parts of the cluster "D." By our overlay of Mapping Inequality polygons, D covers about 50% of Little Rock tract 5, 43% of NLR tract 29, 18% of tract 26, 13% of tract 30.02 and 9% of tract 28 (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/).
- 1950s to 1970s: Interstate building (I-30, I-40) and urban renewal in North Little Rock are UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
- Railroad shops: Missouri Pacific's shops in North Little Rock were a large employer. Dates and job counts are UNCONFIRMED.
- 1970 to 2020: Cluster population fell from about 32,600 to 16,000, a 51% drop. Poverty went from 37% (1970) to 28% (1980) and then 38% (2020 to 2024). The Black share went from 57% to 75% (project file history_long.csv).

## Story strength (1-5)
2. The HOLC grades and the 51% population loss are real, but the local history is not documented in this pass, and the cluster is a patchwork of two cities.

## What makes it distinct
The poor side of a twin city: the rail town across the river from the state capital. That angle is UNCONFIRMED until the railroad shop history is sourced.
