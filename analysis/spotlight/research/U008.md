# U008: Central City, B.W. Cooper, Gert Town, Hollygrove and Dixon (New Orleans back-of-town)

## Name and place
Orleans Parish, New Orleans. The city's Neighborhood Statistical Area layer (https://gis.nola.gov/arcgis/rest/services/apps/BoundariesMayorysViewer/MapServer/3) places the tract centroids in:
- Central City (including the Magnolia / C.J. Peete site, now Harmony Oaks): tracts 86, 94, 140, 143
- B.W. Cooper Housing Development (the "Calliope"): tract 69
- Gert Town / Zion City: tract 72
- Dixon: tract 76.05
- Hollygrove: tract 75.01

This is not one neighborhood. It is two pieces: Central City plus B.W. Cooper near downtown, and Gert Town, Dixon and Hollygrove about two miles northwest, linked by the Pontchartrain Expressway and US 90 corridor.

Population about 14,833. 2020 census: 70% to 88% Black by tract.

## Rule-out flags
- Two separate places joined into one cluster. A story would need to pick Central City (tracts 69, 86, 94, 140, 143) or the Gert Town / Hollygrove side.
- Tract 69 is centered on the B.W. Cooper public housing site (2020 population 1,399). Tract 86 holds much of the old Magnolia site. Public housing shapes these tracts but does not dominate the cluster as a whole.
- No prison, college or base in the tracts (2020 group quarters are 29 people or fewer per tract). Xavier University is adjacent to Gert Town but its dorms do not show in tract 72's group quarters (13).
- Overlaps in theme with U012 (public housing demolition), which is stronger.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1849 to about 1945: Dryades Street is a commercial hub; the article says the district had "over 200 businesses" at its height after World War II, a major Black business district under Jim Crow. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_City,_New_Orleans
- 1932: Thompson-Hayward Chemical opens a pesticide and herbicide plant in Gert Town; it runs under several owners until 1986. Later contamination claims are settled for $51.575 million (date of settlement not given). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gert_Town,_New_Orleans
- 1941 and 1955: The Magnolia public housing development opens (first part 1941), and expands in 1955; peak 1,403 units. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_Projects
- 1940s to 1960s: Gert Town peaks at about 8,700 people in the 1940s; Hollygrove builds out after drainage is reworked in the 1960s. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gert_Town,_New_Orleans ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollygrove,_New_Orleans
- Late 1960s to about 1990: The Dryades commercial strip declines, takes "a steep nose-dive" in the 1980s, and is mostly vacant around 1990. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_City,_New_Orleans
- Mid-1980s to 1990s: The crack epidemic hits Central City, Magnolia and Hollygrove hard. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_City,_New_Orleans ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollygrove,_New_Orleans
- 2001 to 2011: Magnolia is partly demolished starting 2001-02, vacated and razed in late 2008, and replaced by Harmony Oaks (460 units) in 2011. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_Projects
- 2000 to 2010: Central City falls from 19,072 to 11,257 residents (down 41.0%); Hollygrove from 6,919 to 4,377 (down 36.7%). Central City mostly stayed dry in Katrina; Gert Town and Hollygrove flooded. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_City,_New_Orleans ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollygrove,_New_Orleans

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. Central City alone has a well-documented, dated arc (business district, public housing, crack era, demolition) that mostly stayed dry in Katrina, but the cluster mixes two places and overlaps with U012.

## What makes it distinct
Central City is the clearest case in New Orleans of decline that Katrina did not cause: it mostly stayed above the flood, yet lost 41% of its people from 2000 to 2010, after a half-century slide of its Black main street on Dryades Street.
