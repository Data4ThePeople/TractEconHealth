# R019: Martin County, KY and the lower Tug Fork (Inez, Warfield, Kermit, Harts, Chapmanville area)

## Name and place
A rural cluster on both sides of the Tug Fork. Three tracts are in Martin County, Kentucky (9501, 9502.02, 9503), which take in the county seat of Inez and the Warfield area. Five are in West Virginia: Mingo County tract 9571 (Kermit and the north end of Mingo), Logan County tracts 9561.02 and 9561.04 (the Chapmanville side), and Lincoln County tracts 9557 (Harts CDP) and 9558. The place names come from tract centroids and the Census geocoder and are approximate. Population 22,204.

## Rule-out flags
- No prison, college or base in these tracts in 2020 (PL file table P5 shows at most 67 people in group quarters, a nursing home). USP Big Sandy is in Martin County but is not in these tracts.
- Spread across two states and four counties. The Martin County story (about 8,000 people) is the strongest thread, and the West Virginia tracts dilute it. Consider whether the cluster can be cut to the Martin County tracts plus Kermit.
- Overlaps with R007 (McDowell) and R003 (Williamson). Pick one Tug Fork story.

## History
- **April 24, 1964:** President Johnson visited Inez and the porch of Tom Fletcher, an unemployed sawmill worker, to launch the War on Poverty. Area poverty was over 60 percent at the time ([NPR, 2014](https://www.npr.org/2014/01/08/260151923/kentucky-county-that-gave-war-on-poverty-a-face-still-struggles)). The exact date of April 24 is UNCONFIRMED in this search; NPR places it in April 1964.
- **1970s-1990s:** Coal mining, much of it surface mining, became the main employer. Job counts UNCONFIRMED; MSHA data can show them.
- **October 11, 2000:** A Martin County Coal slurry impoundment broke into old mine works below it. It released an estimated 306 million gallons into Coldwater and Wolf creeks (the official MSHA estimate; EPA's early reports said 200-250 million). Inez's water system was left with a 12-day reserve and schools closed ([MSHA investigation report](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20081105071929/http:/www.msha.gov/impoundments/martincounty/martincountya.pdf); [EPA polrep](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2014-03/documents/polrep10.pdf); [ATSDR health consultation, 2006](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/MartinCountyCoalSlurryRelease/MartinCountyCoalSlurryHC080706.pdf)).
- **2006-2007:** Drug distributors sent nearly 9 million hydrocodone pills over two years to one pharmacy in Kermit, a town of 392 ([WV Press / Charleston Gazette-Mail](https://wvpress.org/?p=54841)). The pharmacy's owner pleaded guilty to fraud charges ([FBI, 2012](https://www.fbi.gov/pittsburgh/press-releases/2012/mingo-county-pharmacist-sentenced-to-prison-time-for-conspiracy-to-acquire-controlled-substances-by-fraud/)).
- **2018:** The state Public Service Commission granted the Martin County Water District an emergency rate increase of 26.5 percent. At times the system lost more than 70 percent of the water it treated ([WKYU / Ohio Valley ReSource](https://www.wkyufm.org/post/infrastructure-funding-horizon-beleaguered-martin-county-looks-raise-water-rates-again)).
- **December 2022:** The county declared a state of emergency over widespread water outages and a countywide boil-water notice ([Mountain Citizen](https://mountaincitizen.com/2022/12/31/judge-declares-state-of-emergency-as-winter-weather-cripples-martin-county-water-district/)).
- **2026:** The district still lost about 49 percent of the water it produced in March 2026 ([Mountain Citizen](https://mountaincitizen.com/2026/05/06/martin-county-water-still-losing-nearly-half-its-water-amid-tight-cash-rising-bills/)).

## Story strength
4. The dates are well documented and the coal, slurry, pills and water threads compound. The cluster spreads across four counties, though, and the coal-job series overlaps with stronger coal candidates.

## What makes it distinct
This is the place the War on Poverty used as its face in 1964. Sixty years later its people still cannot count on running water. The local water system is the through line from the 2000 slurry spill to today's leaks and rate hikes.
