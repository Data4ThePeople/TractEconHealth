## Rule-out flags
- A large state prison (likely Parchman) sits in one of the 8 tracts. Drop that tract, or rule this out.
- The cluster joins Arkansas and Mississippi across a stretch of the river with no bridge in between (UNCONFIRMED; the nearest crossings are Greenville to the south and Helena to the north). Residents do not see this as one place.
- Too similar to R030 (Phillips County), which has the stronger, better-documented story.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- Before 1940: Cotton was the main crop, worked by sharecroppers and tenant farmers (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desha_County,_Arkansas).
- 1927: The Great Mississippi Flood. Arkansas City, the former river port and county seat, declined after it. UNCONFIRMED; no source pulled.
- 1942-1945: The Rohwer War Relocation Center in Desha County holds up to nearly 8,500 Japanese Americans (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desha_County,_Arkansas).
- 1940 to 2020: Desha County falls from 27,160 to 11,395, a 58.0% drop. It lost people almost every decade after 1940 (same source).
- Mid-1900s: Mechanization displaced farm labor. Thousands of Black farm workers left in the Great Migration, and farms consolidated (same source).
- Plant closings and hospital closings: no sourced dates found. UNCONFIRMED.
