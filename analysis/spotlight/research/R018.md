# R018: Albion, Michigan (Calhoun County)

## Name and place
Two tracts in and around the small city of Albion in eastern Calhoun County, Michigan. Tract 36 is inside the city limits (north side), and tract 33 covers the south side and nearby rural land. Population 3,923. The city had 7,700 people in 2020, and about 29 percent were Black, a high share for a small Michigan town ([Wikipedia, Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion,_Michigan)).

## Rule-out flags
- **Albion College is in town but not in these tracts.** The 2020 PL file shows zero people in college housing in tracts 33 and 36 (4 people in group quarters total). So the college does not drive these numbers. Students living off campus could still affect income figures; check ACS enrollment by tract.
- Two tracts, about 3,900 people, so the estimates are noisy.

## History
- **1888:** The Albion Malleable Iron Works was founded. It started with farm implements and switched to auto parts around World War I ([Albion Interactive History](https://isaackremer.com/albion/business/b_albion_malleable/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion,_Michigan)).
- **1916:** The Malleable recruited Black workers from Pensacola, Florida and other Southern towns ([Albion Interactive History](https://isaackremer.com/albion/business/b_albion_malleable/)).
- **1960:** The city's population peaked at 12,749 ([Wikipedia, from census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion,_Michigan)).
- **1969:** The Malleable merged with Hayes to form Hayes-Albion ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion,_Michigan)).
- **1975:** A major factory closed, starting a long loss of jobs and people. The factory is not named in the source ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion,_Michigan)). Name and job count UNCONFIRMED.
- **2002:** Harvard Industries closed the Hayes-Albion plant (the Malleable). A local columnist called it the most disastrous year in the town's history ([Albion Morning Star column, 2002](https://stagingthree.albionmich.net/?p=6468)). Job count UNCONFIRMED.
- **2013-2016:** Albion closed its high school and the school district was later annexed into Marshall Public Schools. UNCONFIRMED; not found in this search.
- **2020:** Population fell to 7,700, a 40 percent drop from the 1960 peak ([Wikipedia, from census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion,_Michigan)).

## Story strength
3. The foundry arc is clear and its start and end dates are known. The middle years (the 1975 closure, later plant losses, the school district) need confirmation and job counts.

## What makes it distinct
A small foundry town that recruited Black workers from the Deep South in 1916 lost its foundry, then its high school. The college on the hill stayed. It is the only small-town Great Migration story in this batch.
