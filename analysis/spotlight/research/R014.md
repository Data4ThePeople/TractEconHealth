# R014: Tohono O'odham Nation (Sells, Baboquivari and Pisinemo districts), Pima County, AZ

## Name and place
Two tracts on the Tohono O'odham Nation Reservation, Pima County: 04019940800 (Census Tract 9408, about 1,827 square miles, internal point in Baboquivari District) and 04019941100 (Tract 9411, about 1,949 square miles, Pisinemo District). Population about 5,526. The population-weighted centroid falls in Sells District; Sells is the Nation's capital and largest community (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohono_O%27odham_Nation). The people call themselves Tohono O'odham ("Desert People"). Tucson is the nearest city.

## Rule-out flags
- None major. Boundaries stable since 1970 (stability 1.0).
- Tract 9408 touches the U.S.-Mexico border; Border Patrol facilities exist on the Nation but their staff are unlikely to live in the tracts (UNCONFIRMED).
- Overlap risk: another reservation candidate (R008, M010) may be stronger; this one is distinct because of the border.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1853: The Gadsden Purchase drew the U.S.-Mexico border through O'odham land, splitting it roughly in half (https://www.tonation-nsn.gov/history-culture/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohono_O%27odham_Nation).
- 1900 to 1930: Federal reclamation, industrial growth and BIA recruitment pushed thousands of O'odham into off-reservation wage work, including cotton fields (Meeks 2003, https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-tohono-oodham-wage-labor-and-resistant-adaptation-1900-1930/; https://www.resilience.org/stories/2009-11-04/native-recipe-health/).
- 1916: The main reservation was created by executive order with Indian Oasis (now Sells) as headquarters (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohono_O%27odham_Nation).
- After WWII: Deep wells for Tucson's growth were dug along the reservation boundaries; floodwater farming of crops such as tepary beans declined (https://www.resilience.org/stories/2009-11-04/native-recipe-health/). Exact dates and acreage: UNCONFIRMED.
- 1960: Painted Rock Dam flooded about 10,000 acres of the Nation's Gila Bend reservation (not in these tracts) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohono_O%27odham_Nation).
- 1990s to now: Border enforcement grew on the Nation's 62 to 74 miles of border; in 2014 CBP contracted for surveillance towers, with 52 along Arizona's border by 2018 (same source).
- June 2026: The Nation sued over border wall construction on its land (same source).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
3. The border split and the loss of desert farming are well documented in history, but few public data series track them at the tract level over time.

## What makes it distinct
A nation cut in two by an international border in 1853, then drawn off its desert farms into wage work, and now living inside a growing border enforcement zone.
