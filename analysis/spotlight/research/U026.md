## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1916: The Monroe natural gas field is discovered, said to stretch more than 500 square miles; Monroe becomes known as "the natural gas capital of the world." Parish population rises from 30,319 (1920) to 54,337 (1930). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Parish,_Louisiana
- 1920s: Delta Air Lines' founders include Monroe's Biedenharn family; the company later moves to Atlanta (move date not on the page). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe,_Louisiana
- Carbon black plants built on the gas field, and the West Monroe paper mill (now Graphic Packaging): dates and job counts UNCONFIRMED.
- 1930 to 1980: Monroe grows from 26,028 to 57,597. 1980 to 2020: falls to 47,702 (down 17.2%) while the parish grows to 160,368, a sign of movement to suburbs. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe,_Louisiana ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Parish,_Louisiana
- 2000 to 2020: Monroe's non-Hispanic white share falls from 36.38% to 30.18%. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe,_Louisiana
- I-20 crosses tracts 58, 108 and 111 (TIGERweb). Construction dates and right-of-way through Black neighborhoods: UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
3. Monroe has a vivid gas-boom start and clear suburban drift, but the middle chapters (carbon black, paper, I-20) are not yet documented and the cluster mixes two communities.

## What makes it distinct
A "natural gas capital" city whose gas boom built it in the 1920s and whose core then thinned out as the field played out and families moved to the suburbs, with a paper-mill town across the river.
