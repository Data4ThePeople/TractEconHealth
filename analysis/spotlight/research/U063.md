# U063: Woodlawn (and west edge of Washington Park), Chicago (editor seed)

## Name and place

South Side of Chicago, Cook County, Illinois, just south of the University of Chicago's Midway Plaisance.
- 17031420500, 17031420600, 17031420700, 17031421200: Woodlawn (community area 42), west and central parts, around Cottage Grove and 61st to 67th streets.
- 17031400800: Washington Park community area (40), just west of Woodlawn.

2020 census population: 12,181. No group quarters (4 people). No prison, dorm or base. The Obama Presidential Center in Jackson Park is east of these tracts.

## Rule-out flags

- None on data. Stable tracts back to 1970.
- Income and health are less extreme than Englewood (income pctile 7.8, health 5.5), and the area is now getting investment, which makes it a "decline and reinvestment" story rather than pure decline.

## History (dated bullets with sources)

- **About 1940: HOLC grades.** Our overlay of Mapping Inequality polygons (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining): all five tracts are 77% to 100% grade D (areas D74, D75, D78).
- **1960: peak.** Woodlawn had 81,279 residents (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn,_Chicago, from census).
- **1950s to 1960s: university-led renewal.** University of Chicago renewal began in Hyde Park in the 1950s, and a strip from 60th to 61st St. in Woodlawn was scheduled for clearance for a planned South Campus. The Woodlawn Organization formed in 1961 in response (Woodlawn Wikipedia page). Exact clearance year and household counts: UNCONFIRMED.
- **1968 to 1971: arson and abandonment.** A reported 362 abandoned buildings were destroyed by arson; arson continued into the 1980s (Woodlawn Wikipedia page). The April 1968 riots also hit Woodlawn (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Chicago_riots).
- **1960 to 1990: population collapse.** 81,279 to 27,473, a 66% drop (Woodlawn Wikipedia page).
- **1996: transit cut.** The CTA removed the nearly finished Green Line station and tracks east of Cottage Grove (Woodlawn Wikipedia page).
- **2016 to 2026: Obama Center.** Jackson Park site announced July 29, 2016; construction began August 2021; opened June 19, 2026. Displacement worries led to a Woodlawn housing ordinance proposed in July 2019 and a compromise in July 2020 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_Presidential_Center).

## Cause tags (primary / secondary)

## Story strength (1-5)

4. Well documented and dated, but the main causes are institutional and housing-market events rather than many separate economic shocks, and the latest chapter is reinvestment.

## What makes it distinct

Of the three Chicago seeds, Woodlawn has the most distinct story: a neighborhood shaped for 70 years by a large institution next door, first through university renewal plans, then mass arson and a lost rail line, and now the 2026 opening of the Obama Center. No other candidate in this batch has that arc.
