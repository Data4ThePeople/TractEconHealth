# R020: Rural Cherokee communities around Stilwell, Adair County, Oklahoma

## Name and place
- One tract, 40001376900 (Census Tract 3769, Adair County). About 15 square miles, 2020 population 4,305.
- The tract's bounding box touches the city of Stilwell (the county seat) and the CDPs of Zion, Marietta, Elm Grove, Fairfield and Lyons Switch; it lies in the Cherokee Oklahoma Tribal Statistical Area (TIGERweb, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). Centroid 35.812, -94.638, on the edge of Stilwell.
- 2020 census: 2,270 of 4,305 residents (53%) are American Indian alone (2020 PL 94-171, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl).
- Best name: "Stilwell and the Cherokee communities around it," Adair County. Residents' own community names (Zion, Marietta, Lyons Switch) should be confirmed locally.

## Rule-out flags
- Group quarters: 103 people, including 34 in correctional facilities (likely the county jail in Stilwell, UNCONFIRMED) and 55 in nursing facilities. Not a driver.
- Tract boundaries are stable since 1990 (stability 1.0).
- Possible overlap with other tribal-land candidates in the full candidate pool; this one is Cherokee and agricultural rather than oil.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1838-1839: Stilwell area is near the western end of the Cherokee Trail of Tears (City of Stilwell, https://www.cityofstilwell.com/history; Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilwell,_Oklahoma).
- 1906-1907: Adair County is created at the Oklahoma constitutional convention from the Going Snake and Flint districts of the Cherokee Nation, as tribal land is allotted and the Cherokee government is dissolved for statehood (Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History and Culture, Adair County, https://www.okhistory.org/publications/enc/entry?entry=AD003). Specific allotment land-loss figures for Adair County: UNCONFIRMED.
- 1930s-1940s: Strawberries become the main cash crop, with about 2,000 acres at one point (Wikipedia, Stilwell, citing local history, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilwell,_Oklahoma).
- 1942: Stilwell Canning Company opens (Stilwell Foods after 1974), a major employer (Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History and Culture, https://www.okhistory.org/publications/enc/entry?entry=AD003).
- 1948-1949: First Strawberry Festival; Stilwell named "Strawberry Capital of the World" (Wikipedia, Stilwell, same page).
- 1960: Adair County has the lowest per capita income in Oklahoma (Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History and Culture, AD003, same link).
- Later decades: Strawberry acreage shrinks and the crop's role in the local economy fades (Wikipedia, Stilwell). Dates and acreage counts: UNCONFIRMED; Census of Agriculture would show them.
- Present: A food plant still operates in Stilwell (a 2026 Schwan's job posting, https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/production-manager-stilwell-ok--4cdaa4d4-4d4d-49f3-8b4b-982006b3e578). A Tyson WARN notice for 535 workers in Stilwell appears on a third-party tracker with no date (https://warnfirehose.com/layoffs/oklahoma/stillwell): UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
3. The allotment-to-statehood start, the strawberry rise and fall, and the 1960 lowest-income ranking give a long dated arc, but the later causes (crop decline, plant changes) are not yet pinned to dates and job counts.

## What makes it distinct
A place that has been at or near the bottom of Oklahoma's income ranking since at least 1960, with a majority Cherokee population, where the story is land allotment and a lost specialty crop rather than oil or factories.

## Data that could show it (source, link, what it shows, years covered)
- Census of Agriculture, historical volumes (https://agcensus.library.cornell.edu) and USDA NASS Quick Stats (https://quickstats.nass.usda.gov): strawberry acreage, number of farms, farm size in Adair County, 1930-2022.
- BEA county personal income (CAINC1), https://apps.bea.gov/regional: Adair per capita income versus Oklahoma, 1969 on; 1960 decennial for the earlier point (NHGIS, https://www.nhgis.org).
- NHGIS tract and county series, https://www.nhgis.org: population, American Indian share, income, poverty, 1930 on (county), 1990 on (tract).
- County Business Patterns, https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp.html: food processing employment in Adair County, 1964 on.
- Dawes Rolls and Cherokee allotment records, Oklahoma Historical Society, https://www.okhistory.org/research/dawes: allotments in the Going Snake and Flint districts, about 1902-1907.
- Gateway to Oklahoma History newspaper archive, https://gateway.okhistory.org: dated stories on the cannery and strawberry harvests.
