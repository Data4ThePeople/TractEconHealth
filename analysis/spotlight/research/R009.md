## History (dated bullets with sources)
- August 21, 1921: Two gushers (Desenberg No. 1 and Adamson No. 1) set off the Mexia oil boom; the field produces 5 million barrels in 1921 and 35 million in 1922 (Hays Free Press, This Week in Texas History, https://www.haysfreepress.com/2022/09/21/this-week-in-texas-history-the-down-side-of-an-oil-boom; Texas Historical Commission marker, https://atlas.thc.texas.gov/Details/5293003354).
- January 12 to February 28, 1922: Martial law in Mexia as growth overwhelms local government; population reportedly jumps from about 3,482 to nearly 35,000 (Mexia history blog, https://mexiahistory.blogspot.com/2018/10/january-5-6-1922-general-pershing.html; Hays Free Press, same link). Population figures are from secondary sources.
- Mid-1920s: Reckless production drains the field; population falls to about 10,000 (Hays Free Press, same link; figure UNCONFIRMED against census).
- Cotton and Black farm history: Mexia hosts one of Texas's oldest Juneteenth gatherings at Booker T. Washington Emancipation Park (UNCONFIRMED in this pass; no source pulled).
- 1940s: A state school for people with intellectual disabilities opens on the site of a former World War II camp (UNCONFIRMED in this pass), becoming a main local employer.
- Later decades: No dated local plant closures found in this pass.

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
2. The 1920s oil boom and bust is vivid and well documented, but it is one early event; the later compounding causes are not yet found, and the tract boundaries changed.

## What makes it distinct
A town that went from about 3,500 to tens of thousands in one oil year and needed martial law, then lost the boom within a few years.
