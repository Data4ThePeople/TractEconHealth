# U034: North Birmingham (Collegeville, Harriman Park, Fairmont, North Birmingham and nearby), Birmingham, AL

## Name and place
Eight tracts on Birmingham's north side, Jefferson County: 0005, 0007, 0008, 0016, 0024.02, 0027.02, 0029, 0055. Population about 17,151. Tract centroids (TIGERweb) fall in the North Birmingham, Collegeville, Douglasville, Oakhurst/Hooper City and Avon Park areas, with 0024.02, 0027.02 and 0029 reaching south toward Norwood, Fountain Heights and Smithfield (neighborhood names for those three are UNCONFIRMED; check against the City of Birmingham neighborhood map). Tract 0055 contains the Bluestone (formerly Walter/Sloss) Coke plant at 3500 35th Ave N (Census geocoder match to 01073005500). Residents and local media call the core area "North Birmingham" and the "35th Avenue" neighborhoods.

## Rule-out flags
- None that drive the numbers by themselves. No prison, college or base found in these tracts.
- Stability 0.875 since 1970: one or more tracts were redrawn; check before mapping long series.
- The cluster may join two areas with different stories (north side industrial vs. Fountain Heights/Smithfield, cut by I-65). Consider trimming to the 35th Avenue tracts (0005, 0007, 0008, 0055, 0016).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1930s: HOLC mapped Birmingham with grades including "Hazardous" and "Negro Concentrations" (National Archives map: https://www.docsteach.org/documents/document/redlining-map-birmingham-alabama). Grades for these specific neighborhoods are UNCONFIRMED; check Mapping Inequality.
- 1911 to 2010: U.S. Pipe ran its North Birmingham pipe plant from 1911 (as Dimmick Pipe) until it stopped operating there in March 2010 (https://www.bhamwiki.com/w/US_Pipe). Job count at closing UNCONFIRMED.
- 1947: Aerial photos show coke emissions over Collegeville; the area was once parks and a golf course before the coke works expanded (Allen et al. 2019, UAB: https://www.uab.edu/soph/home/images/documents/news/Allen_et_al_2019_The_Search_for_Environmental_Justice_the_Story_of_North_Birmingham.pdf).
- 1950s to 1970s: I-65 was routed through Fountain Heights and Smithfield, home to many prominent Black families, cutting them off and lowering nearby property values (https://elgl.org/reshaping-the-most-segregated-city-in-america). Exact years UNCONFIRMED.
- 1970s: Sloss Furnaces closed and the region's steel industry began a slow decline (https://www.npr.org/2015/08/18/432683704/u-s-steel-to-end-operations-at-alabamas-fairfield-works-mill; Sloss year UNCONFIRMED).
- 2009 to 2014: EPA air screening near schools in 2009 led to soil testing; EPA proposed the 35th Avenue Superfund site covering Collegeville, Harriman Park and Fairmont (EPA 2014 notice: https://19january2021snapshot.epa.gov/sites/static/files/2014-09/documents/ns-35th-avenue-9-2-2014.pdf). About 2,030 of 2,100 properties sampled and about 669 needed cleanup as of 2022 (https://southern-science.com/2022/09/19/birminghams-pollution-master-class/). NPL status UNCONFIRMED.
- 2015: U.S. Steel ended steelmaking at Fairfield Works, about 1,100 jobs (regional, not in these tracts) (https://www.npr.org/2015/08/18/432683704/u-s-steel-to-end-operations-at-alabamas-fairfield-works-mill).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
5. Dated plant history, a federal Superfund record, HOLC maps, highway routing and a regional steel decline all compound, and each has a public record.

## What makes it distinct
The neighborhood grew up as worker housing next to coke ovens and pipe plants, kept the pollution after the jobs left, and then became a Superfund site.
