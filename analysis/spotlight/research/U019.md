# U019: Springfield, MA (North End, Metro Center, South End)

## Name and place
- Springfield, Hampden County, MA. Eight tracts in three named neighborhoods: the **North End** (Brightwood and Memorial Square, tracts 8006, 8007, 8008), **Liberty Heights' southern edge** (8009), **Metro Center** (downtown, 8011.01, 8012) and the **South End** (8019.02, 8020). Neighborhood names from OpenStreetMap reverse geocoding of tract centroids (TIGERweb) and the city's neighborhood list (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Springfield,_Massachusetts_neighborhoods).
- Residents and media say "the North End" and "the South End." Brightwood is described as over 80% Puerto Rican and the center of the city's Puerto Rican community (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightwood,_Springfield,_Massachusetts).
- Institutions checked: Baystate Medical Center (759 Chestnut St) geocodes to tract 8005, outside the cluster. Springfield Technical Community College (Armory site) geocodes to tract 8013, outside the cluster. No prison, base or dorm college in the cluster found. The old Hampden County jail on York Street (South End) closed in the 1990s (UNCONFIRMED year).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1937-1938: HOLC graded Springfield neighborhoods; the North End and South End riverfront blocks are expected to be C/D (UNCONFIRMED until checked on the map). https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining
- 1960s: Interstate 91 was built on an elevated alignment along the riverfront and cut through the North End, splitting it, and a Main Street clearance program displaced mostly working-class residents of color, including the growing Puerto Rican community. https://thewestendmuseum.org/?p=27717 ; https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/North_End,_Springfield,_Massachusetts
- 1960s: Springfield urban renewal displaced a large segment of the valley's Puerto Rican population. https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans_in_Holyoke
- 1968: The Defense Department closed Springfield Armory, the city's anchor of precision manufacturing; sources date the closing 1968 to 1969, and the loss was followed by the departure of several precision manufacturing firms. https://www.ourpluralhistory.stcc.edu/industrial/armory.html ; https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Springfield,_MA (job count UNCONFIRMED)
- 2004-2009: State finance control board ran the city's finances (UNCONFIRMED dates).
- June 1, 2011: An EF3 tornado hit the South End first and then Metro Center; 3 deaths and about 200 injuries across its path. https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_New_England_tornado_outbreak ; https://www.nepm.org/regional-news/2026-06-01/15-years-after-a-tornado-tore-through-springfield-mayor-sarno-reflects-on-the-citys-past-present
- 2018-2019: The Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America ranked Springfield the No. 1 "Asthma Capital" two years running; No. 4 in 2024. https://aafa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/aafa-2019-asthma-capitals-report.pdf ; https://aafa.org/report-names-most-challenging-u-s-cities-for-living-with-asthma-in-2024/

4. A dated chain (redlining, I-91 and clearance in the 1960s, Armory closing 1968, manufacturing exits, 2011 tornado) with public data for most links; job counts still need sourcing.

## What makes it distinct
A federal arsenal town: the closing of the nation's first armory removed the anchor employer, and a riverfront interstate then cut a Puerto Rican neighborhood in half, with a tornado landing on top decades later.
