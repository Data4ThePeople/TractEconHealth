# U036: Planeview, southeast Wichita, KS

## Name and place
Census tract 65 in Sedgwick County (0.8 sq mi, ZIP 67210), inside Wichita city limits. The tract's bounding box and centroid (37.642, -97.291) match Planeview, the neighborhood residents and local media call "Planeview" (the neighborhood association is Planeview United). It sits just north of the former Boeing Wichita plant and McConnell Air Force Base area. Population 3,156 (ACS); 3,429 in the 2020 census, of whom 1,462 are Hispanic or Latino.

## Rule-out flags
- No prison, college, base or nursing home in the tract (2020 group quarters: 0).
- Stable boundary back to 1970. Good for a long series.
- One tract only, so tract-level numbers are noisy.
- Military base check: McConnell AFB is nearby but not in the tract (group quarters 0).

## History
- **1942-1944:** The federal government built Planeview as temporary housing for aircraft workers; it was planned at about 2,000 units and later reported at more than 4,000 units on 592 acres just north of Boeing ([KMUW Past and Present](https://www.kmuw.org/past-and-present/2018-08-21/wichitas-forgotten-neighborhood-past-present); [The Active Age](https://theactiveage.com/planeview-we-had-a-joint-purpose/)). Unit counts differ across sources; a 1942 Wichita Beacon item cited 4,382 units (search summary, UNCONFIRMED against the original).
- **Wartime peak:** A former resident estimated about 22,000 people lived there ([The Active Age](https://theactiveage.com/planeview-we-had-a-joint-purpose/)). This is a recollection, not a count (UNCONFIRMED).
- **June 1955:** The federal government sold the land, and Wichita annexed Planeview and its schools ([The Active Age](https://theactiveage.com/planeview-we-had-a-joint-purpose/)). Exact month per a search summary of Active Age coverage.
- **1970s on:** New immigration from Mexico and Central and South America reshaped Wichita neighborhoods ([Somos de Wichita, WSU](https://somos.wichita.edu/en/new-migrations-new-strategies-old-challenges/)). Planeview became a main landing spot.
- **2000-2010:** Planeview's Hispanic share rose from 37% to 53%, per a 2011 Wichita Eagle census story ([Wichita Eagle via Brown US2010 clippings](https://s4.ad.brown.edu/Projects/Diversity/News/inthenews.PDFs/us2010.newsjantomay.2011/us2010.news2011.03.27.wichitaeagle.pdf)).
- **2010s:** Boeing ended its Wichita operations, and the area's aircraft jobs went through repeated cuts. Dates and job counts UNCONFIRMED (search budget ran out); BLS QCEW and Wichita Eagle archives can supply them.
- **2024-2025:** City council approved funding for a roughly $10 million Planeview Park Community Center, with construction expected in 2025 ([Yahoo/Wichita Eagle](https://news.yahoo.com/wichita-neighborhood-slated-10m-community-224658967.html)).

## Story strength
3. The origin is unusual and well documented, but the decline chapters (housing aging out, aircraft layoffs) still need dated, sourced job and housing numbers.

## What makes it distinct
A whole town of federal war housing, built in under two years to be torn down after the war, that was never torn down and became the city's poorest immigrant gateway.
