# U013: Flint's north-central and east side (around I-475, the former St. John Street area and Buick City)

## Name and place
Eight tracts in the city of Flint, Genesee County, Michigan: 8, 18, 19, 20, 22, 23, 24 and 136. From tract centroids, these lie north and east of downtown along the I-475 corridor. That area includes the land where the St. John Street neighborhood stood, which was just east of the former Buick City complex, plus east-side neighborhoods off Davison Road ([Flintside](https://www.flintside.com/inthenews/flint-i-475-plan.aspx); [U-M Inclusive History](https://inclusivehistory.umich.edu/project-site/urban-renewal-and-the-university-of-michigan-flint/)). Neighborhood names by tract are approximate. Population 11,767. Median household income is in the 1.2nd national percentile, the lowest in this batch.

## Rule-out flags
- No single institution drives the numbers. Tract 136 has 73 people in college housing out of 1,357 (likely UM-Flint or Mott Community College housing near downtown). That is small.
- Overlaps with U055 (west-side Flint). Pick one Flint story; this one is stronger.
- The Flint water crisis is a single event. Here it is the last of many layers, not the cause.

## History
- **1937:** HOLC graded Flint. The grades for St. John and the east side are UNCONFIRMED here; check Mapping Inequality. Andrew Highsmith's *Demolition Means Progress* (2015) documents Flint's segregation, renewal and highway history.
- **1960:** Flint's population peaked at 196,940 ([Wikipedia, from census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint,_Michigan)). The city announced the urban renewal project that cleared land for I-475, a Buick expansion and an industrial park ([U-M Inclusive History](https://inclusivehistory.umich.edu/project-site/urban-renewal-and-the-university-of-michigan-flint/)).
- **1963:** The state highway department's "Freeways for Flint" report proposed I-475 as an urban renewal project for St. John Street ([Flintside](https://www.flintside.com/inthenews/flint-i-475-plan.aspx)).
- **Early 1970s:** The St. John Street neighborhood, home to Black and some Eastern European families, was cleared for I-475 and the St. John Industrial Park. Historian Andrew Highsmith says the projects "ultimately increased Black poverty and residential segregation." A UM-Flint historian argues renewal did more of the clearing than the highway did ([Flintside](https://www.flintside.com/inthenews/flint-i-475-plan.aspx); [East Village Magazine](https://www.eastvillagemagazine.org/?p=27478)).
- **1978:** GM employment in Flint peaked at about 80,000 and fell to under 8,000 by 2010 ([Wikipedia, Flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint,_Michigan)).
- **June 29, 1999:** Buick City closed its assembly line. About 1,200 workers were left, down from 28,000 at the complex's mid-1980s peak ([WSWS, July 1999](https://www.wsws.org/en/articles/1999/07/auto-j02.html); [Wikipedia, Buick City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buick_City)). GM announced the closure in November 1997.
- **2002-2004 and 2011-2018:** The state put Flint under emergency financial control twice ([Wikipedia, Flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint,_Michigan)).
- **April 25, 2014 to October 16, 2015:** The city drew its water from the Flint River without corrosion control, which led to lead contamination ([NBC News timeline](https://www.nbcnews.com/storyline/flint-water-crisis/bad-decisions-broken-promises-timeline-flint-water-crisis-n499641); [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-25/the-flint-water-crisis-begins)).

## Story strength
5. A neighborhood was cleared for a highway and factory land next to the auto plant that employed it. The plant closed, the city lost 59 percent of its people, and the water crisis followed. Each layer has a date, a scholarly source and a data series.

## What makes it distinct
This is the neighborhood that was cleared to make room for the factory that later left. The same land carried the highway, the Buick expansion and an industrial park, and then sat empty after Buick City closed in 1999.
