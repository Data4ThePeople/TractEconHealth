# R006: Browning, Blackfeet Reservation, Glacier County, MT

## Name and place
Glacier County tract 9402 (10.8 sq mi, ZIP 59417), inside the Blackfeet Indian Reservation. The tract is the town of Browning, the tribal headquarters of the Blackfeet Nation (Amskapi Piikani, the Southern Piegan or Pikuni). Population 5,572 (ACS); 5,670 in 2020, about 95% American Indian.

## Rule-out flags
- No single institution drives the numbers: 2020 group quarters 47 (22 correctional, 25 nursing).
- Stable boundaries to 1990.
- Lowest health percentile in this batch (0.3).
- Series likely includes at most one reservation. This is the runner-up to Pine Ridge (R046).

## History
- **1855:** Lame Bull (Judith River) Treaty set aside lands for the Blackfeet and a shared hunting ground ([OPI Blackfeet Reservation Timeline, 2017](https://opi.mt.gov/Portals/182/Page%20Files/Indian%20Education/Social%20Studies/K-12%20Resources/BlackfeetTimeline.pdf)).
- **January 23, 1870:** The Marias (Baker) Massacre killed 217 people of Heavy Runner's band, mostly women and children, by tribal count ([OPI timeline](https://opi.mt.gov/Portals/182/Page%20Files/Indian%20Education/Social%20Studies/K-12%20Resources/BlackfeetTimeline.pdf)).
- **1873-1880:** Executive orders cut the treaty land, partly restored it in 1875, then took it back in 1880 ([OPI timeline](https://opi.mt.gov/Portals/182/Page%20Files/Indian%20Education/Social%20Studies/K-12%20Resources/BlackfeetTimeline.pdf)).
- **1883-1884:** With the bison gone, the Starvation Winter killed over 500 Blackfeet (some sources say nearly 800) ([OPI timeline](https://opi.mt.gov/Portals/182/Page%20Files/Indian%20Education/Social%20Studies/K-12%20Resources/BlackfeetTimeline.pdf); [Trail Tribes](https://trailtribes.org/shrinking-reservation.htm)).
- **1896:** The tribe ceded a 20-mile strip that became Glacier National Park; the Blackfeet say it was a 99-year lease ([OPI timeline](https://opi.mt.gov/Portals/182/Page%20Files/Indian%20Education/Social%20Studies/K-12%20Resources/BlackfeetTimeline.pdf)).
- **1907-1919:** About 2,656 members got allotments; surplus land for sale was estimated at 156,000 acres in 1911; the 1907 and 1919 acts eroded the land base and "crippled" the young cattle economy ([OPI timeline](https://opi.mt.gov/Portals/182/Page%20Files/Indian%20Education/Social%20Studies/K-12%20Resources/BlackfeetTimeline.pdf); [Encyclopedia of the Great Plains](https://plainshumanities.unl.edu/encyclopedia/doc/egp.na.014.html)).
- **1934-1935:** Members voted 994 of 1,785 eligible to organize under the Indian Reorganization Act; constitution ratified 1935 ([OPI timeline](https://opi.mt.gov/Portals/182/Page%20Files/Indian%20Education/Social%20Studies/K-12%20Resources/BlackfeetTimeline.pdf)).
- **1996-2010:** Elouise Cobell, born on the reservation in 1945, led the class action over federal mismanagement of Indian trust accounts ([Cobell Scholarship](https://cobellscholar.org/?p=6094)). Filing and settlement dates and amounts UNCONFIRMED here (search budget ran out).

4. Many dated, documented losses (massacre, starvation, cession, allotment, trust mismanagement), but the 1930-today economic and health series are thinner than for Pine Ridge.

## What makes it distinct
The reservation lost its mountain front to become Glacier National Park, and a woman from Browning sued the federal government over a century of lost trust income.
