# R042: Western Alabama Black Belt (Marengo, Wilcox, Dallas, Choctaw and Sumter counties, AL)

## Name and place

- Tract internal points (https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). Town names are approximate and UNCONFIRMED unless noted:

## Rule-out flags
- A five-county patchwork is not one place. Residents would name towns (Linden, Gee's Bend, Pine Hill, Thomaston), not this cluster. The story should be told as "the Black Belt," or narrowed to Wilcox and Marengo.
- Boundaries are stable (0.996).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1860: Marengo County has 24,409 enslaved people and 6,761 free whites on 778 plantations and farms. After the war, many freed people became sharecroppers and tenant farmers (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marengo_County,_Alabama).
- 1932-1937: Gee's Bend, Wilcox County, gets Red Cross aid in 1932. In 1937 the Farm Security Administration buys the old Pettway plantation, over 10,000 acres, for $122,000 and rents it to 92 families (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gee%27s_Bend,_Alabama).
- 1940s to 1970: People leave farms during and after World War II, part of the Great Migration (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marengo_County,_Alabama). Marengo falls from 39,923 in 1910 to 19,323 in 2020, a 51.6% drop. Wilcox falls from 35,631 in 1900 to 10,600 in 2020, a 70.3% drop (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilcox_County,_Alabama).
- 1962: Local authorities end the Gee's Bend ferry to Camden after residents used it to go register to vote. It does not run again until September 18, 2006, 44 years later (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gee%27s_Bend,_Alabama).
- 1960s onward: Cotton land shifts to pasture, timber and catfish ponds, and paper, lumber and chemical plants arrive (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marengo_County,_Alabama). International Paper at Pine Hill employs about 400 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilcox_County,_Alabama).
- 2000: Wilcox County has 39.9% of its people in poverty and a median household income of $16,646 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilcox_County,_Alabama).
- 2011 and 2024: The Sheps Center lists closings of SouthWest Alabama Medical Center (2011) and Thomasville Regional Medical Center (2024) (https://www.shepscenter.unc.edu/programs-projects/rural-health/rural-hospital-closures/). Both are believed to be in Thomasville, Clarke County, next to Marengo and Choctaw. The location is UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5)
4. A plantation legacy, a federal resettlement experiment, a ferry cut off over voting, and a 70% population drop make a dated, layered story. It loses a point because the cluster is spread across five counties.

## What makes it distinct
The Gee's Bend ferry was shut down for 44 years after residents used it to register to vote. That turns a common Black Belt story into a specific, datable case of a community cut off on purpose.
