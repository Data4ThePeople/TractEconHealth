# R005: Marksville, Mansura, Cottonport, Bunkie and Simmesport, Avoyelles Parish, LA (plus Morganza area, Pointe Coupee Parish)

## Name and place
Eight tracts covering most of central and southern Avoyelles Parish, plus one in Pointe Coupee (Census TIGERweb places layer):
- 22009030401 and 22009030402: Marksville and the Fifth Ward area.
- 22009030502: Mansura and the edge of Marksville.
- 22009030600: Bunkie.
- 22009030701: Cottonport and Evergreen.
- 22009030702: Plaucheville and Moreauville.
- 22009030900: Simmesport.
- 22077952000 (Pointe Coupee Parish): Morganza.

## Rule-out flags
- Prisons. Tract 307.01 has 1,817 incarcerated people out of 4,152 (44%), which matches Raymond Laborde Correctional Center (formerly Avoyelles Correctional Center) near Cottonport (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Laborde_Correctional_Center). Tract 304.02 in Marksville has 284 more, likely a jail (UNCONFIRMED). Together they are about 8% of the 2020 cluster (2020 PL, table P5).
- This is not one neighborhood. It covers about 30 miles and several towns.
- Tract stability is good (0.952).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1841 to 1853: Solomon Northup was held as a slave on Avoyelles Parish plantations and freed in 1853 with help from Marksville officials (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marksville,_Louisiana).
- 1928 and 1954: After the 1927 flood, the Flood Control Act of 1928 authorized the Morganza Floodway. The Morganza Spillway was finished in 1954 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morganza_Spillway).
- 1939: The Bunkie post office got the mural "Cotton Pickers," marking the cotton economy (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunkie,_Louisiana).
- 1973 and 2011: The spillway was opened during Mississippi River floods, sending water into the Atchafalaya Basin next to Simmesport and Morganza (Morganza article, above). Local damage is UNCONFIRMED.
- 1989: A federal court plan sent more than 600 state prisoners from parish jails to state prisons, with Avoyelles and Angola chosen to take most of them (Raymond Laborde article, above).
- June 1994: The Tunica-Biloxi Tribe opened Paragon Casino in Marksville, the state's first land-based casino (Marksville article, above). This is a growth event, not a decline.
- 1990 to 2024: Cluster history is available in history_long.csv. Specific job losses are UNCONFIRMED.

## What makes it distinct
A French-Catholic cotton and corn parish where the state prison became a main employer, with a tribal casino on the other side of the ledger.
