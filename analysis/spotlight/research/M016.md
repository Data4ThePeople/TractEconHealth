# M016: Clarksdale, Coahoma County, MS (Delta pick)

## Name and place
Five tracts. Four are inside the city of Clarksdale: 28027950400 (north side), 28027950500 (west), 28027950600 (central, 955 people, 94% Black) and 28027950700 (east side, 4,291 people, 97% Black). The fifth, 28027950300, is the rural area south of the city. It touches Lyon, Dublin and the city edge (Census TIGERweb places layer, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). Hopson Plantation is "south of Clarksdale ... along U.S. Route 49" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopson,_Mississippi), so it is probably in tract 9503. That is UNCONFIRMED. Residents and media call the place Clarksdale, "the birthplace of the blues." The batch file lists 15,195 people.

## Rule-out flags
- None serious. Tract stability is 1.0 ("old tracts line up").
- No institution drives the numbers. In 2020, 204 of 15,850 residents lived in group quarters. That includes 43 in a correctional facility in tract 9504 and 11 in a juvenile facility in 9503 (2020 Census PL file, table P5, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl).
- Coahoma Community College's main campus is outside city limits (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarksdale,_Mississippi). It shows no college dorm population in these tracts.
- The batch file codes it "micro" (small city), not rural. That matters if the series has to be strictly 5 urban and 5 rural.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1900 to 1930: Plantations were split into tenant plots. By 1900 the county had more than 2,600 sharecropping households, the second most in the state, and only 6% of Black farm families owned land. In 1930, 94% of all farmers were tenants or sharecroppers (Mississippi Encyclopedia, https://mississippiencyclopedia.org/entries/coahoma-county/).
- 1920s on: Cotton prices fell after 1920. The Illinois Central depot in Clarksdale became the main departure point for Chicago-bound migrants (Lemann, *The Promised Land*, cited at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarksdale,_Mississippi; https://books.google.com/books?id=FnlIhqBBEMkC).
- 1944: The Hopson Planting Company, just south of town, produced the first cotton crop planted, harvested and baled entirely by machine (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopson,_Mississippi; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_production_in_the_United_States). The Clarksdale Wikipedia article gives 1946 for International Harvester's picker trials on Hopson land (https://web.archive.org/web/20100613165631/http://www.dafvm.msstate.edu/landmarks/07/fall/4-6.pdf). The 1944 date for the full crop is the one most sources use.
- 1950 to 2020: County population peaked at 49,361 in 1950. It was 21,390 in 2020, a 57% drop (decennial census, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coahoma_County,_Mississippi). The city peaked at 21,673 in 1970. It was 14,903 in 2020, a 31% drop (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarksdale,_Mississippi).
- 1958 to 1962: Martin Luther King Jr. held the first major SCLC meeting in Clarksdale on May 29, 1958. In 1960 local pharmacist Aaron Henry became state NAACP president and led a two-year boycott of Clarksdale businesses (Dittmer; Morrison, cited at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarksdale,_Mississippi). Vera Pigee organized for the NAACP here from the 1950s to the 1970s (https://mississippiencyclopedia.org/entries/coahoma-county/).
- April 10, 1967: The Senate poverty subcommittee's hunger tour of the Delta ran from Greenville to Clarksdale and ended at the Clarksdale Neighborhood Center, where about 1,000 people were waiting. Marian Wright had told the subcommittee that mechanization and federal cotton acreage rules had put thousands of sharecroppers out of work (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_S._Clark%27s_and_Robert_F._Kennedy%27s_tour_of_the_Mississippi_Delta).
- 1960s to 1980: More than a third of residents had less than five years of schooling, and the county ranked second in the state in public assistance payments received (Mississippi Encyclopedia, above; the year is not stated, about 1960 to 1980).
- 1990 to 2020: The five tracts went from about 23,800 people in 1990 to 15,850 in 2020. Poverty was 43% in 1990 and 38% in 2020 to 2024. Vacancy rose from 6% to 16% (project file analysis/spotlight/history_long.csv, from NHGIS and ACS).
- Plant closures and the hospital: not confirmed in this pass (UNCONFIRMED). The web search budget ran out.

## Story strength (1-5)
5. The machine that ended sharecropping was first proven here, the hunger hearings ended here, and population, tenancy and poverty all have long public series.

## What makes it distinct
Clarksdale is where the cotton picker's first all-machine crop was grown (1944) and where the 1967 Senate hunger tour ended. It is the clearest single place to show how mechanization emptied the plantation system and left behind a poor, shrinking town.
