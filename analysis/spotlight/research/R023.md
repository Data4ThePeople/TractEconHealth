## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1860: Enslaved people were 93% of Issaquena County's population, the highest share in Mississippi (https://mississippiencyclopedia.org/entries/issaquena-county/).
- 1930: Tenants ran 86% of farms in Issaquena County. Sharkey was one of seven Mississippi counties where tenants ran at least 90% of farms (https://mississippiencyclopedia.org/entries/sharkey-county/).
- 1927: The Mississippi River broke levees in at least 145 places. The flood peaked near Mound Landing, Mississippi, and Arkansas City, Arkansas, and the displacement pushed many Black families into the Great Migration (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mississippi_Flood_of_1927). Which of these counties flooded is UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
- 1962: No Black residents had been allowed to register to vote in East Carroll Parish for 40 years. A federal judge personally registered 28 Black residents in Lake Providence (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Providence,_Louisiana).
- 1960 to 2010: Issaquena County shrank by about 60% to 1,406 people, the smallest county in Mississippi. Sharkey County shrank by more than 50% to 4,916 (Mississippi Encyclopedia entries above). Chicot County has lost population since its 1940 peak (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicot_County,_Arkansas).
- 1977: Unita Blackwell became Mayersville's first mayor (Mississippi Encyclopedia, Issaquena).
- 2011: East Carroll Parish ranked fifth of 3,197 U.S. counties in its poverty rate, at an estimated 44% (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Carroll_Parish,_Louisiana).
- March 24, 2023: An EF4 tornado struck Rolling Fork, killing 17 people in Rolling Fork, Midnight and Silver City (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Fork,_Mississippi).
- Cluster tracts: about 29,800 people in 1980 and 16,600 in 2020, a 44% drop (project file history_long.csv).

3. The history is deep and well documented by county, but the cluster is not one place, and it repeats the Clarksdale and Belzoni stories.

## What makes it distinct
These are the emptiest river counties. Issaquena had the highest share of enslaved people in the state in 1860 and is now Mississippi's smallest county. The 1927 flood and the 2023 Rolling Fork tornado bracket the story.
