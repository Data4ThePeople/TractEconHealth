# R043: South and east Guthrie, Logan County, Oklahoma

## Name and place
- Two tracts in the city of Guthrie (TIGERweb place layers, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov):
  - 40083600200: about 5 square miles, touching Guthrie and Cedar Valley; 2020 population 2,975.
  - 40083601000: about 1.1 square miles inside Guthrie; 2020 population 2,456, 20% Black (493).
- Langston (the historic all-Black town and Langston University) is NOT in these tracts.
- Guthrie's historic Black neighborhood "The Elbow" lay west of South 5th Street and south of West Cleveland Avenue along Cottonwood Creek (City of Guthrie, https://www.guthrieok.com/black-history). ## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1889: Guthrie founded in the Land Run; it becomes territorial capital (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guthrie,_Oklahoma). Many Black settlers came, and the town was once called "Little Africa" (City of Guthrie, https://www.guthrieok.com/black-history).
- 1907-1910: Guthrie is the first state capital; in June 1910 a special election moves the capital to Oklahoma City and the state seal is taken there; courts uphold Oklahoma City by 1911-1912 (Oklahoma Historical Society, https://okhistory.org/learn/capitol1 and https://okhistory.org/learn/capitol6).
- After 1910: Guthrie loses government business and residents, and loses rank as the state's second city (Wikipedia, same page). Census counts by decade: not pulled.
- 1923: Mount Zion Baptist Church built in The Elbow (Abandoned Oklahoma, https://abandonedok.com/the-elbow-ok/).
- 1970s-1980s: Repeated Cottonwood Creek flooding damages homes and bridges in The Elbow, and the neighborhood empties; families remained into the 1980s (OKC Fox, https://okcfox.com/news/local/descendants-of-the-elbow-work-with-city-of-guthrie-to-reopen-access-to-historical-neighborhood).
- Late 1970s: Downtown preservation effort to save the Victorian business district (True West, https://truewestmagazine.com/?p=42917). Details: UNCONFIRMED.
- Present: City and descendants plan a trail and pedestrian bridge into the old Elbow (OKC Fox, same link).

## What makes it distinct
A former state capital whose Black neighborhood on the creek was slowly emptied by floods, now on the edge of the Oklahoma City metro.
