# R021: Charleston, Mississippi County, Missouri (Bootheel)

## Name and place
- One tract, 29133950200 (Census Tract 9502, Mississippi County), about 11.6 square miles of land. 2020 population 4,007 (2020 PL 94-171); candidate file pop 3,425 (ACS).
- The centroid falls in the city of Charleston, the county seat, in Tywappity Township (Census geocoder, https://geocoding.geo.census.gov). Charleston had 5,056 people in 2020 (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_Missouri), so this tract is most of the town.

## Rule-out flags
- Prison check: Charleston is home to the state's Southeast Correctional Center, but this tract has only 91 people in adult correctional facilities in 2020 (likely the county jail), so the state prison is not in this tract (2020 PL 94-171 table P5, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl). Prison location: UNCONFIRMED, but it does not drive this tract.
- Stable since 1990 (1.0).
- Overlap: R028 (East Prairie) is in the same county and R022 (Pemiscot area) is 40 miles south with a similar cotton history. Pick one Bootheel candidate. This one has the strongest single anchor (the 1939 demonstration).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1900s-1930s: State-backed levees and the Little River Drainage District drain thousands of acres for farming; cotton becomes the main crop, worked by tenants and sharecroppers (Wikipedia, Mississippi County, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_County,_Missouri; Missouri Bootheel, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Bootheel). By 1935 most Bootheel farms were tenant-run (same).
- July 3, 1910: Two Black men are lynched in Charleston (Wikipedia, Mississippi County, above).
- 1930-1940: County population jumps from 15,762 to 23,149, its peak (decennial census as tabled in Wikipedia, above).
- January 1939: About 1,500 Black and white sharecroppers, evicted as landlords shifted to wage labor and kept federal crop payments, camp along U.S. 60 and 61 in harsh weather; a state historical marker stands near Charleston. Organized by Rev. Owen Whitfield, with landowner Thad Snow (Historical Marker Database, https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=77862; Southeast Missourian, https://www.semissourian.com/news/fall-of-king-cotton-results-in-strike-2742243). State authorities removed the camps after several days.
- 1940-1970: County population falls from 23,149 to 16,647 as farm mechanization cuts labor needs (census via Wikipedia, above).
- 1994-1995: Brown Shoe announces and carries out closure of its Charleston shoe factory (211 to 300 jobs, sources differ), part of 1,400 regional job losses as Brown exits U.S. shoemaking in 1995 (Southeast Missourian, https://www.semissourian.com/news/shoe-plant-closings-affect-1400-jobs-in-southeast-missouri-2790624 and https://www.semissourian.com/news/fewer-workers-will-lose-jobs-at-charleston-plant-2791055).
- 2012: Gates hydraulic hose plant in Charleston announces closing (Southeast Missourian, https://www.semissourian.com/news/hydraulic-hose-plant-in-charleston-closing-1824482). Job count UNCONFIRMED.
- 2010-2020: Charleston falls from 5,947 to 5,056 residents (Wikipedia, Charleston, above).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. The 1939 demonstration, the 1940 population peak, the farm labor collapse and the 1995 shoe plant closing are dated and each has a public series, though tract-level data only goes back to 1990.

## What makes it distinct
The town where the 1939 sharecroppers' roadside demonstration is marked, followed by a second wave of loss when the factory jobs that replaced farm work left in the 1990s.
