# R044: Franklin (city), Venango County, PA

## Name and place
One tract, 42121200300, inside the city of Franklin, the Venango County seat in Pennsylvania's Oil Region (Census geocoder). The tract covers 1.9 square miles of a roughly 4.6-square-mile city; which part (downtown, south side) is UNCONFIRMED. Population 1,587 (batch file).

## Rule-out flags
- 2020 group quarters: 95 of 1,606, all nursing home (2020 PL file, P5). Not enough to drive the numbers alone, but about 6% of the tract.
- Single small tract; thin documentation of tract-level history.
- Boundary stability 0.997; usable from 1990 only (oldest_tract_year 1990), which limits a long tract series.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1859 to 1860: The Drake well near Titusville set off the oil boom; wells were drilled near Franklin the next year (https://www.venangocountyhistory.org/the-cities-and-oil-creek; https://oilregion.org/heritage/victorian-region/).
- Early 1900s: As oil companies moved west to Texas and Oklahoma, Franklin turned to machinery manufacturing (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin,_Pennsylvania). Year UNCONFIRMED.
- 1919 onward: Joseph Joy patented a mining machine in 1919 and soon bought a Franklin machine shop; Joy mining machinery became the main employer, tying Franklin's jobs to coal mining (Venango Extra, https://venangoextra.com/joy-global-shutting-down-plant-1; date of article UNCONFIRMED).
- 1930 to 2020: Population fell from 10,254 to 6,077, a 41% drop, with a loss in every census since 1930 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin,_Pennsylvania).
- 2013 to 2016: About 700 jobs were cut at Joy's Franklin and Reno plants since 2013; Joy then closed Franklin Plant No. 1, cutting about 130 to 150 union jobs (Venango Extra, above; https://explorevenango.com/up-to-150-union-jobs-lost-in-joy-global-plant-shutdown/). Closure year is likely 2016 but UNCONFIRMED.
- 2017: Komatsu bought Joy Global (https://venangoextra.com/rival-firm-to-acquire-joy-global-for-3-7-billion).

## Cause tags (primary / secondary)

## Story strength (1-5)
2. The century-long slide is real, but most dates are county or city level and the tract can only be tracked from 1990.

## What makes it distinct
A town far from any coal mine that was hit by coal's decline anyway, because its last big employer built mining machines, after an earlier life as an oil boom town.
