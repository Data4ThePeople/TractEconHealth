# U046: Detroit's Near West Side (Core City, Jeffries/Woodbridge, North Corktown, NW Goldberg, Midwest, Chadsey-Condon, lower Cass Corridor), MI

## Name and place
Eight Detroit tracts, population about 12,988, scattered along the Lodge (M-10) and Jeffries (I-96) freeways west of downtown. City of Detroit neighborhood layer: North Corktown (5215), Jeffries (5218), Core City (5220), NW Goldberg (5223), Midtown/lower Cass Corridor (5225), Chadsey Condon (5254), Midwest (5279, 5336) (https://services2.arcgis.com/qvkbeam7Wirps6zC/arcgis/rest/services/Current_City_of_Detroit_Neighborhoods/FeatureServer/0). Local media would call this the Near West Side, or name the pieces.

## Rule-out flags
- Public housing is heavy but spread across several sites, not one complex: Jeffries Homes and the Woodbridge Estates phases (5218), Harriet Tubman (5223), Cornerstone Estates on the old Jeffries East site (5225), Warren West (5254), Woodbridge senior (5215) (HUD layer: https://services.arcgis.com/VTyQ9soqVukalItT/arcgis/rest/services/Public_Housing_Developments/FeatureServer/0). Tract 5218 alone could be driven by Jeffries/Woodbridge.
- Tracts are not contiguous; some are 2 to 3 miles apart.
- Boundaries stable (0.985).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1939: HOLC grades 74% of the sampled area "D" and the rest "C" (Mapping Inequality, our point sample: https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining).
- 1953 to 1955: Jeffries Homes opens with eight 14-story towers, then five more towers and low-rises in 1955 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffries_Projects). HUD dates the Jeffries communities to 1946 (https://archives.hud.gov/news/2008/pr08-036.cfm); start date UNCONFIRMED.
- 1950s: The Lodge Freeway is built through the area; Detroit urban renewal citywide displaced about 43,000 people, 70% Black, by a city council memo (https://www.bridgedetroit.com/city-memo-details-effects-of-urban-renewal-and-i-375-on-detroiters/). Lodge segment dates for this area UNCONFIRMED.
- 1970 to 1977: The Jeffries Freeway (I-96) opens in pieces, from I-75/I-94 in 1970 to Livernois in 1971 and Grand River in 1973 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_96). Home counts displaced: not found.
- 1967: Unrest starts on 12th Street, north of this cluster; effect on these tracts UNCONFIRMED.
- 1997 to 2001: Five Jeffries towers demolished in 1997; four of Jeffries West imploded April 2001 and rebuilt as Woodbridge Estates (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffries_Projects).
- 2008: Demolition of Jeffries East begins for Cornerstone Estates; 252 public housing units replaced by 180 mixed-income homes (https://archives.hud.gov/news/2008/pr08-036.cfm).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. Redlining, two freeways and a tower-block project that was built, torn down and rebuilt are all dated and mapped, though job loss and foreclosure details still need sourcing.

## What makes it distinct
The full life cycle of mid-century tower public housing (built, failed, imploded, rebuilt as mixed income) wrapped by two freeways on land the HOLC had already graded "D."
