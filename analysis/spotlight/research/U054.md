# U054: Tryon Hills / Druid Hills / Camp North End area, Charlotte, NC

## Name and place
Single tract 37119005100 (Mecklenburg County), 2.1 square miles, population about 2,416, in Charlotte's "North End" off Statesville Avenue and North Graham Street. The centroid reverse-geocodes to Tryon Hills (OpenStreetMap). The tract contains Camp North End, 1824 Statesville Ave (Census geocoder match to 37119005100). The former Double Oaks public housing site (now Brightwalk) sits next door in tract 0050 (2501 Statesville Ave geocodes to 37119005000). Exact neighborhood mix (Tryon Hills, Druid Hills, Graham Heights) is UNCONFIRMED.

## Rule-out flags
- Large industrial site (Camp North End) takes much of the land, though it is not a group-quarters institution.
- Stable since 1970 (stability 1.0).
- Rapid redevelopment around Camp North End (https://www.wfae.org/race-equity/2023-01-24/rapid-change-in-charlottes-north-end-makes-residents-eager-and-anxious).
- Same city as U022. Pick one.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1924 to 1932: Ford built a Model T assembly plant on Statesville Avenue (one of about 20 in the 1920s); it closed in 1932 and sat empty for about ten years (https://www.wfae.org/local-news/2017-07-10/former-model-t-missile-factory-up-for-redevelopment-as-part-of-camp-north-end; https://hl.mecknc.gov/Properties/Designated-Historic-Landmarks/charlotte/north-inner/charlotte-quartermaster-depotcharlotte-area-missle-plant).
- 1940s to 1960s: The site became an Army quartermaster depot and then the Charlotte Army Missile Plant (same sources). Closing years and job counts UNCONFIRMED.
- 1950: Double Oaks, a 576-unit housing project, opened on Statesville Avenue next to this tract (https://landdesign.com/northend-charlottes-next-great-neigborhood/; developer-related source).
- 1960s to 1970s: Parts of Statesville Avenue were almost wiped out by urban renewal and highway building (2021 survey cited at https://shpo-images.dncr.nc.gov/ER_18-4136.pdf; years UNCONFIRMED).
- 2008: HUD approved a $10 million loan guarantee for the city to buy 70 acres at Double Oaks, later razed for Brightwalk (https://archives.hud.gov/news/2008/pr08-128.cfm).
- Later decades to 2016: The plant site became a Rite Aid distribution center; ATCO bought it in 2016 and opened Camp North End in 2017 (https://www.axios.com/local/charlotte/2021/04/28/how-camp-north-end-evolved-from-a-missile-assembly-plant-to-a-neighborhood-hotspot-256063). Distribution center closing year and jobs UNCONFIRMED.

3. A documented industrial site and a public housing project next door give dated anchors, but the job losses and neighborhood-level decline are not yet documented with numbers.

## What makes it distinct
A neighborhood that grew around one factory site through four uses (Ford, Army, missiles, warehouse) and a public housing project, and is now being remade by the same site's redevelopment.
