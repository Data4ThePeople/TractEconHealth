# U012: Seventh Ward, St. Roch, Florida Area and Desire Area (New Orleans Upper Ninth and Seventh wards)

## Name and place
Orleans Parish, New Orleans. The city's Neighborhood Statistical Area layer (https://gis.nola.gov/arcgis/rest/services/apps/BoundariesMayorysViewer/MapServer/3) places the tract centroids in:
- Seventh Ward: tracts 28, 30
- St. Roch: tracts 22, 23
- Florida Area: tract 14.01
- Desire Area: tract 137.02
- Pines Village (New Orleans East, just across the Industrial Canal and along I-10): tract 17.20
- Viavant / Venetian Isles (far eastern New Orleans East): tract 17.51

The core is a contiguous strip running from the Seventh Ward along the I-10 corridor through St. Roch to the Florida and Desire public housing sites at the Industrial Canal. TIGER road layers show I-10 crossing six of the eight tracts (all but 14.01 and 22).

Population about 12,970. 2020 census: tracts are 80% to 96% Black, except 17.51 (61%).

## Rule-out flags
- No single institution. 2020 group quarters are small: the largest is 204 people in tract 17.51 (31 military). No prison, college or base dominates.
- Tract 17.51 (Viavant / Venetian Isles, 16 square miles, about 1,044 people) is far from the core and has a different story (marsh, fishing camps, industrial land along I-10 and US 90). Recommend dropping it from the cluster or treating it as an outlier.
- The Agriculture Street Landfill (29.98889 N, 90.04194 W per Wikipedia) falls in 2020 tract 137.01, next to this cluster's 137.02 and 14.01, not inside it. The story can still use it, but the data has to say "next door."
- Stability is good (0.964). The Data Center and NHGIS crosswalks will be needed because Katrina-era redraws split some tracts (2020 numbers like 137.02, 17.20, 17.51 are splits).
- The 2000 to 2010 population drops are heavily Katrina-driven. The editor wants a story that does not lean on Katrina alone, and this cluster can show decline from 1960 to 2000 before the storm.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1909: The city begins dumping on low ground at Agriculture Street, which becomes the main city dump. Reclosed officially in 1966 after reuse for Hurricane Betsy debris. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_Street_Landfill
- 1918 to 1923: The Industrial Canal is dredged (begun June 6, 1918) and opens May 5, 1923, cutting the Upper Ninth Ward off from the Lower Ninth. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Canal
- 1946: The Florida public housing development opens, 734 units on 18.5 acres, "largely isolated from the rest of the community." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Projects
- 1956: The Desire public housing development opens (about 1,860 units on 98.5 acres) on a former cypress swamp and dumping ground bordered by the Industrial Canal, railroad tracks and industrial plants. A tenants' report six weeks before opening called it "unsafe for human habitation." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desire_Projects
- 1965: Hurricane Betsy puts Desire under six feet of water. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desire_Projects
- 1969: The elevated six-lane I-10 is built over Claiborne Avenue, replacing the oak-lined neutral ground. Wikipedia says nearly 500 homes were destroyed and businesses closed; the Seventh Ward's Black commercial strip ran along Claiborne. St. Roch "declined sharply" after I-10 was built through it (no date given). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claiborne_Avenue ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Roch,_New_Orleans
- 1978 onward: Press Park, Gordon Plaza and Moton Elementary are built on top of the old landfill. EPA investigations start in 1986; the site is listed on the Superfund National Priorities List on December 16, 1994. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_Street_Landfill
- 1979 to 1999: HUD rates the housing authority "troubled" (1979). In 1994 a HUD audit finds all 150 sampled Desire units fail basic standards, and Florida records 26 slayings. Desire is demolished from about 1997 under HOPE VI; Florida is partly demolished 1998 to 2004. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desire_Projects ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Projects
- 2005 onward: Katrina floods the area; a breach on the Upper Ninth side of the Industrial Canal near Florida Avenue. Population falls: St. Roch from 11,975 (2000) to 6,632 (2010), down 44.6%; Seventh Ward from 16,955 to 10,187, down 39.9%; the Florida development's census population from 1,604 to 6. Gordon Plaza residents campaign for decades for relocation; the city later clears the subdivision for a solar farm (2024 report). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Roch,_New_Orleans ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Ward_of_New_Orleans ; https://lailluminator.com/2024/10/31/superfund-solar-farm/

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
5. Every cause the editor named for New Orleans (I-10 over Claiborne, public housing built and then torn down, the Industrial Canal, the Agriculture Street landfill) touches this cluster, each with a date and a public record, and the decline is visible in census data well before 2005.

## What makes it distinct
A neighborhood hemmed in by infrastructure, one layer at a time: a shipping canal (1923), a city dump turned housing site, two of the largest public housing projects in the South (1946, 1956), then an elevated interstate (1969), with Katrina as the last cut rather than the first. Strongest New Orleans candidate in this batch.
