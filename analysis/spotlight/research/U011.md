# U011: North Memphis (New Chicago, Klondike-Smokey City, south Frayser), Memphis, TN

## Name and place
- Shelby County, TN. 8 tracts, pop 17,105. Centroid 35.1993, -90.0179.
- Tract checks with the Census geocoder (https://geocoding.geo.census.gov):
  - 000400 holds 1101 Breedlove St and 1000-1300 Chelsea Ave. Breedlove Street is the line between Smokey City and Klondike (https://fromcommonground.com/?p=14633). So 000400 and 001900 are Klondike-Smokey City.
  - 000300 (35.177, -90.032) sits west of there, in the New Chicago area around the old Firestone plant. The site is a 71-acre brownfield (https://dailymemphian.com/article/53930/the-former-firestone-site-is-a-brownfield). The exact parcel-to-tract match is UNCONFIRMED.
  - 009902 holds 3200 Thomas St. 010120 holds 2500-3000 N Watkins St and 2800 Thomas St. 010121 holds 1500 Frayser Blvd. 010122 holds 1200 Whitney Ave. These are south Frayser and the Thomas Street corridor.
  - 011200 (35.165, -90.015) is east Klondike. The local name is UNCONFIRMED.
- Residents and local media say "North Memphis," "New Chicago," "Klondike/Smokey City" and "Frayser."
- Institutions: the 2020 PL 94-171 table P5 shows no correctional, college or military group quarters. Group quarters total 81 people across the 8 tracts. No single institution drives the numbers.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1936 or 1937: Firestone opens its tire plant in New Chicago. The Wikipedia North Memphis page gives January 19, 1937 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Memphis,_Memphis,_Tennessee). A Common Ground article gives 1936 (https://fromcommonground.com/?p=14633). The plant grew from a few hundred to more than 3,000 workers.
- 1942: International Harvester builds a plant in Frayser. Wikipedia calls it the largest farm-equipment plant in the South, on 260 acres (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Memphis,_Memphis,_Tennessee; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frayser,_Memphis). Frayser's population grew from 2,450 in 1940 to 16,000 in 1953.
- 1958: Memphis annexes Frayser (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frayser,_Memphis).
- Late 1950s to 1960s: I-40 cuts through Klondike-Smokey City and removes a Kroger and other stores. The exact year is UNCONFIRMED (https://fromcommonground.com/?p=14633).
- 1983: International Harvester closes its Frayser plant. A month later, Firestone announces it will close its North Memphis plant, which Common Ground says took an additional 2,000 jobs (https://fromcommonground.com/?p=14633). Wikipedia dates the Firestone closing to March 1983 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frayser,_Memphis). Note that sources disagree on the Harvester year: Wikipedia's Frayser page says 1985, and a Wisconsin Historical Society record gives Memphis Works an operating span of 1946 to 1985 (https://wisconsinhistory.org/Records/Image/IM45842). Treat the Harvester year as UNCONFIRMED (1983 or 1985).
- 1980 to 2010: Klondike-Smokey City population fell from 11,647 to 5,290, a 54.6% drop (https://fromcommonground.com/?p=14633).
- Other North Memphis plant closings: Cleo, Schering-Plough, Maybelline, Velsicol, Kimberly-Clark and Chemtura. No dates are given (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Memphis,_Memphis,_Tennessee).
- Nearby pollution: Velsicol's plant at 1199 Warford St, about 3 miles east in tract 011100 (outside this cluster), discharged into Cypress Creek before 1963. It sent waste to the North Hollywood Dump, a Superfund site. Manufacturing stopped in 2011 or 2012 (https://tennesseelookout.com/2022/11/14/velsicol-closed-its-chemical-plant-10-years-ago-memphis-still-endures-its-toxic-legacy/; https://protectouraquifer.org/issues/velsicol-chemical). Whether Cypress Creek runs through this cluster is UNCONFIRMED.
- 2016: Northside High School closes (https://fromcommonground.com/?p=14633).
- Memphis was HOLC-graded (https://dailymemphian.com/article/35310). These tracts' grades are UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5)
4. The two big plant closings have dated sources and a job count, and they stack with the I-40 cut and the population loss. The redlining grade and the Harvester closing year still need checking.

## What makes it distinct
Of the two Memphis candidates, this is the more distinct one. A tire plant and the South's largest farm-equipment plant, each employing thousands, closed within about two years of each other. The neighborhoods built around them then lost more than half their people. No other candidate in this batch has an urban mass-layoff story like this.
