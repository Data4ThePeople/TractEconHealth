# R027: Greene County and the western Black Belt (Eutaw, Boligee, Forkland; plus Hale, Sumter and Noxubee edges), AL/MS

## Name and place
Eight rural tracts, population about 16,344: all three Greene County, AL tracts (0600.00, 0601.01, 0602.00, which include Eutaw, the county seat, and Boligee/Forkland areas), three Hale County tracts (0403, 0404.01, 0404.02, west and south Hale; 0404.02 is 5.6 square miles and may be part of Greensboro, UNCONFIRMED), northern Sumter County (0114) and northern Noxubee County, MS (9501, near Macon, MS, UNCONFIRMED). Residents call the core "Greene County" and "Eutaw."

## Rule-out flags
- No prison or college found in these tracts. The University of West Alabama in Livingston appears to sit south of Sumter tract 0114 (centroid 32.85, -88.25), UNCONFIRMED.
- Stability 0.971 since 1990: good.
- Crosses a state line (Noxubee, MS). Consider trimming to the Greene and Hale tracts.
- Story overlaps with R036 and R038 (all Black Belt). This is the strongest of the three for a pure Black Belt story.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1860 to 1960s: Greene County population peaked around 1850 and fell for a century as farm work declined, rural workers left for cities, and machines replaced sharecroppers in cotton (https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/h-1329; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_County,_Alabama). Hale County fell from 31,011 in 1900 to 15,604 in 1980 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hale_County,_Alabama; verify against the decennial census).
- 1936: Walker Evans and James Agee documented tenant farm poverty in Hale County (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hale_County,_Alabama).
- 1965: White families founded Warrior Academy, a private segregation academy in Eutaw; it stayed all-white until 2004 (History of Education Quarterly article, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/history-of-education-quarterly/article/private-school-pivot-the-shrouded-persistence-of-massive-resistance-in-the-black-belt-and-beyond/3A8D5962A128010771C4F84DDA659C1D).
- July 30, 1969: Greene County became the first in the South since Reconstruction with Black majorities on both the county commission and school board (https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/h-1329). The public school system became nearly all-Black (ProPublica Miseducation: https://projects.propublica.org/miseducation/district/0101680).
- Mid-1900s on: Timber companies planted large pine tracts in Hale County while Greensboro's economy declined; many manufacturing plants closed in the late 20th century (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hale_County,_Alabama). Plant names and years UNCONFIRMED.
- 2000: A catfish processing plant opened in Eutaw (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eutaw,_Alabama). Later status UNCONFIRMED.
- 2020: Greene County counted 7,730 people, the least populous county in Alabama (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_County,_Alabama).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. The plantation, mechanization, out-migration and school-split arc is well documented with century-long census series, though the plant closures and health-care history need more digging.

## What makes it distinct
The county that elected Black local government in 1969 and then watched white families and tax base move to a private academy, while the population kept draining away for a century.
