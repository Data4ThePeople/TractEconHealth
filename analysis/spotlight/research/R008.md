# R008: Black Mesa area, Navajo Nation and Hopi Reservation (Piñon, Forest Lake, Whippoorwill, Kayenta, Shonto, Nazlini, Cornfields chapters; part of Hopi Reservation), Navajo and Apache counties, AZ

## Name and place
Eight tracts in Navajo and Apache counties, population about 19,679, spread over several thousand square miles. Tract internal points fall in these Navajo Nation chapters and areas (Census geocoder): 9400.08 Forest Lake Chapter; 9400.10 Whippoorwill Chapter; 9400.11 Piñon Chapter; 9423 Shonto Chapter; 9424 Kayenta Chapter; 9443.02 Nazlini Chapter; 9449.02 Cornfields Chapter; and 9400.12 on the Hopi Reservation (Hopi CCD). The cluster circles Black Mesa (Dziłíjiin in Diné; Hopi villages sit on its southern mesas). The people call themselves Diné (Navajo) and Hopi (Hopituh Shi-nu-mu). The cluster mixes two sovereign nations with a long land dispute between them; any story must treat both carefully.

## Rule-out flags
- **Two nations in one cluster.** One tract (9400.12) is on the Hopi Reservation; the rest are Navajo Nation. Consider splitting, or telling the story as a shared one with Hopi voices included.
- Boundaries: stability 0.646, shifted since 1990; tracts start in 1990 here.
- No prison, college or base found. Kayenta and Chinle-area schools and IHS facilities are not large enough to drive the numbers (UNCONFIRMED).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1882 and 1934: The Hopi (1882) and Navajo (1934) reservations overlapped, creating a Joint Use Area and decades of litigation (https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12953.html).
- 1944 to 1986: Uranium mining on Navajo lands (mostly in other parts of the Nation); 3,000 to 5,000 Navajo workers (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium_mining_and_the_Navajo_people). Mines in this cluster: UNCONFIRMED; check the EPA mine list.
- 1964 and 1966: Peabody signed coal leases with the Navajo Tribe (1964) and Hopi Tribe (1966) on Black Mesa; until 2005 a slurry line pumped about 3 million gallons a day from the Navajo Aquifer (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mesa_Peabody_Coal_controversy).
- 1966 to 2009: The Bennett Freeze barred building and repairs on about 1.5 million acres of the former Joint Use Area, affecting nearly 10,000 Navajo people (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_Freeze). The freeze area lies mostly west of this cluster (Tuba City and Cameron area): UNCONFIRMED.
- 1974 to 1986 and after: The Navajo-Hopi Land Settlement Act (P.L. 93-531) led to a 1977 partition and the relocation of Navajo families off Hopi Partitioned Lands (and about 100 Hopi from Navajo Partitioned Lands); relocation was meant to end by 1986 but continued for decades (https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12953.html; https://www.indian.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/CHRG-109shrg22642.pdf). Estimates of Navajo people moved range from 6,000 to about 12,000 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_Freeze; UNCONFIRMED higher figure). Forest Lake, Piñon and Whippoorwill lie near the partition line: UNCONFIRMED.
- December 31, 2005: Black Mesa Mine closed along with the Mohave Generating Station (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mesa_Peabody_Coal_controversy).
- August and November 2019: Kayenta Mine (August) and Navajo Generating Station (November) closed. The Navajo Nation estimated a $30 to $50 million revenue loss; the Hopi Tribe said 80% to 85% of its general fund was affected (https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/resources/Imminent-Public-Budget-Crisis-Confronts-Hopi-Navajo-Tribes-With-Coal-Economy-Collapse_May-2019.pdf; https://cronkitenews.azpbs.org/2019/11/08/srp-confirms-that-last-day-for-navajo-generating-station-just-days-away/). Mine workers were not offered transfers (same IEEFA source).

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. A federal land partition and relocation, decades of frozen or stalled development, and the 2005 and 2019 coal closures are all dated with records, but tract data only start in 1990.

## What makes it distinct
Coal mined under people's homes for 50 years paid tribal budgets while federal land rulings forced families to move, and then the mines closed in 2005 and 2019.
