# U042: Amani, Park West, Franklin Heights and north Lindsay Heights (ZIP 53206), north side of Milwaukee, Wisconsin

## Name and place
- Eight Milwaukee County tracts: 55079004600, 004700, 006300, 006500, 008800, 008900, 009000, 009800. 2020 population about 17,330 (2020 PL 94-171); candidate file pop 16,006.
- Tract bounding boxes run from about North Avenue (south) to just below Capitol Drive (north), and from about 16th Street to 35th Street (TIGERweb, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). This is the core of ZIP 53206 on Milwaukee's north side. Neighborhood names residents use here include Amani, Park West, Franklin Heights and Lindsay Heights; the exact match of each tract to each name is UNCONFIRMED.
- The former A.O. Smith frame plant (27th to 35th streets, Townsend Street to Capitol Drive) borders the cluster on the northwest (WUWM, 2008, https://www.wuwm.com/post/53206-evolution-zip-code).

## Rule-out flags
- None serious. Group quarters are small: 101 people in an adult correctional facility in tract 008900 (likely a transitional or community corrections site, name UNCONFIRMED) out of about 17,330 (2020 PL 94-171 table P5, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl).
- Tracts are stable since 1990 (stability 1.0) and the oldest tract data is 1970.
- No single public housing complex or institution dominates.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1930s-1950s: The area was a white, largely German working-class district; a resident recalled it as "mainly Germans" about 50 years before 2008 (WUWM, 2008, https://www.wuwm.com/post/53206-evolution-zip-code). HOLC graded Milwaukee in the 1930s; the grade for these specific blocks needs a check on Mapping Inequality (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining). UNCONFIRMED.
- 1960s: Urban renewal and construction of I-43 (the North-South Freeway) and the Park West Freeway forced more than 8,000 homeowners and businesses to sell or vacate; many displaced Black residents moved north into 53206 (TCLF, Lindsay Heights, https://tclf.org/lindsay-heights; WUWM 2008). The Park West Freeway was later abandoned after clearing much of the neighborhood (TCLF).
- July 30, 1967: Civil disorder in the Inner Core (Michigan to Capitol, Holton to 20th). Four deaths, about 100 injuries, a citywide 24-hour curfew and National Guard deployment (Encyclopedia of Milwaukee, https://emke.uwm.edu/entry/civil-disorder-of-1967/).
- 1967-1968: Open housing marches across the city; 2017 reporting ties the freeway to white flight and isolation of central city neighborhoods (Urban Milwaukee / NNS, https://urbanmilwaukee.com/2017/06/27/50-years-after-the-marches-residential-segregation-still-a-problem/).
- 1979-2000: The city lost 47% of its manufacturing jobs (Milwaukee Journal Sentinel figure as cited by Milwaukee Independent, https://www.milwaukeeindependent.com/featured/coming-storm-greatest-fears-future-black-milwaukee/). UWM economist Marc Levine estimated about 55% of employed Black men in 1970s Milwaukee worked in manufacturing (WUWM, https://www.wuwm.com/post/history-helps-explain-connection-between-segregation-and-concentrated-poverty-milwaukee). In nearby Metcalfe Park, residents working in manufacturing fell from 2,949 to 496, 1970-2000 (same WUWM piece). A.O. Smith's plant at the edge of the cluster now sits mostly vacant; closure year UNCONFIRMED in what I found (the frame business went to Tower Automotive in 1997 and the plant closed later; check).
- 2005-2006: 51 homicides recorded in 53206 (WUWM, 2008).
- 2000s-2010s: UWM study found 53206 has one of the city's highest incarceration rates and is one of its poorest areas (WUWM 2019, https://www.wuwm.com/post/report-highlights-major-economic-disparities-milwaukees-53206). The often repeated claim that it is the most incarcerated ZIP in the nation is not supported (PolitiFact, https://politifact.com/?p=307929).
- 2008-2012 foreclosure wave in Milwaukee's north side: likely, but not confirmed in what I found. UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
5. Freeway displacement, the 1967 disorder, the factory job collapse and mass incarceration are each dated and documented, and each has a public data series at tract or ZIP level.

## What makes it distinct
A neighborhood that filled with families pushed north by freeway clearance just as its factory jobs began to disappear, ending as the city's most studied example of incarceration and male joblessness.
