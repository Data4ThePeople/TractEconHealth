# U010: Central and South Akron along the Innerbelt, Ohio

## Name and place

Akron, Summit County, Ohio. Eight tracts that ring downtown on the south, west and north, centered on the route of the Akron Innerbelt (SR 59) near Opportunity Parkway. Neighborhood names below are approximate, from tract internal points; confirm with the City of Akron neighborhood map.
- 39153505600: Summit Lake area, south of downtown.
- 39153501900, 39153505300: South Akron / Lane-Wooster area.
- 39153506800: downtown south, along the Innerbelt. Holds the Summit County Jail (539 people in adult correctional group quarters in 2020).
- 39153504200: east of downtown (28 in correctional quarters).
- 39153508301: north of downtown (138 college dorm residents, likely University of Akron housing).
- 39153506600: north side (North Hill edge).
- 39153508600: West Akron (Copley Road side).

2020 census population: 14,018.

## Rule-out flags

- Tract 5068 includes the county jail: 539 of 2,196 residents there, about 4% of the cluster. Not enough to drive cluster numbers, but exclude group quarters when charting that tract.
- Tracts 5066 and 5086 were graded A or B by HOLC and may not share the core story. The cluster is spread out; consider trimming to the Innerbelt and Summit Lake tracts.

## History (dated bullets with sources)

- **About 1937 to 1940: HOLC grades.** Our overlay of Mapping Inequality polygons (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining): Summit Lake tract 37% D and 34% industrial; tract 5068 49% D; tract 5042 41% D; south side tracts mostly C; tract 5086 56% A and tract 5066 98% B.
- **1960: city peak.** Akron peaked at 290,351 people and lost 34.6% by 2020 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron,_Ohio, from census).
- **1968 to 1986: Innerbelt.** Land buying began as early as 1968; sections opened 1972, 1978 and 1986. Only 2.24 of a planned 21.5 miles were built. It displaced thousands of residents, many low-income and minority, and over 100 businesses (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron_Innerbelt).
- **1978 to 1987: rubber leaves.** Goodyear converted its original Akron plant to a technical center in 1978 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_Tire_and_Rubber_Company). Firestone moved its headquarters out of Akron in 1987 and was sold to Bridgestone in 1988 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firestone_Tire_and_Rubber_Company). By the early 1990s Goodyear was the last major tire maker based in Akron (Akron Wikipedia page). Dates when each company stopped building passenger tires in Akron and job counts: UNCONFIRMED.
- **2000 to 2007:** the number of rubber workers fell by roughly half (Akron Wikipedia page).
- **2008: foreclosure.** Akron resident Addie Polk, 90, shot herself during a foreclosure in 2008 and became a national symbol of the crisis (Akron Wikipedia page). Local foreclosure counts: UNCONFIRMED.
- **2016 onward: Innerbelt closed.** The northern section closed; construction to decommission it began in early 2017, the city took the land in 2021, and a master plan was released March 23, 2026 (Innerbelt Wikipedia page).
- **2016: overdose spike** tied to carfentanil in Summit County: UNCONFIRMED (not checked; search budget ran out).

## Story strength (1-5)

4. The rubber collapse and the Innerbelt are dated and well recorded, and the road's removal gives the story a current chapter. Local job counts and some dates still need checking.

## What makes it distinct

The "Rubber Capital" lost its tire plants, and a highway built through its neighborhoods was later closed and given back as empty land. The story of the highway now being removed is unusual.
