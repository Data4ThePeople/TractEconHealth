# U006: Shreveport's old Black core: Ledbetter Heights ("the Bottoms"), Allendale, Lakeside and Stoner Hill, plus the MLK and Cooper Road area to the north and a piece of old Bossier City

## Name and place
Eight tracts, from TIGERweb extents. The neighborhood names come from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul%27s_Bottoms and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeside,_Shreveport,_Louisiana. Assigning names to tracts is UNCONFIRMED and should be checked against a city neighborhood map.
- 22017025300: downtown's west edge and Ledbetter Heights, the former St. Paul's Bottoms.
- 22017025200 and 22017020600: Allendale and Lakeside, west of downtown.
- 22017021100: south of downtown along the river, probably Stoner Hill.
- 22017020500: north across Cross Bayou, probably the Cooper Road area.
- 22017024601 and 22017024602: the Martin Luther King Drive area of north Shreveport. 24601 probably holds Southern University at Shreveport, which opened in 1967 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University_at_Shreveport).
- 22015011300: old Bossier City across the Red River. This is a different city.

## Rule-out flags

- Tract 246.01 has 118 people in college housing, too few to drive the numbers.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- Early 1900s: St. Paul's Bottoms, west of downtown, was a Black district of hotels, offices and saloons, and a legal red-light district where Lead Belly lived and played (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul%27s_Bottoms; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledbetter_Heights).
- 1920s and 1930s: Lakeside's shotgun houses had no indoor plumbing and rented for $1.25 to $2.50 a week on unpaved streets (Lakeside article, above).
- Late 1930s: HOLC graded the Bottoms, Allendale and the area north of Cross Bayou "D," the lowest grade. By our overlay of Mapping Inequality polygons on 2020 tracts, D areas cover about 52% of tract 206, 42% of 252, 29% of 205 and 29% of 253 (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/). Tract 211 was mostly graded B.
- 1951: Elamito Terrace (later Jackson Heights), the area's first public housing complex, opened. It was torn down before 2006 (Lakeside article, above).
- 1960s: Interstate 20 was built through the city (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport,_Louisiana). Its exact path through these tracts is UNCONFIRMED. In 1963, the home of civil rights leader Dr. C. O. Simpkins was bombed (same source).
- 1960s to 1980s: The Castle Hotel, which hosted Martin Luther King Jr., was hit by fires and crime and was later demolished. Many Bottoms buildings were torn down (St. Paul's Bottoms article, above).
- 1980s to 2012: Oil jobs collapsed in the 1980s, and United Gas had already left. Western Electric's plant, with about 7,500 workers at its peak, closed in 2001. General Motors' Shreveport plant closed in August 2012 (Shreveport article, above).
- 1970 to 2020: Cluster population fell from about 38,100 to 19,500, a 49% drop. Poverty was 48% in 1970 and 48% in 2020 to 2024 (project file history_long.csv).
- 2020s: A proposed I-49 "inner-city connector" through this area is under debate (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport,_Louisiana mentions plans unveiled in 2020). The route through Allendale is UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5)
4. Redlining maps, public housing, demolitions, an interstate, an oil bust and two big plant closures are each dated and documented. The jail tract and the Bossier tract need trimming.

## What makes it distinct
It was a Black district famous for its music (Lead Belly's "Bottoms"). It was graded "D," cleared and cut off, and then hit by an oil bust in an oil town. Poverty has stayed near 48% for 50 years while half the people left.
