## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1871: Huntington founded as the western end of the C&O Railway (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington,_West_Virginia).
- 1937: The Ohio River flood made 25,000 of 40,000 people in flooded areas refugees; floodwalls were built from 1938 (same source).
- 1950: City population peaked at 86,353. It fell to 46,842 in 2020, a 46% drop (same source, census table).
- 1970s: Federal urban renewal cleared several parts of downtown (same source).
- Early 1980s: Steel and manufacturing layoffs and plant closures hit the region (same source). ACF's Huntington railcar plant once employed as many as 1,600 people; most buildings were demolished in 2024 (same source).
- 1993: The Owens-Brockway glass container plant closed after 80 years, costing more than 600 jobs (e-WV, https://www.wvencyclopedia.org/entries/717).
- 1980s to 1990s: Fairfield, a traditionally Black working-class area cut off from richer areas by the railroad, deteriorated as crack cocaine spread (WV Living, https://wvliving.com/a-city-on-the-mend/). Decade UNCONFIRMED.
- 2015 to 2016: Fentanyl arrived in early 2015 (STAT, https://www.statnews.com/2016/08/22/heroin-huntington-west-virginia-overdoses/). On August 15, 2016, dozens of people overdosed within hours; reported counts range from 26 to 28 (UNCONFIRMED exact count; https://www.ksl.com/article/41341721/4-hours-in-huntington-how-the-heroin-epidemic-choked-a-city).
- 2017: The city sued opioid distributors including McKesson, Cardinal Health and AmerisourceBergen (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington,_West_Virginia).
