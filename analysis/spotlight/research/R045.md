# R045: Northern Cheyenne Reservation (Lame Deer, Busby, Birney) and the Decker area, MT

## Name and place
Two tracts. Rosebud County tract 9404 (378.7 sq mi) holds Lame Deer, the seat of the Northern Cheyenne Tribe, in the Lame Deer District. Big Horn County tract 9404 (925.5 sq mi) holds Busby (Census geocoder) and runs south into the Decker CCD, home of the Decker coal mine. The population-weighted centroid falls in the Birney District of the Northern Cheyenne Indian Reservation. The people call themselves Tsitsistas and So'taeo'o; the tribe's name is the Northern Cheyenne Tribe. Population 4,487 (ACS); in 2020 about 90% American Indian. Note: the batch description lists "Crow and Northern Cheyenne areas in Big Horn County," but this cluster is a Northern Cheyenne story. Whether any Crow Reservation land falls in Big Horn tract 9404 is UNCONFIRMED.

## Rule-out flags
- No prison, college or base (2020 group quarters 21, mostly juvenile).
- Stable boundaries to 1990.
- The Big Horn tract mixes reservation land and the off-reservation Decker mine area, whose workers mostly lived in Wyoming ([Washington Times/AP, 2021](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2021/feb/21/court-approves-deal-between-union-bankrupt-coal-mi/)).
- Series likely includes at most one reservation. This is weaker than Pine Ridge (R046) and Blackfeet (R006) on documented land loss.

## History
- **1877-1879:** 972 Cheyenne were moved to Oklahoma; in 1878 about 297 started back north, and in 1879 the Fort Robinson outbreak followed after soldiers withheld food, water and heat ([Montana Historical Society, Northern Cheyenne Timeline](https://mhs.mt.gov/education/IEFA/NorthernCheyenneTimeline.pdf)).
- **1884:** Executive order created the reservation on the Tongue River; extended by executive order on March 19, 1900 ([MHS timeline](https://mhs.mt.gov/education/IEFA/NorthernCheyenneTimeline.pdf); [American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-northern-cheyenne-reserve); [Montana Indian Law](https://indianlaw.mt.gov/fedstate/acts/cheyenne)).
- **1919-1929:** BIA killed the Cheyenne horse herd from about 15,000 to 3,000 to make room for a tribal cattle herd, which the BIA ended as a failure in 1924 ([MHS timeline](https://mhs.mt.gov/education/IEFA/NorthernCheyenneTimeline.pdf)).
- **1926 and 1930-1932:** The Allotment Act passed; all 1,457 enrolled members got 160-acre allotments ([MHS timeline](https://mhs.mt.gov/education/IEFA/NorthernCheyenneTimeline.pdf)). Unlike many reservations, no surplus land was opened to settlers in the timeline (UNCONFIRMED as a general claim; check BIA land tenure).
- **1950:** 50 children and elders died in a German measles epidemic ([MHS timeline](https://mhs.mt.gov/education/IEFA/NorthernCheyenneTimeline.pdf)).
- **1966-1980:** Coal permit sales leased over 56% of the reservation; the tribal council voted to cancel them in 1973; in 1976 the tribe won Class I air status applied to Colstrip; a 1980 act of Congress cancelled the leases ([MHS timeline](https://mhs.mt.gov/education/IEFA/NorthernCheyenneTimeline.pdf)).
- **1976-2021:** Coal came to Big Horn County in 1976 and ran the county budget for decades ([Strong Towns, 2021](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2021/6/2/rbc2021-big-horn-county-montana-leaving-coal-behind)). Decker's owner filed for bankruptcy in December 2020 and 145 jobs were cut by February 2021 ([Mining Monthly](https://www.miningmonthly.com/archive/news/1271619/decker-workers-lose-jobs); [Sheridan Press](https://newslj.com/decker-cuts-70-workers-parent-company-files-bankruptcy); county finance effects in [YPR](https://www.ypradio.org/energy/2021-02-02/eastern-montana-county-reevaluates-finances-following-coal-upheavals)).

## Story strength
3. The treaty, removal, and coal-lease history is well documented, but the coal chapter is a story of the tribe keeping coal out, so the compounding decline is less clear than at Pine Ridge or Blackfeet.

## What makes it distinct
A nation that walked home from Oklahoma, won its land back by executive order, then fought off coal leases on more than half its land while coal ringed the reservation.
