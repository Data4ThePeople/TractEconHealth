# R022: Pemiscot County Bootheel (Caruthersville, Hayti area) plus pieces of Lake County, Tennessee, Fulton County, Kentucky, and New Madrid County, Missouri

## Name and place
- Eight tracts across three states along the Mississippi River (Census geocoder, https://geocoding.geo.census.gov; TIGERweb, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov):
  - Pemiscot County, Missouri: 29155470100 (Concord Township, rural north of Hayti), 470200 (Hayti), 470400 (Little Prairie Township, Caruthersville area).
  - New Madrid County, Missouri: 29143960400 (La Font Township, rural).
  - Lake County, Tennessee: 47095960100 (Tiptonville area) and 960200.
  - Fulton County, Kentucky: 21075960100 (Fulton area) and 960200 (Hickman area).
- 2020 population about 23,060 (2020 PL 94-171); candidate file pop 22,046.

## Rule-out flags
- Prison: tract 47095960100 has 2,062 people in adult correctional facilities out of 4,872 (2020 PL 94-171 P5, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl). This is very likely the Northwest Correctional Complex near Tiptonville (facility name UNCONFIRMED in my searches). Tract 21075960200 (Hickman) has 471 more in correctional facilities. Together about 11% of the cluster.
- The cluster spans three states and four counties with different histories (Missouri cotton, Kentucky rail and river towns, a Tennessee prison town).
- Recommend reworking to the three Pemiscot County tracts (about 7,450 people), which carry the Bootheel cotton story, or folding this into R021 as the single Bootheel pick.
- Stability 1.0 for all tracts.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1880-1930: Bootheel population more than triples as cotton spreads onto drained swamp land and workers are brought in (Wikipedia, Missouri Bootheel, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Bootheel).
- Early 1900s: Four Black men lynched in Caruthersville (Wikipedia, Pemiscot County, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pemiscot_County,_Missouri).
- 1940: Pemiscot County peaks at 46,857 people, one of Missouri's most populous counties then (census via Wikipedia, above).
- January 1939: Bootheel sharecroppers' roadside demonstration on U.S. 60 and 61 (Historical Marker Database, https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=77862). Participation by Pemiscot families: UNCONFIRMED.
- 1940-1970: Pemiscot falls to 26,373 (minus 44%) as mechanization cuts farm labor and Black families leave in the Great Migration (census via Wikipedia, above).
- 1995: Brown Shoe closes its Caruthersville plant, 412 workers (Southeast Missourian, https://www.semissourian.com/news/shoe-plant-closings-affect-1400-jobs-in-southeast-missouri-2790624; https://www.semissourian.com/news/towns-in-region-cope-with-jobs-loss-from-shoe-plant-closures-2765802). A riverboat casino (Aztar) gave some laid-off workers first chance at jobs.
- April 2, 2006: F3 tornado damages about 60% of Caruthersville; 141 to 205 single-family homes destroyed, high school badly damaged (Southeast Missourian, https://www.semissourian.com/news/caruthersville-crushed-more-than-half-the-town-sees-destruction-1146791; https://www.semissourian.com/news/insurance-agencies-send-rapid-response-and-catastrophe-teams-to-disasters-1152991).
- 2013: Pemiscot Memorial Health Systems in Hayti, the county's only hospital, loses more than $3 million and nearly closes several times (from search summary; primary source UNCONFIRMED).
- 2020: Pemiscot at 15,661, a 67% drop from the 1940 peak (census via Wikipedia).
- Fulton, Kentucky: once a hub of the Illinois Central main line where "more than 70%" of U.S. bananas passed through; population falls from 3,502 in 1930 to 2,357 in 2020 (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulton,_Kentucky). Dates of the decline of the rail and banana trade: UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
3 as drawn, 4 if cut to the Pemiscot tracts. The Pemiscot arc (1940 peak, mechanization, shoe plant, tornado) is dated and charted easily, but the three-state cluster with a prison tract muddies it.

## What makes it distinct
A county that lost two-thirds of its people since 1940, the steepest long run loss in this batch, with a factory closing and a tornado stacked on the farm labor collapse.
