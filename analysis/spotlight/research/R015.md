## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1911: First rail shipment of coal from Harlan County; production boomed after (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlan_County,_Kentucky).
- 1910 and 1917: International Harvester founded Benham; U.S. Steel's subsidiary founded Lynch. By the 1940s the complex employed more than 4,000 (https://sites.google.com/site/visitappalachia/recommended-readings/harlan-a-century-of-mining; https://appalachianprojects.as.uky.edu/coal-camps/lynch).
- May 5, 1931: Battle of Evarts, part of the 1930s Harlan County mine wars (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlan_County,_Kentucky).
- 1940: County population peaked at 75,275; 26,831 in 2020, a 64% drop (same source, census table).
- 1950 to 1965: Mining jobs fell from 13,619 to 2,433, an 82% drop, as mechanization spread (Harlan mining history citing A New History of Kentucky, https://sites.google.com/site/visitappalachia/recommended-readings/harlan-a-century-of-mining).
- Early 1960s: U.S. Steel sold Lynch's company houses to residents; Lynch incorporated in 1963; U.S. Steel sold its mines in 1984 (https://sah-archipedia.org/node/13168; Harlan mining history above).
- 1970s: The Brookside strike (film "Harlan County, USA," 1976). Mining jobs rose again to 4,419 in 1981 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlan_County,_Kentucky; Harlan mining history above).
- 1996: Rail lines to Lynch and Benham abandoned. June 2016: employed miners down to 764. 2019: miners blocked a coal train for almost two months over unpaid wages after a mine company bankruptcy (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlan_County,_Kentucky).

## What makes it distinct
The archetype of the coal company town: built by steel companies, fought over in the mine wars, emptied by mechanization, and finished off by the last mine bankruptcies.
