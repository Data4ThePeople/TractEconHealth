## History (dated bullets with sources)
- Before WWII: Compton was about 95% white (https://www.npr.org/transcripts/993993572).
- 1948 to 1950s: After courts ended enforcement of racial covenants, the first Black families moved in; real estate agents used blockbusting to drive panic selling (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton,_California; https://www.nprillinois.org/equity-justice/2021-05-05/how-a-predatory-real-estate-practice-changed-the-face-of-compton).
- 1950 to 1970: Non-Hispanic Black share rose from 4.5% (1950) to 39.3% (1960) to 71.0% (1970). White-owned businesses left with white residents, hurting the tax base (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton,_California; https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/compton-california-1867).
- 1965: The Watts uprising sped up white flight from Compton (https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/compton-california-1867).
- 1969: Douglas Dollarhide became the first Black mayor of a metropolitan city in California (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton,_California).
- 1975 to 1986: South LA plant closures (GM South Gate 1982, Bethlehem Steel 1982, Goodyear, Firestone) cut about 12,000 jobs in the region around Compton (https://scalar.sparcinla.org/great-wall-institute/labor-and-union-organizing; https://memorywork.irle.ucla.edu/archives/4956).
- 1980s: Middle-class Black residents began leaving as unemployment, gangs and crack cocaine took hold (https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/compton-california-1867).
- 1980 to 2020: Latino share rose from 21.1% to 70.81%; Black share fell to 25.43% (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton,_California).
- 2000: The LA County Sheriff replaced the Compton Police Department (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton,_California).
- Alameda Corridor rail trench (opened 2002) runs next to this tract: UNCONFIRMED.

## What makes it distinct
A suburb that flipped twice: from white to a Black middle-class suburb, and then, after plant closures and the crack era, to mostly Latino and low-income.
