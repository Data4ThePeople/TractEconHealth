## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1940: Owsley County population peaked at 8,957; 4,051 in 2020, a 55% drop (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owsley_County,_Kentucky).
- 1960s to 1970s: Many Owsley men found work in coal mines in neighboring counties; Owsley's own mines were smaller (Al Jazeera, Nov. 8, 2016, https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2016/11/8/inside-owsley-americas-poorest-white-county).
- 1980 to 2014: Owsley's cancer death rate rose 45.6%, the largest increase of any U.S. county (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owsley_County,_Kentucky).
- 1980s to 1990s: Walmart stores opened in neighboring counties and local businesses closed (Al Jazeera, above).
- 2000: Owsley median household income was $15,805 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owsley_County,_Kentucky).
- 2001 to 2004: Tobacco, the mainstay of Owsley farming, collapsed after the tobacco settlement and the 2004 federal quota buyout (Fair and Equitable Tobacco Reform Act); production fell more than 95% over a decade (Kentucky ag development plan, https://www.kyagr.com/agpolicy/documents/ADF_Councils_Plans_OWSLEY.pdf). The exact dates differ across sources.
- 2009: Government benefits made up 53% of personal income in Owsley County (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owsley_County,_Kentucky).
- 2010s: Rise in prescription painkiller and meth use (Al Jazeera, above).

## What makes it distinct
A farm-and-tobacco story, not a coal one: small burley farms lost their cash crop when the federal tobacco program ended, on top of decades of out-migration.
