# U014: Cedar Grove and nearby south Shreveport neighborhoods (Sunset Acres, Caddo Heights and Hollywood area), Caddo Parish, LA

## Name and place
Eight tracts in south Shreveport, all inside city limits (TIGERweb). Cedar Grove runs from Hollywood Avenue south to 85th Street and from Line Avenue west to Linwood Road (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Grove,_Shreveport,_Louisiana). That matches tracts 22017022500, 22017023200, 22017023300, 22017023702 and 22017023802. Tracts 22017023401, 22017023402 and 22017023500 lie west of Linwood. Their names (Sunset Acres, Caddo Heights or Hollywood) are UNCONFIRMED. The 2020 census counted 20,167 people, 89% Black.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1930s: Cedar Grove was known for working-class gin joints, brothels and gambling, such as the Woodlawn Club (Cedar Grove article, above).
- Late 1930s: HOLC graded parts of it "C" and "D." By our overlay, D areas cover 51% of tracts 225 and 237.02 and 35% of 238.02, and C covers 86% of tract 232 (https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/). The western tracts were not graded, so they were probably built later. That is UNCONFIRMED.
- 1970 to 1990: In the project's tract data, poverty rose from 17% in 1970 to 21% in 1980 and 38% in 1990 (history_long.csv; the 1970 and 1980 fit is 0.657). Over the same years the share of residents who were Black went from 28% (1970) to 36% (1980), 65% (1990), 81% (2000) and 90% (2010), from the same file. The data show the turnover. Written sources on why it happened (school desegregation, new suburbs) were not found in this pass (UNCONFIRMED).
- 1980s: The oil bust hit Shreveport. Many companies cut jobs, and South Park Mall closed in the late 1990s (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreveport,_Louisiana). Whether the mall was in or next to these tracts is UNCONFIRMED.
- 1980s: Cedar Grove became known as an inner-city neighborhood during the crack cocaine epidemic. In September 1988 a riot lasting two days led police to seal off the neighborhood (Cedar Grove article, above).
- 2001 and 2012: Western Electric's Shreveport plant (about 7,500 workers at its peak) closed in 2001, and General Motors' plant closed in August 2012 (Shreveport article, above).
- 2020 to 2024: Poverty was 42% (history_long.csv).

## Story strength (1-5)
4. The tract data show a fast, clear turn: poverty went from 17% to 38% and the Black share from 28% to 65% between 1970 and 1990. The 1988 riot and the plant closures are dated. Written sources on the turnover still need to be found.

## What makes it distinct
This was a working-class area that was not poor in 1970 and became one of the poorest by 1990. That makes it a story of a neighborhood that fell within one generation, not one that was always poor.
