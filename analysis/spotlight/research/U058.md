## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1868 to 1905: The city annexed the land south of Broadway in 1868; St. James Court was developed from 1890 and fully occupied by 1905 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Louisville).
- 1860s: Limerick was built as housing for Louisville and Nashville Railroad freight-yard workers (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick,_Louisville).
- 1930s: Many large homes were converted to boarding houses in the Depression; federal low-interest loans encouraged conversion to apartments (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Louisville).
- 1937: The Ohio River flood pushed many remaining wealthy households to higher ground (same source).
- After 1945: Vacancy rose and landlords cut rents (same source).
- 1965 to 1971: 639 buildings were razed under urban renewal (same source).
- 1970 to 1980: Old Louisville's population fell from 16,338 to 12,678, a 22% drop (same source).
- 1975: Made a local historic preservation district; National Register listing July 12, 1984 (same source).

## What makes it distinct
A grand Victorian neighborhood that slid into subdivided rooming houses after the Depression and the 1937 flood, then partly came back through preservation, leaving a poor pocket beside restored mansions.
