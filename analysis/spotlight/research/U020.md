# U020: North Baton Rouge fenceline (Istrouma, Delmont Place, Legion Village, Brookstown, Scotlandville edge, Fairfields / Melrose East)

## Name and place
East Baton Rouge Parish, Baton Rouge. The parish GIS neighborhood layer (https://maps.brla.gov/gis/rest/services/Cadastral/Neighborhood/MapServer) places the tract centroids in:
- Legion Village: tract 1
- Brookstown: tract 2
- Delmont Place: tract 3
- Istrouma / Dixie: tract 5
- "North Baton Rouge": tract 6.02
- Scotlandville (south edge): tract 33
- East Fairfields / Melrose Place: tract 11.03
- Smiley Heights / Melrose East: tract 11.04

Tracts 1, 3 and 5 sit just east of the ExxonMobil refinery and chemical complex. I-110 runs through tracts 1, 3, 5 and 33. Tracts 11.03 and 11.04 are about two miles southeast, in Mid City, and are a weaker fit.

Population about 26,467. 2020 census: 83% to 96% Black by tract.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1909: Standard Oil opens the Baton Rouge refinery, now ExxonMobil, one of the largest in the U.S. (540,000 barrels a day in 2020; about 4,000 direct employees). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_Rouge_Refinery
- 1914: Southern University relocates to Scotlandville, which becomes a destination for Black migrants around World War I. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotlandville,_Louisiana
- 1942: The first commercial fluid catalytic cracking unit starts at the refinery (May 25, 1942), part of a wartime build-out. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_Rouge_Refinery
- Mid-century to later: Standard Heights, the neighborhood next to the plant, first housed refinery workers; as they moved out it became a low-income Black community, and after expansions and accidents ExxonMobil bought out most of its houses (buyout dates UNCONFIRMED). https://leanweb.org/community-atlas/communities/baton-rouge ; https://bittersoutherner.com/neighbors-of-the-fence
- 1957 to 1984: I-110 is built north from downtown in stages: Plank Road to Hollywood Drive around 1969, to Airline Highway in 1976, to Harding Boulevard in the late 1970s, and through Scotlandville to Scenic Highway in January 1984. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_110_(Louisiana)
- 1978 and 1980: Parts of Scotlandville are annexed into the city of Baton Rouge. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotlandville,_Louisiana
- June 14, 2012: A benzene leak at the refinery leads Standard Heights residents to sue; an EPA review later found pervasive corrosion inside the refinery (reported by NPR, 2013). https://www.npr.org/2013/05/30/187044721/baton-rouge-s-corroded-overpolluting-neighbor-exxon (page timed out on fetch; facts from search summary, verify before use)

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
4. A dated refinery history, a staged interstate build, buyouts and well-covered pollution events give several data series, but documentation of the neighborhood's own decline (when workers left, buyout years) is thinner than for New Orleans.

## What makes it distinct
A former company-town edge where the plant stayed and the people who worked there left: the refinery kept growing for a century while the blocks along its fence turned over from worker housing to low-income Black neighborhoods, then were cut by I-110 and partly bought out.
