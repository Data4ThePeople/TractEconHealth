# R011: Okemah, Okfuskee County, Oklahoma

## Name and place
- One tract, 40107080900 (Census Tract 809). About 26 square miles, 2020 population 3,449.
- Covers the city of Okemah (county seat) and nearby Bearden, inside the Muscogee (Creek) Oklahoma Tribal Statistical Area (TIGERweb, https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov). Centroid 35.424, -96.302 is in Okemah.
- 2020 census: 1,043 of 3,449 (30%) American Indian alone; 85 Black alone (2020 PL 94-171, https://api.census.gov/data/2020/dec/pl).
- Note for the editor: Boley and the other all-Black towns of Okfuskee County are NOT in this tract. Boley lies west of Okemah. This candidate is Okemah.

## Rule-out flags
- Group quarters: 154 people (4%), including 21 correctional (likely county jail) and 108 nursing. Not a driver. The John Lilley Correctional Center is in Boley, outside this tract.
- Tract boundaries are stable since 1990 (stability 1.0).
- Story overlaps in theme (Creek allotment, early oil) with other eastern Oklahoma candidates.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1907: Okfuskee County is created at statehood from the former Creek Nation, with Okemah as the county seat (Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History and Culture, Okfuskee County, https://www.okhistory.org/publications/enc/entry?entry=OK005).
- May 25, 1911: Laura Nelson and her son L.D. are taken from the Okemah jail by a mob and lynched from a bridge over the North Canadian River; no one was charged (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynching_of_Laura_and_L._D._Nelson; BlackPast, https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/lynching-of-laura-and-l-d-nelson-1911).
- 1912: Woody Guthrie born in Okemah (well known, but source not checked in this pass: UNCONFIRMED).
- Mid-1920s: Oil boom from the nearby Cromwell field; Okemah's population, estimated at 7,000 during the boom, is 4,002 at the 1930 census; the county peaks at 29,016 in 1930 (Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History and Culture, Okemah, https://www.okhistory.org/publications/enc/entry?entry=OK004 and OK005; OKGenWeb, https://okgenweb.net/~okokfusk/histories/okboom01.htm).
- 1935: Tenants run 2,702 of 3,520 county farms (77%) (Encyclopedia, OK005, same link).
- 1930-1960: Okemah falls to 2,836 people by 1960 as the oil boom ends and cotton tenancy collapses (Encyclopedia, OK004).
- 1982-1986: Statewide oil bust; Oklahoma loses about 51,000 jobs from 1982 to 1985 and crude prices fall about 70% in four months in 1986 (Kansas City Fed, https://www.kansascityfed.org/pdf/article/articlepage/5446/; Christian Science Monitor, https://csmonitor.com/1986/0213/afall-f.html). Okemah-specific effects: UNCONFIRMED.
- Late 1970s: County ranks among Oklahoma's top peanut and pecan producers (Encyclopedia, OK005). Later decline of peanuts: UNCONFIRMED.

## Story strength (1-5, with one sentence why)
3. A clear dated arc from Creek allotment through a 1920s oil boom, a tenant-farm collapse and the 1980s bust, with a population series to show it, though the post-1960 local job record is still thin.

## What makes it distinct
A Creek Nation county seat that boomed twice on oil, with three-quarters of its farms run by tenants in 1935, and a 1911 lynching that is part of its documented racial history.
