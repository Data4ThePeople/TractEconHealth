# M002: Uptown south and South Central Butte, Butte-Silver Bow, MT

## Name and place
Butte-Silver Bow tract 1.02 (0.7 sq mi, ZIP 59701). The tract runs from the south edge of Uptown Butte (the historic business district) south through the South Central neighborhood toward South Butte and the Silver Bow Creek corridor (tract bounding box 45.995 to 46.018 N). Residents say "Uptown" and the "South Side." Population 2,937 (ACS); 2,977 in 2020.

## Rule-out flags
- **Boundary:** a piece of a larger 2010 tract with no 1990 match. Any long series has to use the old tract outline or a Butte-wide series.
- **Group quarters:** 286 of 2,977 (10%): 100 correctional and 186 college (2020). Not dominant, but the correctional piece (likely a county detention or prerelease site; UNCONFIRMED) should be named.
- Mining is not on the cause list as a tag; use other:mining_copper.

## History
- **1895:** The Anaconda Copper Mining Company was incorporated ([BPSOU history](https://bpsou.com/history/)).
- **1880s-1916:** Most South Central houses date from the 1880s and 1890s, built for clerks, carpenters, teamsters and miners near Uptown ([Historic Montana, South Central](https://historicmt.org/tours/show/92)).
- **1917-1920:** Butte's population peaked; the 1920 census counted 41,611 in the city and 60,313 in Silver Bow County ([Montana Standard timeline](https://mtstandard.com/special-section/news/what-are-some-key-dates-in-buttes-history/article_1fde95c0-fc07-5b04-981f-b2841d857dca.html); [Mining Childhood excerpt](https://www.explorebigsky.com/mining-childhood-growing-up-in-butte-1900-1960/8214)).
- **1955:** Open-pit mining began at the Berkeley Pit, replacing many underground miners with heavy machinery ([BPSOU history](https://bpsou.com/history/); [Butte history overview](https://www.bigskyfishing.com/Montana-Info/butte_mt-2.shtm)).
- **1971-1977:** Chile nationalized Anaconda's mines in 1971; Atlantic Richfield bought the weakened company in 1977 ([Montana Historical Society](https://mths.mt.gov/education/StoriesOfTheLand/Part2/Chapter10/Ch10Educators/ACCompany); [BPSOU](https://bpsou.com/history/)).
- **1980-1983:** The Anaconda smelter closed in 1980 and Butte mining stopped in 1983; the pit's pumps were shut off and it began to fill with acidic water; unemployment topped 17% ([Montana Historical Society](https://mths.mt.gov/education/StoriesOfTheLand/Part2/Chapter10/Ch10Educators/ACCompany); [Daily Kos history essay](https://www.dailykos.com/stories/2014/3/27/1287789/-Requiem-For-an-Old-Mountain-Mining-Town-Stories-of-Butte-Montana)). The 17% figure needs an official LAUS check.
- **1988-2020:** Superfund cleanup began in 1988; by August 2019 the Residential Metals Abatement Program had assessed 3,495 properties and cleaned 795 attics and 484 yards; a partial consent decree was proposed February 13, 2020 ([BPSOU](https://bpsou.com/history/)).

## Story strength
4. A century of dated, well-recorded company and Superfund history, but the tract itself cannot be compared to 1990 without rebuilding the old outline.

## What makes it distinct
The "richest hill on earth" neighborhood that lost its company, its smelter and its population, then lived beside a toxic pit and a 30-year cleanup of its own attics and yards.
