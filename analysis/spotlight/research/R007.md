# R007: McDowell County coalfields, WV (Welch, Kimball, Gary, War, Iaeger area)

## Name and place
Rural coal camps of McDowell County, West Virginia, around the county seat of Welch (the editor's seed, 8.8 km away). Six of the eight tracts are in McDowell County (tracts 9536, 9538, 9542, 9545.01, 9545.03, 9545.04). These tracts take in the Elkhorn Creek and Tug Fork camps (Kimball, Keystone, Northfork, Gary, Anawalt, War, Bradshaw and nearby places). The tract-to-town match comes from tract centroids and is approximate. Two tracts are across the state line in Virginia: Buchanan County tract 105 and Tazewell County tract 209, both near Richlands, VA.

Tract populations from the 2020 PL file (Census API): 17,347 total for the cluster.

## Rule-out flags
- **Prison in the cluster.** Tract 54047954503 had 1,881 people in adult correctional facilities in 2020, out of 4,755 (2020 PL file, table P5). That is FCI McDowell, a federal prison about four miles north of Welch that took its first inmates in 2010 ([Wikipedia, FCI McDowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Correctional_Institution,_McDowell)). About 11 percent of the cluster's population is incarcerated. Drop tract 9545.03 or check that the numbers hold without it. This does not sink the candidate. The other seven tracts have no large group quarters.
- The cluster crosses two states and three counties. Mostly McDowell, so county series for McDowell will carry the story.
- Overlaps with R003 (Mingo/Tug Valley) and R019 (Martin County/Tug Fork). Only one central Appalachian coal story should be picked; this is the strongest of the three.

## History
- **1950:** McDowell County's population peaked at nearly 100,000, the third largest in the state, before coal employment began to fall in the 1950s. This claim appears in local-history sites ([abandonedonline.net](https://abandonedonline.net/the-fading-glory-of-coal-country-gary-iaegar-and-war)) but not yet in a primary source. The exact 1950 count (often given as 98,887) is UNCONFIRMED; pull it from NHGIS.
- **1950s-1960s:** Mechanization of underground mining cut coal jobs while output held up. The county-level job counts are UNCONFIRMED here; MSHA and County Business Patterns can show them.
- **1982-1986:** U.S. Steel wound down its Gary mines. Sources disagree: Wikipedia says about 550 miners were laid off in March 1982 and all Gary mines closed by the end of 1982; other accounts say the operations closed in 1986 with more than 1,200 jobs lost ([Gary, WV, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary,_West_Virginia); [WSWS 2010](https://www.wsws.org/en/articles/2010/08/welc-a03.html)). Dates need a primary source.
- **2001 and 2002:** Major floods in the Tug Fork and Elkhorn valleys. UNCONFIRMED in this search; FEMA declarations and USGS records would confirm.
- **2010:** FCI McDowell opened on a former surface mine site near Welch ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Correctional_Institution,_McDowell)).
- **2016:** The county's only Walmart, in Kimball, closed. 140 people lost jobs and the county lost its main grocery store ([WV Public Broadcasting](https://www.wvpublic.org/post/what-happens-when-walmart-closes-one-coal-community)).
- **2000s-2010s:** The opioid epidemic. County overdose death rates are available from CDC WONDER; not pulled here.

## Story strength
5. McDowell is the textbook case of coal-driven decline, with seven decades of population loss and a long, dated chain of closures, and each step has a public data series.

## What makes it distinct
The highest-output coal county in the nation's history emptied out across seventy years. The story runs through mechanization, the steel company leaving, floods, a prison and the loss of its only big grocery store. It is the clearest case of an economy built on one mineral and one buyer.
