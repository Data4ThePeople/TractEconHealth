# R038: Eufaula, Clayton and Union Springs (Barbour and Bullock counties, AL) with Georgetown/Quitman and Randolph counties, GA

## Name and place
Eight rural tracts, population about 23,970: Barbour County, AL tracts 9502 (Clayton area, 199.6 square miles), 9506 and 9509 (parts of Eufaula); Bullock County, AL tracts 9521, 9522.01 and 9522.02 (Union Springs and surroundings); Quitman County, GA tract 9603 (Georgetown) and Randolph County, GA tract 7902 (south of Cuthbert, UNCONFIRMED).

## Rule-out flags
- PRISONS: Ventress Correctional Facility (379 AL Hwy 239, Clayton) geocodes to tract 01005950200, and Bullock Correctional Facility (104 Bullock Dr, Union Springs) geocodes to tract 01011952202. Inmate counts in 2020 UNCONFIRMED (Census group quarters API was not reachable). Two of eight tracts include a state prison.
- Crosses a state line and four counties; Eufaula's industrial story and Bullock's plantation story differ.
- Stability 1.0 since 1990: good.

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1800s to early 1900s: Bullock County was a center of plantation cotton (https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/bullock-county/).
- 20th century: Bullock remains among the poorest counties in Alabama, about 33 percent below poverty (same source). Mechanization and out-migration timing UNCONFIRMED.
- 2007: Wellstone Mills closed two Eufaula plants, about 117 and 120 jobs (https://www.apr.org/2007-12-05/second-eufaula-mill-scheduled-to-close).
- 2011: Beaulieu of America closed its Eufaula carpet yarn plant, 190 jobs (https://www.timesfreepress.com/news/2011/nov/14/beaulieu-closing-eufaula-alabama-cuts-190-jobs/).
- 2017: A latex glove plant on the Alabama-Georgia line closed; a new owner planned to reopen and hire up to 400 in 2020 (https://alreporter.com/2020/01/24/eufaula-plant-to-reopen-hire-up-to-400).
- 2025: Nucor announced closing its Eufaula building plant, 79 jobs (https://www.steelmarketupdate.com/2025/03/03/nucor-to-close-alabama-plant-move-work-to-texas).
- Randolph County, GA hospital closure: UNCONFIRMED.

A Chattahoochee River border region where plantation counties and a mill town lost jobs in waves and replaced some with state prisons.
