# U003: The Westside (College Hill Courts), Chattanooga, TN

## Name and place
- Hamilton County, TN. 1 tract (47065001600), 0.9 sq mi, pop 2,892. Internal point 35.0437, -85.3234, inside Chattanooga, just west of downtown.
- Local name: "the Westside." The Chattanooga Housing Authority's College Hill Courts (497 units) and Gateway Tower (132 units) are there (https://chcrpa.org/project/westside-evolves/; https://www.timesfreepress.com/news/2021/mar/05/westside-evolves/). That both are inside tract 16 is likely but UNCONFIRMED.
- The 2020 census counts 2,070 housing units in the tract and no group quarters (PL 94-171 and DHC). If both complexes are inside, the 629 public housing units are about 30% of all housing units. Other subsidized senior towers may raise the share (UNCONFIRMED).

## Rule-out flags
- Public housing makes up a large share of the tract. It is not a single institution, but public housing explains much of the income level by itself. The editor's brief names this as a reason to rule a place out. Treat it as a strong flag.
- Tract boundaries are stable (1.0).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1940: College Hill Courts opens as segregated public housing for Black residents, built with U.S. Housing Authority funds as "slum clearance" (https://livingnewdeal.org/sites/college-hill-courts-chattanooga-tn/; https://www.timesfreepress.com/news/2021/apr/29/how-will-chattanoogas-westside-evolvecity-loo/).
- 1955: The city announces federal funding for Westside urban renewal. The project, later called Golden Gateway, becomes the 12th costliest ($10,667,000) and 5th largest (410 acres) urban renewal project in the U.S. (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=167722).
- 1950s-1960s: Highway building clears plants and homes. I-24 ran over plants that had employed many Black workers who lived nearby (https://www.aarp.org/livable-communities/getting-around/info-2023/before-the-highway-chattanooga-tennessee-moses-freeman.html; fetch blocked, cited from the search summary). Exact years are UNCONFIRMED.
- 2017: The Times Free Press reports the Westside "struggling to stay alive" (https://www.pressreader.com/usa/chattanooga-times-free-press/20170530/281496456239443).
- 2021-2023: The "Westside Evolves" and "One Westside" plans call for replacing all 629 units one-for-one. A master developer is chosen in 2023, and the project is billed at $1 billion (https://www.timesfreepress.com/news/2023/mar/02/chattanooga-westside-evolves-plans-1-billion-redevelopment-tfp/; https://www.chattnewschronicle.com/top-stories/chattanooga-breaks-ground-on-phase-2-of-one-westside-revitalization-project).
- Chattanooga HOLC map: UNCONFIRMED whether one exists for Chattanooga in Mapping Inequality.

## Story strength (1-5)
3. The urban-renewal record is strong and dated, but the tract's numbers mostly reflect public housing. That weakens the case that decades of stacked causes, not one institution, explain the outcome.

## What makes it distinct
Golden Gateway was one of the largest urban renewal projects in the country. The neighborhood is now in the middle of a $1 billion rebuild, so the story would catch a place mid-change.
