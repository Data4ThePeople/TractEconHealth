# U040: Downtown New Kensington and Arnold, Westmoreland County, PA

## Name and place
Three small tracts on the Allegheny River about 20 miles northeast of Pittsburgh (Census geocoder):
- 42129800100: Arnold (1500 Fifth Ave geocodes here)
- 42129800600, 42129800700: New Kensington, downtown and the Fifth Avenue corridor

Locals call the twin towns "the Alle-Kiski Valley" or "New Ken and Arnold." Population 4,766 (batch file).

## History (dated bullets with sources)
- 1891: New Kensington founded; the Pittsburgh Reduction Company, later Alcoa, was its first large employer (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kensington,_Pennsylvania). Chambers Glass opened in Arnold the same year (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold,_Pennsylvania).
- 1930: Alcoa's research lab opened in New Kensington with about 500 workers. At its peak Alcoa's river plant employed nearly 25% of the city (WESA, Aug. 30, 2017, https://www.wesanews.org/post/rebuilding-new-kensington-city-alcoa-made).
- 1940s: Wartime Alcoa employment topped 7,000 (reported by the Valley News Dispatch, https://archive.triblive.com/local/valley-news-dispatch/hundreds-of-jobs-big-plans-at-new-kensingtons-schreiber-industrial-park; UNCONFIRMED, not read directly). 1941: Aluminum City Terrace built for Alcoa defense workers (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kensington,_Pennsylvania).
- 1950: New Kensington population peaked at 25,146; 12,170 in 2020, a 52% drop. Arnold peaked at 10,898 in 1940; 4,772 in 2020, a 56% drop (Wikipedia census tables, links above).
- 1965: Alcoa moved research to a new technical center in Upper Burrell, about 7 miles away (WESA, above).
- 1971: The Alcoa New Kensington works closed (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kensington,_Pennsylvania). The site became the Schreiber Industrial Park.
- 1970s onward: Downtown New Kensington has had high vacancy rates "since the 1970s" (same source).
- 2008: The redevelopment authority began condemning and demolishing abandoned buildings (same source).
- Arnold glass plant ("the Glasshouse," American Glass Co.) closure date: UNCONFIRMED.

## What makes it distinct
The birthplace of the American aluminum industry, where the founding company slowly moved its plant and labs out of town over four decades and left a downtown that has been emptying since the 1970s.
