```
Does health stop improving past a certain income? We thought it might. The data only partly agreed.

We put about 78,000 U.S. census tracts and about 2,900 counties into one free, interactive chart, with income and other economic measures on one side and physical and mental health on the other.

What we found:

Health improves fastest at the low end of income. Across census tracts, the rank correlation between median household income and a health index built from 14 CDC measures is 0.82.

The gains slow above about $110,000 in median household income, but they do not stop. Above that level, the health index rises about 27% as fast, and it still rises in every state with enough tracts to test.

The cleanest check is life expectancy, counted from death records rather than a model. Across U.S. counties, it runs from 70.7 years in the lowest-income group to 80.8 years in the highest.

The chart shows the link. It does not show which way it runs or what causes it. But it raises a fair question: should economic development and public health be planned together?

Pick any of 15 economic measures and 18 health measures, and look at your own state. Link in the first comment.
```
