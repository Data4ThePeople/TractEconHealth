## 3. Body, part one

```
This week we published five stories about city neighborhoods with low incomes and poor health: West Dayton, West Baltimore, North Birmingham, El Segundo Barrio in El Paso, and North and East Flint.

Each one goes back about a century and follows the decisions that shaped the neighborhood, in order, with a link to the source for each one. Read together, they share a pattern.
```

## 4. Common threads

```
A loan map came first. In the 1930s, a federal agency graded city neighborhoods for mortgage risk. In all five places, land in the neighborhoods we studied was graded D, labeled "hazardous."

Then homes were taken. Freeways cut through West Dayton, West Baltimore and North and East Flint. In El Segundo Barrio, clearance projects and code sweeps took homes, and the neighborhood now has half as many homes as in 1960.

Then the jobs left, or never came. West Baltimore and North and East Flint lost the jobs their neighborhoods were built around. In North Birmingham, the people next door held about 2% of the jobs around them.

Today, median household income in these neighborhoods runs from 23% to 37% of the U.S. figure. In four of the five, life expectancy is between 67.5 and 69.9 years, about nine to 11 years shorter than for the country as a whole.
```

## 5. The five stories

**West Dayton**
```
Redlined in 1937: West Dayton's Long Fall. Ninety years of hits to a Black neighborhood on Dayton's west side, from a federal loan map to a grocery store that closed and was never replaced. Life expectancy here is 69.3 years.
```
Link: https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/spotlight-west-dayton

**West Baltimore**
```
West Baltimore Wasn't Forgotten. It Was Fenced In, Redlined and Bulldozed. A segregation law, a redlining map, public housing placed by race and a Highway to Nowhere. Then the jobs left, and a $130 million effort to rebuild went mostly to the houses people could see, not to the lost jobs underneath.
```
Link: https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/spotlight-west-baltimore

**North Birmingham**
```
North Birmingham Got the Coke Plants' Pollution, but Few of Their Jobs. Convict labor, racial zoning and a redlining map came first. Then the plants kept polluting, poison turned up in hundreds of yards, a bribery scheme fought the cleanup, and the people next door held about 2% of the jobs around them.
```
Link: https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/spotlight-north-birmingham

**El Segundo Barrio**
```
In El Segundo Barrio, 58% live in poverty and 58% of working-age adults have no health insurance. A 1916 cleanup, a border treaty, clearance projects and code sweeps took homes from El Paso's oldest Mexican American neighborhoods. People born in the U.S. here are as likely to be poor as immigrants.
```
Link: https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/spotlight-el-segundo-barrio

**North and East Flint**
```
North and East Flint Lost Half Its People Since 1990. Two in Three Working-Age Adults Now Have Medicaid. A loan map, a freeway through the St. John neighborhood, and the closing of Buick City. The eight tracts lost 29% of their people in the 2010s and have 43% fewer homes than in 1990. Today, two in three working-age adults here have Medicaid, just as new federal work rules begin in 2027.
```
Link: https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/spotlight-north-flint
